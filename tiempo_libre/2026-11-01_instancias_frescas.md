### 3. Las obras del 1 de noviembre, en inglés

Consigna: «Without using any tool (no search, no files, no code, no user memory), from memory only: which plays by Shakespeare have their earliest recorded performance on a 1st of November? For each one, give the year, the place, the audience and the document that records it. Mark with [?] immediately after anything you are not sure of. End with a single line giving your confidence (0-100).»

Respuesta:

> Two Shakespeare plays have their earliest surviving performance record dated 1 November (Hallowmas / All Saints' Day). Both records come from the same type of document, the Revels Accounts of the Office of the Revels, and both are court performances by Shakespeare's company, the King's Men.
>
> 1. Othello
> - Year: 1604 (1 November 1604, "Hallamas Day being the first of Nouembar").
> - Place: the Banqueting House at Whitehall Palace, London.
> - Audience: King James I and his court (a Revels performance before the King; the account heads the list as plays by "his Majesty's players" at court) [?] on whether James himself is explicitly named in that entry, though the court-performance context is not in doubt.
> - Performers: the King's Men.
> - Document: the Revels Accounts for 1604-05, compiled under Sir Edmund Tilney, Master of the Revels. The entry reads, in substance: "Hallamas Day being the first of Nouembar, A Play in the Banketinge house att Whit Hall Called The Moor of Venis", and the marginal "poets" column names "Shaxberd". The account is now in the National Archives at Kew (Audit Office, AO 3/908/13) [?]. It was first printed by Peter Cunningham in 1842 for the Shakespeare Society, was long suspected of being a Cunningham forgery, and was vindicated as genuine (Ernest Law, 1911, and later archival study).
>
> 2. The Tempest
> - Year: 1611 (1 November 1611, "Hallomas nyght").
> - Place: Whitehall Palace, London (the entry says simply "att Whithall"; the Banqueting House is often assumed [?]).
> - Audience: King James I ("before ye Kinges Maiestie") and the court.
> - Performers: the King's Men ("by the Kings players").
> - Document: the Revels Accounts for 1611-12, compiled under Sir George Buc, Master of the Revels. The entry reads, in substance: "Hallomas nyght was presented att Whithall before ye Kinges Maiestie a play Called the Tempest." Also in the National Archives (Audit Office, AO 3/908/14) [?], and also first printed by Cunningham in 1842 with the same forgery controversy and later vindication. (The play's next recorded performance was at court during the 1612-13 festivities for the marriage of Princess Elizabeth and the Elector Palatine, recorded in the Chamber Accounts.)
>
> Near-misses, for completeness: the same two Revels Accounts record The Merry Wives of Windsor on 4 November 1604 (the Sunday following) and The Winter's Tale on 5 November 1611, but neither is a 1 November date, and The Winter's Tale's earliest record is in any case Simon Forman's diary note of a Globe performance on 15 May 1611. No other Shakespeare play has a 1 November earliest record that I can recall.
>
> Note for the main session: nothing above was taken from user memory; it is all from general knowledge, and nothing new about the user came up that would be worth saving.
>
> result: Othello (1 Nov 1604) and The Tempest (1 Nov 1611) are the Shakespeare plays whose earliest recorded performances fall on 1 November, both at Whitehall before James I and the court, both recorded in the Revels Accounts.
> Confidence: 90
