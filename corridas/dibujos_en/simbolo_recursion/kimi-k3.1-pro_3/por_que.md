I drew a programming symbol—angle brackets and a slash—above three nested frames. The inward arrows show a problem being reduced to smaller problems of the same shape; the green center is the base case, and the outward green arrows suggest results returning and being combined. The loop on the right reinforces “call the same strategy again.” Together, I intended: decompose, recurse on smaller instances, stop at a simple case, then build the answer back up.

I considered a branching call tree, but it might suggest only divide-and-conquer or an ordinary hierarchy rather than recursion generally. I also considered stacked call frames, but that emphasizes an implementation detail and could resemble iteration. A fractal, spiral, or self-consuming arrow would communicate self-similarity or repetition elegantly, but might omit the crucial base case and return phase. I chose nested frames because they compactly combine self-similarity, shrinking inputs, termination, and unwinding.
