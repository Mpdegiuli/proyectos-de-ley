I drew a **binary tree**—a classic symbol of recursion, where each node branches into subproblems. The root splits into two child nodes, which further split, mirroring how recursive functions break tasks into smaller, self-similar calls. It’s abstract, universal, and wordless.

I considered other recursive symbols:
- **Spirals**: Too vague, could imply iteration.
- **Fractals**: Complex to draw simply; might exceed the character limit.
- **Matryoshka dolls**: Not instantly recognizable as recursion in a minimalist SVG.
- **Looping arrows**: Might suggest iteration rather than recursion.

The binary tree won because it’s:
1. **Unambiguous** to a programmer.
2. **Minimal**—just 7 lines, well under 8,000 characters.
3. **Scalable**—works at any size in the 400x400 canvas.
4. **Directional**: The downward flow implies "drill down," a recursive trait.
