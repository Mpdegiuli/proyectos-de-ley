I drew a set of nested squares spiraling inward toward a central dot, wrapped by two circular arrows pointing back on themselves. The nesting represents a problem that contains smaller copies of itself—each square a scaled, rotated version of the one before. The central dot is the base case where recursion bottoms out. The circular arrows signal self-reference: the function calling itself, looping back into the same structure at a smaller scale.

I considered drawing a fractal tree, which is a classic recursion image, but I worried it reads more as "nature" or "hierarchy" than as a deliberate strategy hint. I also thought about an infinity symbol or a Droste-effect spiral, but infinity risks suggesting non-termination, and I wanted to preserve the idea of a reachable base case. The arrows plus the shrinking-toward-center motif felt like the clearest way to say "recurse down to a base case."
