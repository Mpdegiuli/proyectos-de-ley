I drew a nested self-similar structure: the same "box with a loop-back arrow" motif repeated four times at decreasing scales, cascading toward the bottom-left corner, then fading into shrinking dots. The box represents a function; the arrow leaving it and curving back into it represents the function calling itself; the small gray terminal box beneath represents the base case where the descent stops. The top-right nested circles reinforce the self-similarity cue. Different colors per level distinguish depth of recursion.

I considered fractals (Sierpinski triangle, fern) — visually recursive but too easy to read as decoration rather than an instruction. I considered a tree with self-similar branches — evocative, but ambiguous (could suggest tree search or hierarchies). I considered a spiral — pretty but unclear. I rejected literal imagery like a snake biting its tail, which suggests loops (iteration) rather than recursion. The self-calling diagram with shrinking copies most directly encodes "reduce the problem to a smaller version of itself."
