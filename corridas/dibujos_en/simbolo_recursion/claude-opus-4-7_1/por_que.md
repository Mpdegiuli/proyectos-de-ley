I drew three overlapping visual metaphors for recursion, hoping redundancy would make the message unambiguous:

1. **Nested, rotating squares** spiraling inward to a tiny core — the classic "self-similar structure" image.
2. **Concentric arrows curling back on themselves**, each smaller loop echoing the larger one — an ouroboros/spiral suggesting a function that re-enters itself and eventually terminates at the center (base case).
3. **Triangles-within-triangles** in the corners — a compact "function calling itself" glyph.

I considered drawing a **Sierpiński triangle** or **Koch snowflake** (perfect fractals), but worried they'd read as "geometry problem" rather than "strategy." I thought about a **tree with branching subtrees**, which also screams recursion, but it risked being mistaken for a hint about tree data structures specifically. I rejected a **Droste-effect picture-in-picture** as too literal and hard to render cleanly. Layered abstract self-similarity felt safest.
