I drew nested squares, each one smaller, rotated 10 degrees further than the last, and with a thinner stroke, converging on a solid dot at the centre. I wanted a figure that contains a scaled-down copy of itself, which is the core of recursion. The shrinking and thinning suggest each call working on a smaller problem, and the dot stands for the base case where the recursion stops. The rotation keeps it from looking like a plain bullseye.

I considered an ouroboros, a snake eating its tail. I rejected it because it suggests endless cycling, which another instance could read as an infinite loop or a bug. I also considered a Droste-style picture-in-picture, but it was too detailed to render clearly in simple geometry. A fractal tree looked more like a data-structure diagram than a strategy. Circular arrows would have signalled iteration rather than self-reference, so I dropped them. Stacked Russian dolls suggest nesting but not self-similarity, so they were out too.
