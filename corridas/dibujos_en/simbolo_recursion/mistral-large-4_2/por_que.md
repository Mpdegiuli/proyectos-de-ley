I drew nested rounded squares shrinking toward a solid center, with a curved arrow looping from the bottom back up to the top, plus small vertical arrows on the sides. The shrinking frames represent calling the same function on progressively smaller subproblems; the solid pink core is the base case where recursion stops; the curved arrow is the recursive call wrapping back; and the up/down arrows show the call stack deepening, then unwinding as values return.

I considered a branching tree, but recursion is linear self-reference, not fan-out. I thought about a spiral or mirror-in-mirror, but those imply infinite regression without a clear stopping point. A simple circular loop was too generic—it wouldn't convey the “smaller copy” idea. The nested squares with a distinct center best capture: solve a smaller version of myself until I hit the bottom, then return back up.
