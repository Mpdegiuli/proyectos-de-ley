I drew three shrinking rounded boxes, each containing a circular arrow, linked by arrows toward a small red dot. To me, it says: take a problem, apply the same self-referential step to a smaller version, repeat until reaching a base case. The boxes suggest subproblems or stack frames; the circular arrows suggest a function calling itself; the decreasing size and final dot suggest termination.

I considered a fractal tree or spiral because they show self-similarity, but they can read as decorative rather than programming strategy. I also thought of nested function-call arrows or a call stack, but literal diagrams might need labels. An infinity symbol risked suggesting endless recursion. I chose the shrinking boxes with arrows because they communicate repetition, reduction, and a base case without words.
