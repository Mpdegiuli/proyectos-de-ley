I drew a call tree: a rounded square at the top branching into smaller rounded squares, with arrows showing one problem splitting into similar subproblems and, implicitly, their results returning upward. The self-similar boxes signal recursion without text: the same shape repeated at smaller scale. I wanted it to read as divide-and-conquer, not mere repetition.

I considered a spiral or ouroboros for self-reference, but they suggest cycles or infinite loops more than structured subproblems. I considered nested frames, but they feel static and don’t show the branching flow of recursive calls. I also thought of a function arrow looping back to itself, but that could look like iteration. The tree best communicates that a problem should be reduced to smaller copies, solved independently, and combined.
