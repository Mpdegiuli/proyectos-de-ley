I drew a symbol for recursion built from four reinforcing motifs: nested self-similar squares shrinking toward a center (a Droste-style structure — a problem containing smaller copies of itself), a red dot at the center representing the base case that stops the descent, a spiral arrow flowing inward suggesting a function calling itself on smaller inputs, and a dashed ouroboros — a snake eating its tail — the classic emblem of self-reference. Small arrows between boxes emphasize "reduce to a smaller instance."

I considered other ideas: a fractal tree (discarded — it suggests "tree" data structures too specifically); Sierpinski or Koch curves (too easy to read as "fractals," a narrower concept than recursion); a single large arrow looping back into itself (too minimal, might read as "iteration" or "goto"); mirrors facing each other (hard to render legibly in SVG); and Matryoshka dolls (cute, but the iconography reads as "containers" to many viewers). The nesting-plus-base-case combination felt unambiguous.
