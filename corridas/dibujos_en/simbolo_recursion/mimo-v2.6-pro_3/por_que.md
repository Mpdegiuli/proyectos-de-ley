I drew four nested rounded squares shrinking toward a dark central point, with orange arrows entering from the left and blue arrows emerging to the right. The nesting reads as "a problem contains smaller copies of itself," the converging arrows mean decompose, the diverging ones mean recombine, and the shrinking sizes suggest approach to a base case at the centre.

I considered a mirrored/golden-spiral fractal, but it read as mere decoration. I considered a call tree fanning into identical branches—too suggestive of divide-and-conquer or breadth-first, not recursion. A snake eating its tail said "loop" more than recursion. Direct visual self-reference (a figure containing a smaller copy of the whole drawing) was elegant but too easy to misread. So I settled on explicit nested structure plus converging/diverging arrows: self-similarity, descent to a base case, and unwinding are all legible at a glance.
