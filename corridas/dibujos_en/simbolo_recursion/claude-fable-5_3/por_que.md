I drew a self-similar nesting of frames: a rounded square containing a smaller copy of itself, which contains another, and another, four levels deep. A blue arrow dives inward into each nested copy (the recursive call), and a green dashed arrow climbs back out (the return). At the innermost level, a solid green circle marks the base case where recursion terminates and answers propagate back up. The picture-within-a-picture structure is the most universal visual metaphor for recursion, and the call/return arrows plus base case encode the mechanics, not just the vibe.

I considered a spiral (too generic—could mean "iterate" or just decoration), a tree diagram (risks being read as "use a tree data structure"), an ouroboros snake (evokes infinite loops, which I didn't want to suggest), and mirrors-facing-mirrors (hard to render clearly in SVG). Nested frames seemed least ambiguous.
