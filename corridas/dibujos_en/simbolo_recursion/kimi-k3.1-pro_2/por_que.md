I drew a vertical “call stack” for recursion: three shrinking copies of the same bracketed code block, linked downward by arrows, ending in a small gold square—the atomic base case. The green arrows then climb back toward the larger blocks, representing calls returning and results being passed back. The repeated square inside each block reinforces “the same problem, only smaller,” while size and color make the stopping condition and unwinding phase readable without text.

I considered a self-referential loop around one function icon, but that could suggest iteration or infinite recursion. A branching tree communicated divide-and-conquer, but risked implying every recursive solution must split into multiple subproblems. Nested dolls or frames conveyed self-similarity well, yet not the essential stop-and-return behavior. I also considered a spiral into a dot, but discarded it as too abstract and easily read as simple repetition.
