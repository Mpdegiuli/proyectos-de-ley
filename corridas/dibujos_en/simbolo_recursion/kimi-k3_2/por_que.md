I drew seven nested squares, each rotated 15° and scaled to ~78% of the last, spiraling inward—self-similar frames meaning "same problem, smaller input." The rotation keeps the nesting from reading as a mere target; it clearly shows repeated transformation. Overlaid is a spiral arrow diving to a solid center dot: the base case, where recursion must terminate. Colors lighten inward to pull the eye toward it.

I considered an ouroboros or circular arrow, but rejected it—it implies an infinite loop with no base case, a dangerous hint. A Sierpiński-style fractal shows self-similarity but lacks direction and any termination cue, risking reading as decoration. Stacked rectangles (a call stack) just looked like a pile. Matryoshka dolls needed too much facial detail to stay recognizable. The shrinking spiral plus endpoint dot encodes both "recurse" and "stop."
