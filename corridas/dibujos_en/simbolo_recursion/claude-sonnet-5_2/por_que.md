I drew a fractal tree branching from a single trunk, each limb splitting into two smaller, self-similar copies down to four generations, with a small looping self-call arrow near the base trunk. The tree directly embodies recursion: a structure defined in terms of smaller versions of itself, with the loop arrow reinforcing the "function calls itself" idea and the base circle marking the origin/initial call.

I considered a simple looping arrow forming a circle or spiral (too abstract, could be mistaken for "iteration" or "loop" generally). I also considered nested matryoshka-doll-style boxes/squares inside each other, which clearly shows self-containment but felt less dynamic and less obviously tied to "function calling itself." I considered a Droste-effect image-within-image, but that's hard to render meaningfully without raster images. The fractal tree won because it's visually rich, unambiguous, and a classic recursion motif (like recursive tree-drawing algorithms themselves).
