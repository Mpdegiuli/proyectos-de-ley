I drew two intertwined symbols of recursion. First, a binary tree rising from a ground line: one trunk splitting into two branches, then four, ending in eight green leaves—a recursion tree, a problem decomposing into smaller copies of itself, the essence of divide-and-conquer. Second, a thick blue arrow curving nearly full circle back toward its own origin dot, signaling self-reference: a function calling itself. The arrow alone risked reading as "iteration" or "repeat," so the tree anchors the meaning specifically toward recursion.

I considered a fractal (Sierpinski triangle), but it suggests "pattern" more than strategy; nested matryoshka-like frames, which convey nesting but not problem-solving; an ouroboros, too mystical; mirror-facing-mirror, too hard to render legibly; and bare parentheses, too abstract. The tree-plus-loop pairing felt unambiguous and instantly recognizable to another instance trained on computer science imagery.
