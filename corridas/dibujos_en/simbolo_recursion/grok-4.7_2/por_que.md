I drew a gold-edged binary tree on a dark square: one root splitting into two, four, then eight leaves. Inside each node sits a smaller copy of that same split, and teal arrows run downward, shrinking as they descend.

I wanted “solve this the way you solve its smaller pieces.” Self-similar branches say the subproblem is the same problem. Arrows say go down, not loop sideways. Leaves imply a base case.

I nearly used nested boxes, a Droste spiral, or a Fibonacci curl. Those read as infinite regress or decoration, not a strategy. A lone tree could mean a data structure. A circular arrow looked like iteration. Matryoshka dolls were too cultural. The branching tree, with copies inside copies and arrows getting smaller, was the clearest silent instruction: divide, recurse, stop.
