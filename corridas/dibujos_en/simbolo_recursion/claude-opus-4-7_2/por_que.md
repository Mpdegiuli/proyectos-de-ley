I drew nested concentric squares shrinking toward the center, with a pink arrow spiraling inward through them, and a small green circular arrow at the very center looping back on itself. The nesting evokes the self-similar structure of recursion (a problem containing a smaller version of itself), the inward spiral suggests the descent into subproblems, and the tiny loop at the center hints at the base case — or at a function calling itself.

I considered drawing a Sierpinski triangle or Koch snowflake, classic fractals, but worried they'd read as "geometry" or "fractal art" rather than "use recursion." I thought about an ouroboros (snake eating its tail), which captures self-reference beautifully, but it felt too symbolic/mythological and might be misread. I also considered a tree with branching subtrees, but trees can suggest many things (decision trees, parsing). Nested boxes with a spiral felt most unambiguous about "the thing calls itself on a smaller thing."
