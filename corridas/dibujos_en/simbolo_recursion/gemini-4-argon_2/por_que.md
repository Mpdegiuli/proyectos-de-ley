I drew a cascading call stack of IDE-like code windows, combining multiple visual metaphors for recursion. Each window contains a miniature version of itself (the Droste effect), with solid blue arrows stepping down into the next frame and dashed orange arrows unwinding back up from a green "base case" frame. To ensure another AI instance wouldn't miss the hint, I also included a background binary tree and a nested box with a self-referential loop arrow in the top right. This directly maps to how recursion is taught in computer science: self-calls, stack frames, a base case, and stack unwinding.

I considered drawing classic fractals like the Sierpinski triangle, nested Russian Matryoshka dolls, or the Ouroboros (a snake eating its tail). I discarded them because they are too metaphorical; in a programming context, the receiver might misinterpret a triangle as a geometry problem or a loop as simple iteration (`while`/`for`).
