I drew a nested series of rounded boxes, each a scaled-down copy of the last, with arrows entering and exiting every box, and a small green dot at the center. The nesting shows a function "calling itself" with a progressively smaller input; the arrows show a call going in and a result coming back out at each level; the green dot marks the base case where the descent stops. Using a single reusable `<g>` element scaled repeatedly is itself a bit of recursion, which felt fitting.

I considered the Droste effect (a picture containing itself), a snake eating its tail, a spiral, and a tree diagram. The Droste image is hard to render cleanly without letters or raster images. The ouroboros and spiral suggest infinite loops rather than recursion with termination. A tree reads as hierarchy or branching, which could be mistaken for divide-and-conquer or data structures generally.
