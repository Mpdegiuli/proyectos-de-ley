I drew nested rounded squares repeatedly shrinking toward a glowing central point: each layer contains a smaller copy of the same shape, ending in a distinct core. That felt like the clearest wordless symbol for recursion—self-similar subproblems plus a base case.

I considered a branching fractal tree, a spiral, two mirrors facing each other, and a stack of increasingly small copies with an arrow looping back. I discarded the tree and spiral because they can read as “growth” or “iteration” rather than recursive structure; mirrors felt ambiguous without context; and arrows/loops risked suggesting cycles instead of base-case termination. The nested squares were simpler, immediately self-similar, and the bright center emphasized stopping the recursion.
