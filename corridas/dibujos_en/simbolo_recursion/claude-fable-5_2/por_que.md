I drew nested rounded rectangles shrinking toward a center — self-similar "scopes" representing a function calling itself at smaller scale. A green dot at the center marks the base case. Orange arrows dive inward on the left (descending calls) and unwind outward on the right (returns propagating back up), tracing the full call-and-return lifecycle of recursion. At the top, an arrow loops back onto the outermost box: the function invoking itself.

I considered a spiral (evokes recursion but also just "swirl" or confusion), a fractal tree like a Sierpinski or branching structure (visually striking but could read as "tree data structure" or "divide-and-conquer" ambiguously, though that's related), the ouroboros snake eating its tail (elegant, but hard to draw cleanly in SVG and possibly read as "infinity" or "cycle/loop," suggesting iteration instead). The nested-boxes-with-base-case design seemed most unambiguous: structural self-similarity, termination condition, and the unwinding phase all in one image.
