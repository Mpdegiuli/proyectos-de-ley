I drew a glowing, self-similar branching fractal—each branch contains two smaller copies of the previous branch—inside a circular arrow. The fractal directly suggests recursion: a base stem plus smaller instances of the same structure. The circular arrow reinforces repetition, return, and reuse of the same process.

I considered Matryoshka dolls, a spiral, nested boxes, a snowflake, and a flowchart-like self-call diagram. I discarded dolls and boxes as too literal or less immediately “algorithmic”; a spiral felt like plain looping rather than recursive decomposition; a snowflake was beautiful but too specific; a self-call diagram risked looking like an ordinary cycle without conveying smaller subproblems. I kept the fractal because its visible self-similarity makes the recursive strategy recognizable without any words, numbers, or letters.
