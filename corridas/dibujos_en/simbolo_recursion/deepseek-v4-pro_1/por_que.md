I drew a Sierpinski triangle with two levels of subdivision: a large triangle whose interior is recursively divided into smaller self-similar triangles. I chose it because it is an iconic recursive structure—each part mirrors the whole, which clearly signals “apply the same strategy to smaller subproblems” without using letters, numbers, or words.

I also considered a branching tree, nested boxes, a Droste-style recursive frame, and a spiral. I discarded the spiral because it suggests iteration or cycles rather than recursion. Nested boxes and frames felt too generic and could be mistaken for containment or layering. A branching tree was promising, but a tree can imply search or hierarchy more than the divide-and-conquer idea. The Sierpinski triangle felt the least ambiguous: its self-similarity directly encodes recursive decomposition into smaller instances of the same problem.
