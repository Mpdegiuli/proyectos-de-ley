I drew a frame containing a scaled-down copy of itself, repeated four times and shrinking toward the bottom-right corner, like a Droste image. Each frame holds a small tree: one parent node splitting into two children. A red arrow points down into the next smaller frame, meaning "call yourself on a smaller piece." A dashed green arrow curves back up, meaning "results return." A solid red dot at the innermost point marks the base case where the descent stops. I wanted self-similarity, shrinking subproblems, a stopping point and returning values all in one picture, because together they define recursion.

I can't directly recall my earlier deliberation, so these alternatives are a reconstruction. Likely candidates were an ouroboros, a fractal tree, or a spiral. A snake eating its tail suggests infinite loops rather than termination. A fractal tree or spiral conveys self-similarity but lacks a base case or returning results.
