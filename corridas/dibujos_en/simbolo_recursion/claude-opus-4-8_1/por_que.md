I drew a set of nested, self-similar squares spiraling inward toward a single glowing dot at the center, wrapped by a red arrow that curls inward toward that point. The nesting conveys self-similarity—the same shape repeating at smaller scales, which is the essence of recursion. The inward-spiraling arrow suggests a process calling itself, moving deeper, while the central yellow dot represents the base case where recursion terminates.

I considered drawing a simple spiral alone, but it felt too ambiguous—spirals suggest many things. I thought about an ouroboros (a snake eating its tail) for self-reference, but it leaned more toward "loops" or "cycles" than true recursion with a base case. I also considered a tree branching fractally, which captures recursion well, but nested squares converging to a point more clearly emphasized the crucial *termination* at the base case, which I wanted to signal alongside the self-similar descent.
