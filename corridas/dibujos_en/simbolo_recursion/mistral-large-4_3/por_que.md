I drew three nested rounded squares with a circular arrow looping around them and pointing back inward, plus a dot at the center. The nested boxes say “the problem contains a smaller copy of itself,” the arrow says “solve it, then come back,” and the center dot is the base case—the smallest self that stops the recursion.

I also considered an ouroboros (snake eating its tail), a mirror reflecting itself, a branching tree, and a stack of arrows. I discarded the snake because it reads as infinity or a loop, not self-similar solving; the tree reads as hierarchy or DP tabulation rather than a function calling itself; the mirror was too vague; and stacked arrows suggested iteration. Nested frames plus a return arrow felt like the most direct, word-free picture of “recurse on a smaller instance, then return.”
