I drew a recursive call tree inside a circular two-way flow. A large top node branches into smaller nodes, which branch again into terminal diamond-shaped base cases. Curved paths beneath them converge back toward the center, representing returned results combining as the call stack unwinds. The nested hexagon inside the root repeats the same shape at a smaller scale, reinforcing self-reference. The surrounding arrows suggest descent into subproblems and return from them.

I considered drawing a fractal, such as a Sierpiński triangle or nested spirals, because self-similarity strongly implies recursion. I discarded that because it might communicate only “pattern” or “infinity,” not a programming strategy. I also considered nested boxes or mirrored hands pointing at themselves, but those could suggest containment or self-reference without base cases. A branching tree with leaves and returning paths more clearly conveys recursive calls, termination, and result aggregation.
