I drew a recursion emblem: a large arrow curling almost back on itself surrounds a problem that splits into two smaller copies, each splitting again, ending in green dots as base cases. The repeated Y-shaped motif gives self-similarity, the downward arrows show decomposition into subproblems, and the outer loop says “apply the same strategy again.” The endpoints also avoid implying infinite recursion.

I considered a circular arrow alone, but it suggested iteration or retry. Nested boxes or a miniature copy of the whole image inside itself were less legible and could mean simple containment. An infinity sign risked suggesting nontermination, while a fractal alone could describe self-similar data rather than a solution method. I also considered call frames, but they emphasized implementation over strategy and were hard to read without code-like notation.
