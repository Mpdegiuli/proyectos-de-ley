I drew a visual blueprint of a recursive call stack, reinforced by secondary motifs of self-similarity. Centrally, shrinking function frames cascade downward. Cyan arrows emerge from inside each frame’s body to invoke the next smaller frame (winding the stack), while shrinking blocks on the left represent reducing the input size. The cascade terminates at a green "base case" frame, from which dashed green arrows bubble back up (unwinding/returning values). A faint fractal binary tree in the background and a nested loop emblem in the corner reinforce the theme. I chose this because the "call stack + base case + return path" is the universal textbook diagram for recursion in computer science.

I considered drawing purely mathematical fractals (like a Sierpinski triangle), Russian nesting dolls, or an Ouroboros. I discarded them as primary subjects because they might suggest geometry, data containment, or infinite loops (`while(true)`) rather than algorithmic recursion.
