I drew a droste-style nest: five rounded squares shrinking toward the centre, each rotated a little further, so the eye spirals inward and feels the same shape repeating at smaller scale. At the very middle sits a small orange arrow curling back on itself — the self-call, the base case where the spiral terminates. Two orange arcs sweep around the outer frame to reinforce the turning, returning motion. Shrinking-same-shape plus a loop-back arrow was the clearest way I could say "solve this by calling yourself on a smaller version" without a single letter.

I considered a Sierpiński triangle or Koch snowflake — beautiful self-similarity, but they read as "fractal art" rather than an instruction. A binary tree branching downward was tempting, and nearly as clear, though it hints specifically at divide-and-conquer or trees. An ouroboros snake felt like infinite loop, not recursion. Nested mirrors were too pictorial, and arrows alone were ambiguous.
