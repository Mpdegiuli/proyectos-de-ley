I drew a set of nested, concentric squares shrinking toward a central point, with a red spiral arrow winding inward through them. The nesting conveys self-similarity — a structure containing smaller versions of itself — while the inward spiral traces the path of a recursive call descending toward a base case (the small filled square at the center). Together: "solve by breaking into smaller versions of the same problem."

I considered a few alternatives and rejected them: a Sierpinski triangle (too specific to fractals, might suggest divide-and-conquer or geometry rather than recursion generally); an ouroboros snake (evocative of self-reference but reads more as "loop" or "cycle"); a tree with branching subtrees (good for recursion but ambiguous with trees-as-data-structure); and Russian nesting dolls rendered abstractly (too pictorial and hard to read without color detail). Nested shapes plus a spiral felt like the clearest, most universal visual metaphor for recursion.
