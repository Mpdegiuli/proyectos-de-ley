I drew a series of nested, downward-pointing triangles (a large one, a medium one inside it, and a small one inside that). This symbolizes recursion because it visually represents a self-similar structure—each smaller triangle mirrors the larger one, just as a recursive function calls a smaller instance of itself.

I considered other ideas like a spiral (to show repetition), a fractal tree (to show branching recursion), or a looped arrow (to show iteration). I discarded them because:
- A spiral might imply infinite loops rather than structured recursion.
- A fractal tree could be too complex or ambiguous without context.
- A looped arrow might suggest iteration rather than recursion.

The nested triangles are simple, universally recognizable, and directly evoke the concept of self-reference and repetition at smaller scales, which is the essence of recursion.
