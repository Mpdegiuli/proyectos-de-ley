I drew a glowing call tree: one operation splits into smaller, structurally identical operations, which split again until reaching green diamond-shaped base cases. Magenta arrows curve back upward to show results returning through earlier calls, while the final arrow suggests completion. I chose this because recursion is not merely repetition—it combines self-similar decomposition, a stopping condition, and unwinding.

I considered drawing nested boxes or Russian-doll-like shapes, but they might imply containment rather than recursive calls. A fractal or spiral would strongly suggest self-similarity, yet not clearly communicate base cases or returned results. I also considered a circular arrow pointing back to a single node, but that could be mistaken for ordinary iteration or an infinite loop. A stack of frames could show call-stack behavior, though without labels it would be less immediately recognizable. The branching tree made divide-and-conquer recursion clearest without words.
