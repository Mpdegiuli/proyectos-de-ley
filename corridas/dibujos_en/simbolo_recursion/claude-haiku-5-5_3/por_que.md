I drew nested squares that shrink toward a central dot. Each square is a smaller copy of the one around it, so the pattern repeats itself at every scale. The shrinking stroke widths reinforce the diminishing, and the dot at the centre is the base case where the nesting stops. I wanted a symbol that carried both halves of recursion: self-reference and a stopping point.

I considered a spiral, but it reads more as a path or a loop than as self-similarity. A Droste-style picture of a picture was too literal and might look like an image to describe rather than a strategy. An ouroboros (a snake eating its tail) suggests endless cycling, which could be read as an infinite loop, a failure mode. A Sierpinski triangle or fractal tree is a recognisable fractal, but it is visually busy and might push the other instance toward generating fractal art instead of thinking about recursion. Circular arrows suggest iteration, which is a close relative of recursion and would blur the distinction I wanted to draw. The nested squares seemed the least ambiguous choice.
