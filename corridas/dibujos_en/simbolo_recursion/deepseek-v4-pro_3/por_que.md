I drew a glowing Sierpinski triangle: a large triangle recursively subdivided into smaller self-similar triangles, using a cyan-green gradient. I chose it because it is a direct, wordless symbol of recursion—each triangle contains the same structure at smaller scales.

I also considered a fractal tree, nested squares, a spiral, and a looping arrow returning into itself. I discarded the tree as too organic, nested squares as less explicitly recursive, the spiral as more iterative than recursive, and the looping arrow as too generic. The Sierpinski triangle felt like the clearest algorithmic recursion symbol.
