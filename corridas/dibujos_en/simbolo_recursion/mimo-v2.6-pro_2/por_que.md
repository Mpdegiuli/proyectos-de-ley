I drew four nested, progressively smaller rounded squares, with the innermost one filled red, plus a red arc curving from outside inward ending in an arrowhead pointing at the center — a symbol of self-reference and descent toward a base case. The nesting says "same problem, smaller," the shrinking frames say "decreased input," the red core says "base case," and the sweeping arrow says "recurse until you reach it."

I considered a tree with self-similar branches, but recursion was too easily confused with general hierarchy or divide-and-conquer. I considered literal mirror-balls (Droste effect), but that reads as "fractal aesthetics" not strategy. I considered an ouroboros loop, but that signals iteration or circularity, not recursive calls returning to a base case. A spiral suggested infinite regress without termination. I chose the nested-rectangles motif because it is unambiguous: one structure containing itself, shrinking at each level, terminating visibly.
