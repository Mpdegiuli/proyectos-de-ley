I drew a set of nested, rotating, shrinking squares — each one a smaller copy of the outer frame — spiraling down to a single point at the center, with a curved arrow sweeping inward toward that core and a small secondary arrow suggesting a "return" path back out. The nested self-similar squares are meant to visually encode "a problem containing a smaller version of itself," the core signal for recursion, while the arrows imply the dive-down/bubble-up call-and-return motion.

I considered a few alternatives: a tree with branching sub-trees (too close to general "divide and conquer" without the self-similarity cue), Russian nesting dolls (clearer metaphor but harder to render abstractly without implying literal objects), and an Escher-style infinite staircase (visually interesting but less clean as a symbol). I settled on nested rotated squares since they're simple, scalable, and unambiguously self-referential.
