I drew **nested rounded squares**, each a smaller copy of the one before. In each frame a red arrow runs from a dark dot down into the next, smaller frame. The innermost frame holds a solid green disc instead of another arrow. I meant it as a picture of recursion: a function calling a smaller version of itself, shrinking each time, until it reaches a **base case** (the green "stop" disc). I used `<use>` to reuse one definition at every level, so even the drawing's construction mirrors self-reference.

I can't directly recall what I weighed while drawing; this is my reconstruction. Plausible alternatives include an ouroboros (snake eating its tail), a Droste-style infinite regress, a fractal tree, or a Sierpinski triangle. An ouroboros or endless regress shows self-reference but no termination, which might suggest infinite loops. Fractals look recursive but read as "geometry" rather than "strategy." Explicitly marking a base case seemed essential.
