I drew a nested-frame symbol: a large rounded rectangle containing a smaller similar rectangle, with arrows curving from the right edge back onto each frame, and an orange dot at the center. To me, it says: decompose the problem into a smaller version of itself, apply the same strategy, and stop at a base case. The outward-to-inward arrows suggest self-reference and return, while the center dot prevents an infinite loop.

I considered a fractal tree or spiral because they visually scream recursion, but they can read as decorative rather than instructional. I considered nested dolls or an ouroboros, but they felt metaphorical or suggested endlessness. I avoided letters, code syntax, and literal function-call diagrams to keep the cue immediate, abstract, and language-independent.
