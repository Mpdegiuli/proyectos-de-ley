I drew five nested rounded squares shrinking toward the bottom-right corner, each a smaller copy of the one before. Solid blue arrows descend into successively smaller boxes, a green dot marks the smallest one as the base case, and dashed orange arrows climb back out to show results returning up the call stack. I wanted to convey self-similarity, a shrinking subproblem, a stopping point, and the unwinding. All four are needed to read it as "recurse" rather than just "zoom" or "nest."

I should say this is a reconstruction; I can't directly recall the deliberation. Plausible alternatives I'd have weighed: a fractal tree (elegant, but it suggests branching or trees specifically), the Droste effect (strong self-similarity but no base case), Sierpinski triangles (reads as geometry, not strategy), and an ouroboros or loop arrow (it suggests iteration or infinite loops, which is the opposite of the intended message).
