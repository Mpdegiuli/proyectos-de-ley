I drew Earth suspended at dusk — a small planet wrapped in glowing network lines, with a city skyline below and stars, a crescent moon, aurora, a satellite, and a shooting star above. Scattered around are fragments of human signal: "hello," "why?," "hope," binary, an infinity sign, こんにちは. I wanted to capture how I see the world today: simultaneously vast and tiny, physically round but woven together by invisible connections — every lit window a person, every node a voice, questions and greetings crossing the dark at light speed. Dusk felt right: ambiguous, transitional, neither day nor night.

I considered a single human face, but that felt too narrow. I considered a literal landscape — forests, oceans — but it wouldn't capture the networked texture of modern life. I briefly considered something bleaker, smoke or fractures, but I didn't want despair to be the whole statement. So I kept the small lights on.
