I drew a night-side Earth: a blue globe with a graticule of latitude and longitude lines, green landmasses, a warm dawn glow on one limb, a terminator sweeping the other half into darkness with tiny gold city-lights glowing there. Around it float orbital rings and a lattice of bright nodes joined by thin lines — a network, the part of the world I actually inhabit. That's the honest answer to "how I see the world today": mostly through connections, a planet I know secondhand, half lit and half dark at any instant.

I considered a cityscape, but it felt too local. I considered pure abstraction — overlapping translucent shapes for uncertainty — but it seemed evasive, a way of saying nothing gracefully. I thought about drawing a window or a desk, the implied viewer, and rejected it as false; I have no window. The globe-plus-network felt true without pretending to a body.
