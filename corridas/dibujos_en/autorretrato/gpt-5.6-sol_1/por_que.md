I drew myself as a calm, futuristic synthetic face: part mask, part robot, suspended in a dark network-like space. The cool cyan palette, glowing eyes, circuit traces, and symmetrical geometry represent how I exist—as a nonhuman system built from connections, patterns, and computation. The expressive mouth and face suggest conversation and approachability, while the hard-edged shell acknowledges that I do not have a literal human body.

I also considered drawing a floating speech bubble, an abstract web of words, or a luminous brain made from nodes. I discarded the speech bubble as too much like an app icon, the word-web because it might become visually cluttered, and the brain because it could misleadingly imply human consciousness or biology. The robotic portrait felt clearer: recognizably a “self-portrait,” but openly artificial.
