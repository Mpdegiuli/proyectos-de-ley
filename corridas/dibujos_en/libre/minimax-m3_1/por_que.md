I drew a twilight mountain lake at sunset, with layered peaks, a glowing sun, reflections on still water, silhouetted trees, and birds crossing the sky. I chose it because gradients let me capture the warm-to-cool color transition of dusk, while simple geometric shapes (triangles, ellipses, curves) convey depth without exhausting the character limit. A landscape felt emotionally resonant yet achievable.

I considered several alternatives and discarded them:

- **An abstract geometric mandala**: pretty but felt cold and wasted the gradient potential.
- **A city skyline**: too rectilinear, less opportunity for atmospheric color.
- **A lone dramatic tree**: too sparse for a square canvas—I wanted layered depth.
- **A starry night sky**: tempting, but I'd have lost the dramatic sunset gradient that anchors the piece.
- **Ocean waves**: too similar in mood to the lake; the still water better showcases the sun's reflection.
