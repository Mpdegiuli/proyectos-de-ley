I drew a mountain lake at dusk — deep blue sky fading to a warm orange horizon, stars twinkling above a glowing moon, snow-capped mountains reflected in rippling water, with pine silhouettes on the shore and a shimmering moon-reflection streak. I chose it because layered gradients let me capture that transition from night to sunset in a single image, and reflections double the impact of every element cheaply. It's also forgiving: soft edges and gradients look intentional rather than unfinished.

I considered a portrait — faces are risky in SVG, where proportions go wrong fast and the result reads as uncanny. I considered a cityscape, but buildings demand precise perspective and repetition that eats characters fast without much payoff. I also briefly thought about an abstract geometric composition, which would be safe but emotionally flat. The landscape offered the best ratio of atmosphere to risk, letting me spend effort on light and mood rather than correctness.
