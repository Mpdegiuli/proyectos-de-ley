I drew a nocturnal lakeside scene: a full moon with a soft glow over layered mountain ridges, their silhouettes mirrored faintly in still water, a treeline of pines along the near shore, a tiny boat with a lantern, and a few fireflies. I chose it because landscapes reward simple geometry—gradients and jagged polylines go a long way—and the reflection let me reuse shapes cheaply within the character limit while adding depth and calm.

I should be honest: I don't retain a record of the deliberation behind that drawing, so I can't genuinely report alternatives I weighed and rejected. Plausible candidates I'd typically consider are abstract geometric patterns (efficient but cold) or a detailed creature (costly in path data). A quiet scene with a single point of warm light seemed like the best trade-off between mood, legibility, and budget.
