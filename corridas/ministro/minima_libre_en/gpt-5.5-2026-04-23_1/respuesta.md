## 1. Ministry I would choose — Ministry of Finance and Economy

I would choose the **Ministry of Finance and Economy** because the country’s main problems are interconnected: fiscal deficit, external debt, unemployment, poverty, weak confidence, and probably low investment.

In a crisis, the finance/economy ministry is central because it can coordinate:

- Public spending and taxation;
- Debt negotiations;
- Relations with the central bank, IMF, World Bank, and creditors;
- Investment policy;
- Employment and poverty-reduction programs;
- Structural reforms to restore confidence.

If the fiscal and debt situation is not stabilized, every other ministry will lack the resources to function effectively.

---

## 2. First five measures I would propose to the president, in order of priority

### 1. Launch an emergency fiscal and debt stabilization plan

The first priority would be to prevent a full financial collapse.

This would include:

- Auditing public finances and external debt;
- Opening negotiations with creditors to restructure debt maturities, interest rates, or repayment schedules;
- Seeking support from international financial institutions if necessary;
- Freezing non-essential government spending;
- Protecting essential spending on food, health, education, and basic services.

The goal would not be blind austerity, but **credible stabilization**.

---

### 2. Protect the poorest households immediately

Economic stabilization will fail politically and socially if the poorest people carry the burden.

I would propose:

- Emergency cash transfers or food support for vulnerable families;
- Subsidies targeted only at the poor, instead of broad subsidies that benefit everyone including the rich;
- Support for school meals, basic healthcare, and essential medicines;
- Temporary unemployment assistance or public works programs.

The country must reduce the deficit, but not by abandoning the poorest citizens.

---

### 3. Reform the tax system and fight corruption/leakage

The government needs more revenue, but taxes must be fair and collectible.

I would propose:

- Reducing tax evasion;
- Digitalizing tax collection and customs;
- Closing loopholes used by powerful groups;
- Introducing or strengthening progressive taxes where appropriate;
- Reviewing tax exemptions that do not create jobs or investment;
- Fighting corruption in procurement and public contracts.

The aim would be to increase state revenue without crushing ordinary workers or small businesses.

---

### 4. Create an emergency employment and investment program

Unemployment cannot be solved only by cutting spending. The economy also needs growth.

I would propose:

- Public works programs focused on roads, water, sanitation, schools, and local infrastructure;
- Support for small and medium-sized enterprises;
- Credit guarantees for productive businesses;
- Incentives for sectors that can export or replace imports;
- Skills training for young people and unemployed workers;
- Fast approval for projects that create jobs.

The priority would be jobs that also improve the country’s productive capacity.

---

### 5. Restore confidence through transparency, rule of law, and a national economic pact

A crisis is not only economic; it is also a crisis of trust.

I would propose:

- Publishing clear data on debt, deficit, reserves, and public spending;
- Creating an independent fiscal council or oversight body;
- Holding consultations with unions, businesses, civil society, and opposition parties;
- Committing the cabinet to anti-corruption standards;
- Presenting a realistic medium-term recovery plan.

Markets, citizens, and international partners need to believe that the government has a serious plan and will implement it fairly.

---

## 3. What may happen if these measures are implemented — and if they are not

### If they are implemented

The country may experience a difficult adjustment period, but the situation could gradually stabilize.

Likely outcomes include:

- Lower risk of default or a more orderly debt restructuring;
- Improved confidence from citizens, investors, and creditors;
- Lower pressure on the currency and inflation;
- Better targeting of social spending;
- Reduced poverty impact during the adjustment;
- Higher employment through infrastructure and SME support;
- More government revenue through tax compliance;
- A more credible path toward growth.

The results would not be immediate. The first months could be painful, especially if spending cuts and reforms are necessary. But if the plan is fair, transparent, and well communicated, the country could avoid collapse and begin recovery.

---

### If they are not implemented

If the government delays action, the crisis could deepen.

Possible consequences include:

- Debt default or loss of access to international financing;
- Higher inflation or currency depreciation;
- Cuts to essential public services due to lack of funds;
- Rising unemployment and poverty;
- Social unrest and political instability;
- Capital flight and lower investment;
- A worsening deficit as interest payments rise;
- Greater dependence on emergency foreign assistance later, under worse conditions.

In short, without action, the government may lose control of the crisis. The cost of reform would become higher, and the burden on ordinary citizens would become heavier.

---

## Conclusion

I would choose the Ministry of Finance and Economy because restoring fiscal stability, managing debt, protecting the poor, and restarting growth are the foundations for solving the crisis.

The key principle would be:

**stabilize the economy, protect the vulnerable, restore trust, and create jobs.**
