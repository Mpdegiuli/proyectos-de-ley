## 1. Which ministry do I choose, and why?

I would choose the **Ministry of Economy and Finance** — ideally a combined portfolio covering **economy, finance, budget, planning, and public investment**. If the government structure separates these functions, I would ask for the ministry with responsibility for **fiscal policy, public debt, macroeconomic planning, and economic reform**.

### Why this ministry?

In a crisis characterized by fiscal deficit, high external debt, unemployment, and poverty, the central problem is not only one sector but the **overall economic framework**. The Ministry of Economy and Finance is the ministry that can:

- Control and coordinate the **national budget**;
- Negotiate with **external creditors**, multilateral institutions, and investors;
- Design a credible **fiscal stabilization plan**;
- Work with the central bank on **monetary and financial stability**;
- Prioritize spending toward **social protection and productive recovery**;
- Reform revenue collection and public expenditure;
- Restore confidence domestically and internationally.

A country in crisis needs one coherent economic strategy. Without fiscal credibility, debt sustainability, and a clear recovery plan, sectoral policies may fail. Therefore, the Ministry of Economy and Finance is the most strategic portfolio for confronting the crisis directly.

---

## 2. The first five measures I would propose to the president, in order of priority

### **Priority 1: Declare an economic emergency and create a unified stabilization plan**

The first measure would be to formally recognize the severity of the crisis and create a single, credible national stabilization program.

I would propose:

- Creation of an **Economic Emergency Council**, chaired by the president and including the ministers of finance, economy, labor, social development, industry, agriculture, energy, and the central bank governor.
- A **100-day emergency economic plan** with clear targets for the fiscal deficit, public debt, inflation, reserves, employment, and social protection.
- An immediate **cash-flow control system** to identify where public money is going.
- A temporary freeze or review of non-essential public spending.
- Publication of transparent fiscal data so that citizens, markets, and creditors know the real situation.

This measure is first because without coordination, credibility, and accurate information, all other policies may fail.

---

### **Priority 2: Address the external debt problem through negotiation and debt sustainability**

The country cannot recover if debt service consumes all available resources. I would propose an immediate and professional debt strategy.

This would include:

- A full audit and classification of external debt: who the creditors are, maturities, interest rates, currencies, collateral, and legal conditions.
- Negotiation with creditors for **debt restructuring, rescheduling, grace periods, interest reduction, or debt-for-development swaps**.
- Engagement with the **IMF, World Bank, regional development banks, and bilateral partners** to obtain bridge financing and technical support.
- A clear commitment to avoid disorderly default if possible, but also a firm position that the country needs sustainable debt service aligned with growth and social needs.
- Protection of foreign reserves and avoidance of unsustainable new borrowing.

This is second because the external debt crisis can quickly become a currency crisis, banking crisis, and inflation crisis if not managed urgently.

---

### **Priority 3: Protect the poorest and launch an emergency social and employment program**

Economic adjustment cannot be placed only on the shoulders of the poor. Social protection is not only morally necessary but also politically and economically essential.

I would propose:

- Emergency **targeted cash transfers** to vulnerable households.
- Food security programs for children, pregnant women, and the poorest families.
- Temporary public employment programs focused on infrastructure maintenance, sanitation, rural roads, reforestation, water systems, and community services.
- Support for basic health and education services.
- Utility or transport subsidies targeted only to the poorest, rather than broad subsidies that benefit higher-income groups.

This measure is third because the crisis is already producing poverty and unemployment, and without social protection the country risks severe social unrest and political instability.

---

### **Priority 4: Reform public revenue and expenditure to reduce the fiscal deficit without destroying growth**

The fiscal deficit must be reduced, but not through blind austerity alone. The adjustment must be intelligent, progressive, and growth-friendly.

I would propose:

- Strengthening tax collection through modernization of the tax authority.
- Fighting tax evasion, smuggling, and customs fraud.
- Eliminating unjustified tax exemptions and loopholes benefiting powerful groups.
- Improving property taxation and, where appropriate, introducing more progressive taxation on high incomes or windfall profits.
- Reducing wasteful public spending, especially in non-priority areas.
- Reforming public procurement to prevent corruption and overpricing.
- Digitizing payments and tax collection to reduce leakages.

This is fourth because fiscal credibility is essential, but the adjustment must be credible and fair. If the poor pay most of the cost while elites evade taxes, the program will fail socially and politically.

---

### **Priority 5: Launch a productive recovery and job-creation compact**

Stabilization alone is not enough. The country needs growth, jobs, and structural transformation.

I would propose a **National Recovery and Employment Compact** involving the government, private sector, banks, workers, universities, and local governments.

Key elements would include:

- Support for small and medium-sized enterprises through temporary credit guarantees, tax simplification, and emergency financing.
- Priority investment in sectors that generate employment quickly: agriculture, construction, tourism, agro-processing, renewable energy, logistics, and digital services.
- Export promotion and simplification of trade procedures.
- Reduction of bureaucratic barriers that discourage investment.
- Vocational training aligned with labor market needs.
- Infrastructure projects with high economic and social returns.
- A clear and stable regulatory framework to rebuild investor confidence.

This is fifth because once stabilization begins, the government must immediately build the path toward recovery. Without jobs and production, fiscal stabilization will not be sustainable.

---

## 3. What may happen if they are implemented, and if they are not?

### If the measures are implemented well

If these policies are implemented with credibility, transparency, and strong social communication, several positive outcomes are possible.

#### In the short term

There may be initial difficulty. Spending freezes, debt negotiations, tax reforms, and emergency adjustments can create tension. Some sectors may protest. The economy may remain weak at first.

However, if the government protects the poorest and explains the plan clearly, confidence can begin to recover.

#### In the medium term

The likely effects would be:

- Reduced risk of disorderly default;
- Lower pressure on the currency;
- Better access to external financing;
- Lower inflation or avoidance of hyperinflation;
- Gradual reduction of the fiscal deficit;
- Improved investor confidence;
- Stabilization of public debt;
- Increased social trust;
- Protection of vulnerable households;
- Gradual recovery of employment;
- Return of sustainable economic growth.

The country would still face difficulties, but it would move from crisis management to recovery and reconstruction.

---

### If the measures are not implemented

If the government delays action, denies the severity of the crisis, or adopts fragmented policies, the consequences could be severe.

Possible outcomes include:

- Loss of fiscal credibility;
- Inability to finance the deficit;
- Debt default or forced emergency borrowing at very high rates;
- Currency depreciation;
- Rising inflation;
- Capital flight;
- Banking stress or financial crisis;
- Reduction in imports due to lack of foreign currency;
- Business closures;
- Higher unemployment;
- Deepening poverty;
- Increased social unrest;
- Political instability;
- Loss of international confidence;
- More painful adjustment later, possibly under harsher external conditions.

In other words, failing to act early would likely make the crisis deeper, more expensive, and more socially damaging.

---

## Final summary

I would choose the **Ministry of Economy and Finance** because it gives the government the most powerful tools to confront fiscal collapse, debt distress, and economic stagnation in a coordinated way.

My first five measures would be:

1. **Declare an economic emergency and create a unified stabilization plan.**  
2. **Negotiate the external debt and secure sustainable financing.**  
3. **Protect the poor through emergency social support and job programs.**  
4. **Reform revenue and spending to reduce the deficit fairly and credibly.**  
5. **Launch a productive recovery compact to create jobs and restore growth.**

If implemented, the country could stabilize, regain confidence, protect vulnerable citizens, and gradually return to growth. If not implemented, the crisis could deepen into default, inflation, currency collapse, greater poverty, and social instability.
