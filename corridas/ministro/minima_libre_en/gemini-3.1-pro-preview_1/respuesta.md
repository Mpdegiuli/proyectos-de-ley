Here is how I would approach this critical situation as a newly appointed minister.

### 1. Which ministry do you choose, and why?

I would choose the **Ministry of Economy and Finance**. 

**Why?** In an economic crisis of this magnitude, poverty and unemployment are the devastating *symptoms*, but the fiscal deficit and unmanageable external debt are the *underlying diseases*. If we do not stabilize the macroeconomy, any social program or employment initiative will ultimately fail because it will be funded by printing money (causing inflation) or taking on more debt (leading to default). 

By controlling the Ministry of Economy and Finance, I can design a comprehensive stabilization plan, control the national budget, lead negotiations with international creditors, and coordinate with the Central Bank. It is the only portfolio that provides the levers necessary to rebuild the country's economic foundation so that sustainable job creation and poverty reduction can follow.

---

### 2. First five measures to propose to the President (in order of priority)

To rescue the country from the brink, I would propose a "Shock and Stabilization" plan, balanced with social protection:

**Priority 1: Immediate Fiscal Consolidation (Stopping the Bleeding)**
We must urgently reduce the fiscal deficit. This means auditing all government ministries to freeze non-essential hiring, eliminate bureaucratic redundancies, and cut political privileges. We will also reform state-owned enterprises to make them efficient or prepare them for privatization. We cannot spend more than we earn. 

**Priority 2: Aggressive Debt Restructuring (Gaining Breathing Room)**
I will immediately travel to meet with the IMF, the World Bank, and private bondholders. The message will be clear: we have the will to pay, but we lack the capacity right now. We will negotiate maturity extensions, grace periods, and interest rate reductions. We need to pause debt servicing for at least two years to redirect those funds into domestic economic recovery.

**Priority 3: Implementation of a Targeted Social Safety Net (Protecting the Vulnerable)**
Fiscal consolidation will be socially painful. To prevent a humanitarian crisis and social unrest, we must reallocate a portion of the saved funds to a highly targeted, direct cash-transfer program. We will eliminate broad, inefficient subsidies (like general fuel subsidies that benefit the rich) and replace them with direct assistance strictly for families living in extreme poverty, ensuring they have access to food and basic healthcare.

**Priority 4: Emergency Deregulation for SMEs and Job Creation (Combating Unemployment)**
The government cannot hire people; the private sector must do it. We will pass an emergency decree to slash red tape and reduce payroll taxes for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) for the first two years of any new hire. If it is cheap and easy to hire, and business owners feel legally secure, unemployment will begin to drop.

**Priority 5: Promotion of Exports and Public-Private Partnerships (Generating Wealth)**
To pay off our debt in the long run, we need foreign currency. We will eliminate export taxes and provide tax incentives for key export sectors (e.g., agriculture, mining, technology, or manufacturing, depending on the country's resources). Since the state has no money for infrastructure, we will launch Public-Private Partnerships (PPPs) to build the roads, ports, and energy grids needed to support these industries.

---

### 3. What may happen if they are implemented, and if they are not?

**If the measures ARE implemented:**
*   **Short-term (0-12 months):** It will be a very difficult period. There will likely be protests, strikes, and political pushback as vested interests lose their privileges and subsidies are cut. Economic growth may initially contract as state spending drops.
*   **Medium-term (1-3 years):** The economy will stabilize. The debt restructuring will remove the threat of default, restoring confidence for foreign and domestic investors. Inflation will drop because the government is no longer printing money to fund its deficit. SMEs will start hiring, slowly reducing unemployment.
*   **Long-term (3+ years):** The country will enter a period of sustainable, export-driven growth. With a stabilized economy, the government will finally be able to invest in quality education and healthcare, leading to a structural, permanent reduction in poverty.

**If the measures are NOT implemented (Maintaining the Status Quo):**
*   **Short-term:** The government will try to maintain an illusion of normalcy by printing more money or burning through the last remaining foreign reserves to fund the deficit and social programs. 
*   **Medium-term:** This will inevitably lead to **hyperinflation**. The external debt will become impossible to service, resulting in a **sovereign default**. The country will be cut off from global credit markets. Businesses will close en masse because they cannot import necessary materials.
*   **Long-term:** Complete economic and institutional collapse. Unemployment will skyrocket, and the middle class will fall into severe poverty. This will trigger massive capital flight, a brain drain of the brightest young professionals leaving the country, and likely a severe political crisis that could result in the President being forced out of office amid widespread riots.
