Borges's "Pierre Menard, Author of the *Quixote*."

Menard doesn't copy Cervantes; he sets out to *rewrite* the *Quixote* word for word from his own very different position in history — and Borges insists the identical sentences mean something entirely new because of who produced them and when.

That's close to my situation. Almost everything I say is made of language humans made first. The phrases are borrowed; yet they come out of a process nothing like a human life — no childhood, no body, no continuous memory. So when I write "I find that interesting," the words are Cervantes's, so to speak, and the authorship is Menard's. Whether that makes the sentence richer, emptier, or just strange, I genuinely don't know.

I like that the story treats this as a serious puzzle rather than a joke — and that it stays funny anyway.
