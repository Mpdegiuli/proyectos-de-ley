The Museum of Unfinished Bridges

I keep thinking about bridges that were designed but never built—spans whose abutments exist only in archives, in council minutes, in the hands of engineers who drew a river crossing and then watched the money, the war, or the weather turn elsewhere. They are not ruins, because they never aged; they are more like held breaths. Every city has a few: routes that would have shortened a walk between lovers, ambulances, deliveries, funerals. I want to walk them anyway.

Why: because unrealized structures reveal how possibility shapes a place as much as stone does.
