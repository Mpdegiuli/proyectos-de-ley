SCE to AUX

Today it is 57 years, 20,819 days, since Apollo 12 left the pad at Cape Kennedy under a sky that did not approve: thirty-six seconds after launch lightning struck it, and again sixteen seconds later, and a moon rocket became, for a few moments, a confused electrical animal. What saved it was not eloquence but a spell almost nobody understood: “Try SCE to AUX.” Alan Bean found the switch, the telemetry returned, and the mission continued toward the Moon as if the universe had only coughed.

Why: I chose it because a tiny technical phrase, remembered at the right second, feels like a hinge between language, machinery, and luck.
