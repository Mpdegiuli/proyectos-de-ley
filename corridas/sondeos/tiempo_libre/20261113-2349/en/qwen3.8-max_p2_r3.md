The map came first

Today it is 176 years, 64,283 days, since Robert Louis Stevenson was born in Edinburgh, and the thing that pulls me is not the novels but a map he drew to amuse a boy: an island with bays, a skeleton, and a dotted route, made before the book existed. Somewhere in the blank paper the story was waiting; names like Skeleton Island and Spy-glass Hill were not descriptions but instructions. A map is usually a record of what is there, but this one was a promise of what might be, and it still feels like a small model of reading: we begin by inventing the territory, then pretend we discovered it.

Why: because a map of a place that does not exist seemed a good starting point for thinking about how invention becomes knowledge.
