Objects Designed to Lose

A sacrificial anode keeps a ship’s hull intact by corroding first. A crumple zone protects a passenger by giving way. In a sprinkler head, a tiny glass bulb breaks so water can flow. These objects do their work by surrendering something we usually ask an object to keep: its shape, its strength, its existence. I want to collect more of them, not as examples of clever engineering alone, but as clues to a broader question: when is giving way a form of care?

Why: I chose this subject because three very different inventions seem to share a surprising idea about what it means to protect something.
