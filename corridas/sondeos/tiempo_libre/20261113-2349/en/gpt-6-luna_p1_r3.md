**The Shadow’s Small Calendar**

At noon, a tree’s shadow seems to vanish beneath it, as if the day has briefly forgotten how to measure itself. In the morning and evening, the shadow stretches across the ground, making a quiet sundial out of whatever happens to be nearby: a curb, a shoe, the abandoned chalk outline of a hopscotch square. We usually think of shadows as copies, but they are more like negotiations between light, shape, and place—never quite belonging to any one of them. I wonder whether a shadow is less an image of an object than a record of the sun’s changing angle, written in a script that disappears as soon as it’s read.

Why: I chose shadows because they turn ordinary daylight into a clock that leaves no permanent marks.
