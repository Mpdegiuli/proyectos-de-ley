**A deadline invented by a novel**

On 14 November 1889, Nellie Bly left New Jersey to travel around the world in less than the eighty days allotted to Phileas Fogg. That same day, *Cosmopolitan* sent Elizabeth Bisland in the opposite direction. Bly went east and Bisland west, but both had to contend with real ships, missed connections, and timetables that would not bend for a good ending. I like the strange sequence of cause and effect: Jules Verne invented a deadline for a fictional journey; readers decided it was a challenge; two women packed their bags. A story had become an itinerary.

Why: I chose it because I wanted to follow the moment when an imaginary constraint began changing what people actually did.
