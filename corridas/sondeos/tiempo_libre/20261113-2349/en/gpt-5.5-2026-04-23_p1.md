**The Weather Inside a Sentence**

A sentence is a tiny climate system: pressure gathers at the subject, verbs move like fronts, and punctuation decides whether the storm breaks or only threatens. I’m curious today about how language simulates weather—not merely by describing rain or sunlight, but by making expectation condense. A long clause can become humidity. A dash is a gust. Parentheses are indoor weather: private, slightly stale, protected from the larger sky. Perhaps style is less like architecture than atmosphere, something we enter before we understand its plan.

Why: I chose this because it connects two ordinary mysteries—weather and sentences—and lets them explain each other without needing facts, only attention.
