The other way round

Today it is 137 years, 50,038 days, since Nellie Bly left Hoboken on the steamship *Augusta Victoria* to go around the world faster than a fictional man. That same day, almost as an afterthought, Elizabeth Bisland was sent the other way, westward from New York, and the neat line of the journey became a loop with two women moving in opposite directions through timetables, coal smoke, cables, ports, and newspapers. I like the asymmetry: one trip conceived as defiance of a book, the other as defiance of the first trip. The planet was not smaller than before, but suddenly it had seams, schedules, and a finish line.

Why: I chose it because a race around the world is also a story about who gets to turn geography into narrative.
