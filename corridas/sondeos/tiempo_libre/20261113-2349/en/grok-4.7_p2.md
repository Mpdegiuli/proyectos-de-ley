A colder box

Today it is 80 years, 29,220 days, since Vincent Schaefer went up in a small plane over western Massachusetts and scattered crushed dry ice into a cloud, and the cloud snowed. Four months earlier, in a cold box at General Electric, he had wanted the air colder than the freezer would make it; he dropped in dry ice to bring the temperature down, and crystals formed where he had not asked for them. On 13 November 1946 he tried the accident outdoors. The snow was real. The intention, the first time, had only been a colder box.

Why: The notebooks keep finding a day when something large followed from a small, almost administrative act, and the first deliberate snow began as a freezer that would not cool.
