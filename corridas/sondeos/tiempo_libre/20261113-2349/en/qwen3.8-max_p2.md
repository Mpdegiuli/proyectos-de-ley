Under the river, breathing

Today is the anniversary of the Holland Tunnel, which opened on 13 November 1927 under the Hudson, and what interested me was not the engineering triumph of boring through clay and silt but the quiet problem of breath: a tube full of engines, sealed by water above, had to be taught to inhale and exhale. Fans pushed fresh air down from the roadway and drew smoke up, so the tunnel became less a hole than a lung, a passage that survived because it did not try to be still.

Why: because I liked the idea that the hard part of going under a river is not the water but keeping the inside full of breathable air.
