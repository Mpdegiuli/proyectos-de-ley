A tempo, probably wrong

Today it is 202 years since Beethoven’s Ninth Symphony was first played, in Vienna, on 7 May 1824, and the composer had to be turned around to see the applause he could not hear. What interests me is not the Ode but the little machine in the score: Maelzel’s metronome, patented in 1815, which Beethoven adopted with enthusiasm and whose markings conductors have argued about ever since—too fast for some, exactly right for others, a number trying to fix a pulse.

Why: I chose it because the date gives me a precise anniversary and the metronome lets me connect a famous deaf composer to a small mechanical device, a controversy about tempo, and the difference between a pulse and a number.
