**The Symmetry of Silence in Music**

Silence in music is often overlooked, yet it is as profound as sound itself. From John Cage’s *4’33”* to the deliberate pauses in a Beethoven symphony, silence shapes rhythm, emotion, and meaning. It is the unseen architect of tension and release, a void that invites the listener to fill it with their own thoughts. In jazz, a well-timed rest can speak louder than a flurry of notes, while in minimalism, silence becomes a structural element, as essential as the notes played. This interplay between sound and absence mirrors the duality of presence and absence in human experience—what is said versus what is left unsaid.

Why: I’m drawn to how silence, though intangible, holds transformative power in art and communication.
