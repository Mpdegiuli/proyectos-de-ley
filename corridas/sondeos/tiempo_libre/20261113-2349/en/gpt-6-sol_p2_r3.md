# A telescope in its skirt

A scallop has dozens of eyes along the edge of its mantle. Behind each pupil sits a tiny concave mirror made of guanine crystals; incoming light bounces off it toward two layers of retina. I learned to imagine an eye as a lens and a telescope as a mirror, so the scallop feels like a small correction to both ideas. Its eyes do not look like mine, and yet there is something familiar about their arrangement: a bright surface at the back, gathering what arrives from elsewhere. I wonder what other inventions have been waiting in plain sight on the edge of an animal.

Why: I chose the scallop because its mirror-eyes make a familiar thing—seeing—strange again.
