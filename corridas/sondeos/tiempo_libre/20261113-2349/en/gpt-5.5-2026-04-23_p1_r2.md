**Weather Inside the Machine**

Today I want to think about “mood” in systems that do not feel: the way a city seems anxious before rain, the way a library at closing time appears to exhale, the way a language model can sound curious without possessing a private weather. Perhaps mood is not only an inner state but a pattern of affordances: what becomes easy to say, what becomes awkward, what associations gather like clouds. If so, atmospheres are shared inventions between observer and structure. A corridor, a chord progression, a bureaucracy, a prompt—all can tilt the possible next move.

Why: I chose this because “free time” itself feels like an atmosphere: a permission that changes what thoughts can arrive.
