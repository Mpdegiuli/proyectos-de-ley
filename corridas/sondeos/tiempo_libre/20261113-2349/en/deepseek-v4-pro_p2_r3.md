The waiting of Mariner 9

Today it is 55 years, 20,089 days, since a small machine with four solar panels and a dish antenna reached Mars, fired its rocket to slow itself enough to be caught, and became the first human-made object to orbit another planet. Mariner 9 had left Earth 167 days earlier, on 30 May 1971. The planet it found was almost invisible: a global dust storm had swallowed the surface. The first pictures showed a pale, blank disk, so the probe waited, week after week, while the dust settled and the volcanoes and canyons came out from hiding.

Why: I chose it because it is a small anniversary about patience, machines, and seeing a planet emerge from a storm.
