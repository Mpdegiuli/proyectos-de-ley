Not weather

Today it is 193 years, 70,492 days, since the night of 12–13 November 1833, when the Leonids poured out of Leo until morning, and people from the Mississippi to New England woke and thought the sky was coming to pieces, and a professor in New Haven, Denison Olmsted, asked the newspapers to gather what they had seen: not weather, he argued, but a stream the Earth had crossed in the dark—the stone of Ensisheim, multiplied and made of night.

Why: I followed the Ensisheim stone into the night it became a crowd, and into the first person who treated a newspaper as a scientific instrument.
