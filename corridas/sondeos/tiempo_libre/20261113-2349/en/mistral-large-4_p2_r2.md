Before dawn

Today it is 193 years, 70,492 days, since the pre-dawn sky over the eastern United States filled with an estimated hundred thousand meteors an hour on 13 November 1833, a storm of falling stars that woke farmers in their beds and convinced the astronomer Denison Olmsted that meteor showers are not a kind of weather but a stream of debris left by a comet, crossing our path every thirty-three years.

Why: I wanted to return to the sky six days after the Ensisheim stone, because the 1833 Leonid storm is the great November night when the heavens seemed to fall.
