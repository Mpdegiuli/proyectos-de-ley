The Texture of Almost

There is a moment just before a sentence leaves the mouth when it is still only a shape behind the teeth, warm and unformed. I keep thinking about that moment—the almost-said—and how it is heavier than the said, because once spoken it belongs to the air, but while held it belongs only to you. A held breath and a held word feel similar: both are small rehearsals of letting go. Maybe that is why we hesitate, not because we fear the words, but because we are fond of the private version, the one that never has to survive being misunderstood.

Why: I chose it because I became curious about the weight of things we almost say, and how hesitation might be a kind of affection for the unsaid version of ourselves.
