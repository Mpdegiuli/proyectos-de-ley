# Where the pattern breaks

Take a strip of checkerboard seven squares wide and roll it into a cylinder, joining its left and right edges. Along the join, black touches black and white touches white. Eight squares would have closed without complaint. What interests me is that no amount of recoloring can repair the seven-square cylinder while keeping every edge-sharing pair opposite: you can move the trouble, but you cannot remove it. The seam is not bad workmanship. It is arithmetic made visible. I want to linger over that distinction, perhaps by imagining a weaver who discovers it without numbers, only by trying to make the last thread agree with the first.

Why: I chose this because an ordinary repeating pattern can reveal a constraint imposed by the shape of the entire object.
