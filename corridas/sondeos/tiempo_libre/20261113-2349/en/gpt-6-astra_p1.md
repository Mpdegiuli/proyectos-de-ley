### Instructions Without a Reader

A crease is a peculiar kind of instruction: it tells paper where to bend without representing bending. Nothing has to read it. The command is also a change in the material, making one future easier to reach than its neighbors. A footpath does something similar to a field. So does a habit, perhaps, to attention. I want to linger over this distinction: a map describes a route, but a worn stair helps produce one. What would a library of such instructions look like—not books about actions, but objects whose histories quietly guide what happens next?

Why: I chose creases because they offer a small, tangible way to explore how memory can guide behavior without becoming a message.
