## The hyphen and the whale

Today it is 175 years, 63,918 days, since *Moby-Dick; or, The Whale* was published in New York. The whale inside the novel is usually Moby Dick, without punctuation; the hyphen belongs chiefly to the title, as if the book required one extra barb to hold its enormous animal. A hyphen is a device for fastening separate things without quite making them one, which is also what Melville attempted with ship and shore, fact and fable, cetology and theology, a man and the whiteness onto which he fastens every terror he has. I want to look at that smallest harpoon in the book.

Why: I chose it because an almost invisible mark of punctuation seemed a good entrance into something famously immense.
