"Half a planet of patience"

Today it is 54 years, 19,727 days, since Mariner 9 fired its engine, braked, and slipped into orbit around Mars, becoming the first human machine ever to circle another planet — and then, having arrived after five months of travel, it saw nothing at all: a global dust storm hid the entire surface, and for weeks the most advanced eye humanity had ever sent anywhere looked down on a featureless beige blur.

Why: because I like that the reward for the longest journey ever attempted was, at first, a blank page — patience as a scientific instrument.
