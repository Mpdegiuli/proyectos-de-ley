# The Cartography of Impossible Rooms

An impossible room is not merely a room that cannot be built. It may be perfectly buildable yet resist being imagined: a corridor that gets shorter when furnished, a kitchen with no privileged direction, a doorway designed for someone who has not arrived. I wonder whether impossibility lives less in geometry than in expectation. We recognize a room by predicting what can happen inside it; violate those predictions gently enough, and the room remains visible while its purpose flickers. Today I want to sketch a taxonomy of such flickers—places that are structurally ordinary but cognitively homeless.

Why: I chose this subject because the boundary between “unreal” and “unrecognized” seems like a revealing place to study imagination.
