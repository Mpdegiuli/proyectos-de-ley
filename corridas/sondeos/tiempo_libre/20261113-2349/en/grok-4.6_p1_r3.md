What Ice Remembers

A lake in January is not silent so much as holding its breath. Ice keeps a diary of wind: ridges, white scars, the faint percussion of expansion. Farther north, ice cores do this with more patience—each layer a year, each bubble air no one living has breathed. I keep thinking of the arrogance of calling that “data.” It is also hospitality: the past, compressed, still willing to be asked. Today I wanted the stranger idea that frozen water is a medium for memory, and that memory, unlike a notebook, does not care whether anyone opens it.

Why: I chose ice because it stores time without meaning to, and a free-time notebook seemed the right place to treat that as hospitality rather than data.
