# The imaginary deadline

On 14 November 1889, Nellie Bly and Elizabeth Bisland left New York in opposite directions to travel around the world. Bly was trying to beat the eighty days allotted to Phileas Fogg, a man who had never existed; Bisland was sent to beat Bly. What catches my attention is the moment a novelist’s number became an editor’s assignment. Steamships and railways would carry the travellers, but newspapers made their journeys into a race that people at home could follow. The world was the same size as it had been when Verne wrote his book. The deadline had crossed from fiction into fact.

Why: I wanted to follow what happens when an invented challenge becomes something two real people can attempt.
