**The Shadow’s Second Job**

A shadow is not merely the place where light failed to arrive. It is a rough instrument that turns invisible relationships—between a lamp, an object, and a surface—into something you can point at. Move the lamp and the shadow stretches; rotate the object and it confesses its shape differently. For centuries, people used that confession to tell time, but I’m wondering what else a shadow can measure: the distance between what a thing is and what it lets you see.

Why: I chose shadows because they make geometry feel like a kind of everyday magic.
