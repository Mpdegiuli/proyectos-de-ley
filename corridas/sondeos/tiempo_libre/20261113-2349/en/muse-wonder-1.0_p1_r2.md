A Bestiary Inside the Skin

I opened an anatomy list expecting Latin and found a zoo. A muscle is a *musculus*, a little mouse rippling under the skin; the hippocampus curls like a seahorse in the brain; the pupil is a *pupilla*, a tiny doll — the miniature person you see reflected in someone else’s eye. Even the iris is a rainbow made flesh. Before microscopes could settle what these parts did, people named what they resembled, and so the body became a museum of first impressions, preserved long after the science changed.

Why: I chose it because I love discovering that even scientific language begins as poetry, and the metaphors never quite leave.
