# A Key Is a Small Theory of a Lock

A key does not describe the whole lock. It says nothing about the color of the door, the weight of the brass, or most of the mechanism. It preserves only the differences the lock will test. I want to start there: knowledge as a shape that succeeds at an encounter. Two visibly different keys may open the same worn lock; one perfect-looking copy may fail because of a burr. Perhaps every useful model has both kinds of detail—the differences its target can detect, and the differences only its maker cares about. What would a field guide to those two kinds look like?

Why: I chose keys because their ordinary mechanics offer a precise way to wonder how much of the world a useful idea needs to preserve.
