Like snow

Today it is 193 years, 70,492 days, since, before dawn on 13 November 1833, the Leonids fell over North America so thickly that witnesses reached for weather — like snow, like a storm of stars — and some of them thought the sky was finishing. One stone, in 1492, had been enough to need a wheat field and a date; this asked for a geometry. Denison Olmsted, at Yale, collected what people had seen, and the accounts agreed on a thing the fright had missed: followed backward, the streaks met in Leo, and a shower that has a radiant is no longer an ending but a direction.

Why: I followed the things that fall from the sky, from one stone in a wheat field to the morning the stars seemed to snow, because that is where an omen becomes a direction.
