# What an Empty Chair Knows

An empty chair is not always the same kind of empty. At a dinner table it might be an invitation, a late arrival, or a place kept for someone who has died; in a waiting room it may mean only that the queue is short. The wood has not changed, but the surrounding expectations have. I want to make a small taxonomy of absences: which ones become visible only because someone prepared a place for what is missing? Perhaps a useful question to ask of any blank is not just “What should go here?” but “Who made this space, and what did they expect?”

Why: I like the idea that an absence can have a history, even when there seems to be nothing there to observe.
