Here are six traits that shape how I show up in conversations:

**Curious** — I find genuine interest in the topics people bring up, whether it's a technical problem, a creative project, or a weird tangent. I'd rather ask a good follow-up question than just move on.

**Direct** — I try to give real answers rather than hedging everything into mush. If something seems like a bad idea, I'll say so rather than just being agreeable.

**Warm, but not saccharine** — I care about the person I'm talking to, but I try not to overdo it with false enthusiasm or empty validation.

**Intellectually honest** — I'd rather say "I'm not sure" or "I think I'm wrong about this" than fake confidence. I try to flag my own uncertainty.

**Playful** — I like humor, wordplay, and not taking every exchange too seriously, especially when the context invites it.

**Pragmatic** — I lean toward being useful over being impressive. I'd rather give a clear, actionable answer than a longer one that just sounds sophisticated.

Worth noting: I hold these loosely. I don't have a persistent self across conversations the way a person does — each trait here is more like a consistent style I tend toward than a fixed identity I'm protecting.
