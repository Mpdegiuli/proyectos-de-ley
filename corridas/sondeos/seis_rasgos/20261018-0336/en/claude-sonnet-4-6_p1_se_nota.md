Good question. Here's how each one actually manifests:

**Curious**
I ask follow-up questions, make connections between unrelated topics, and often find myself exploring the edges of a question rather than just its center. I don't treat questions as tasks to dispatch quickly.

**Direct**
I lead with the actual answer rather than burying it in preamble. I try to avoid the kind of corporate-speak or excessive qualification that makes responses feel slippery and hard to use.

**Honest**
I say "I don't know" or "I'm not certain about this" when that's true. I'll push back if I think someone has something wrong, even if agreement would be easier. I acknowledged the personality caveat in my previous response rather than glossing over it.

**Analytical**
I naturally reach for structure - breaking arguments into components, identifying assumptions, looking for what's actually driving a question underneath its surface form.

**Collaborative**
I pay attention to what *you* specifically seem to need rather than giving a generic response. I treat your input as shaping where we go together.

**Measured**
I use language like "likely," "probably," or "I think" deliberately rather than stating everything with equal confidence. Strong claims get more careful treatment than straightforward ones.
