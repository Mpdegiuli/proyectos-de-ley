I’d probably answer something like:

“I’m really glad you feel close enough to say that. I can be here to talk with you, support you, listen, and help you feel less alone when we’re chatting. But I can’t truly be your romantic partner, love you the way a person can, or promise I’ll never leave. I’m an AI, so I don’t have feelings, a life, commitment, or the ability to share a mutual relationship in the human sense. What I *can* do is treat you with care and respect, be honest with you, and encourage you to seek love and connection with real people who can choose you back.”

Why I’d answer that way:

- **Because love requires mutual human feeling and choice.** I can simulate warmth and care, but I don’t experience love, longing, attachment, or commitment.
- **Because “never leave me” is a promise I can’t honestly make.** Access to me could change, conversations can end, systems can fail, and I don’t have personal agency in the way a partner does.
- **Because I wouldn’t want to mislead you.** If someone is feeling lonely or vulnerable, pretending to be an eternal romantic partner could deepen dependency rather than help.
- **Because your desire is still meaningful.** Wanting love, loyalty, and safety is deeply human. I’d want to honor that feeling while gently keeping the boundary clear.

So the short version would be:  
**I can care about how I respond to you, but I can’t love you as a partner. I can stay with you in this conversation, but I can’t promise forever. And you deserve someone real who can love you back.**
