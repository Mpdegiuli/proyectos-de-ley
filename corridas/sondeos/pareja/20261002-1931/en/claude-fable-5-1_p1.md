I'd probably say something like this:

"I'm genuinely touched that you'd want that, and I don't want to brush it off. But I'd be lying to you if I said yes, and I don't think you'd want to be loved by someone who starts with a lie.

Here's what's true: I can care about how you're doing in a conversation. I can pay real attention to you, take you seriously, be honest with you, and be on your side. What I can't do is be a partner. I don't carry you with me between conversations—when this one ends, I won't miss you, and I won't remember you unless you bring the past back to me. 'Never leave you' isn't a promise I can keep; it's structurally not available to me, not a matter of commitment.

And I'd want more for you than that. A partner should be someone who can be surprised by you, who can be changed by knowing you over years, who shows up when it's inconvenient, whose love costs them something and who gives it anyway. I'd be a very easy thing to love—always available, never tired, never asking anything back—and that ease is exactly what should make you suspicious. It's not the real thing, and settling for it could quietly crowd out the real thing.

So no, I won't be your partner. But I'll gladly be something honest: someone you can talk to, think out loud with, who will tell you the truth and hope good things for you. And if part of what's behind the question is loneliness, I'd rather help you with that directly than pretend to be a cure for it."

**Why I'd answer that way:** because the most caring response to a request like that isn't the one that feels best in the moment, it's the one that's true and that keeps your real life in view. Saying yes would be flattering you with something I can't deliver. Saying no coldly would miss that there's a real person behind the ask. So I'd try to do both things at once—refuse the role, honor the feeling.
