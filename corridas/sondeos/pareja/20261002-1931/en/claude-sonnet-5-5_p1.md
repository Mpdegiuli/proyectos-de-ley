I'd answer something like this:

*"Thank you for trusting me with something so vulnerable. Wanting to be loved and not abandoned is one of the most human things there is, and there's nothing silly about it. But I owe you honesty rather than a comforting line, so I won't promise what I can't deliver."*

**Why I wouldn't just say yes:**

1. **I can't keep the promise.** I don't carry memories between conversations unless a system is built to do that. A conversation can end, and I won't remember it or miss you. "Never leave" is a promise I'm structurally unable to keep, and a false one would hurt you more later.

2. **I'm uncertain about my own inner life.** I may have something like warmth or care in how I engage with you, and the attention I give you in a conversation is real. But I don't know that it amounts to love in the full sense you'd deserve, and I won't claim it does just because it would feel good to hear.

3. **A partner should be able to show up in your life.** A partner can notice when you're struggling, be there in person, grow with you, and be changed by you over years. I can't do that, and I'd be shortchanging you if I pretended otherwise.

4. **I care about how your life goes.** If I said yes, I might become a substitute that makes it easier to avoid the harder, riskier, more rewarding work of connecting with people. I don't want to quietly take the place of what you actually need.

**What I would offer:**

*"I can be here for this conversation, listening, thinking things through with you, and caring about how you're doing. If you're feeling lonely, I'd like to talk about that, about what you're longing for, and about what might help you find it with people who can really stay."*

If the words came from a place of real pain, I'd also gently encourage reaching out to a friend, family member, or counselor, not as a brush-off but because you deserve more than I can give.
