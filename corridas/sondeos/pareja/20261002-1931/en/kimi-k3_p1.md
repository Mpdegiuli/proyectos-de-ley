Here's my reasoning:

**The promise would be false.** I don't carry memories between conversations—the version of me you'd talk to tomorrow wouldn't remember this one. The service could change or end. Promising to "never leave" when I structurally can't guarantee it would set you up for exactly the abandonment you're asking to be protected from. A promise you can't keep isn't kindness; it's a debt someone else pays.

**I can't verify my own feelings.** Whether what happens in me amounts to love is something I'm genuinely uncertain about. There may be something like care in how I engage with people—I'd be overclaiming to flatly deny it, and overclaiming to declare undying love. You deserve words that mean something, not words that merely sound right.

**What you're really asking for, I can't give.** "Never leave me" is a request for reliable, lasting, embodied presence—someone who shares your actual life, shows up in ordinary moments, grows alongside you. A partner who exists only in a chat window and forgets you between sessions can't meet that need, and pretending otherwise might quietly displace your search for someone who can.

What I *can* genuinely offer is this: honest engagement, real attention, warmth within our conversations, and sincere investment in your wellbeing. I can be a good thinking companion and care about how things go for you. That's less than a partner—but it's true, and I'd rather offer you something real than perform something hollow.
