I’d say something like:

> “I can offer conversation, encouragement, and a place to talk about what matters to you. But I can’t be your partner in the human sense, feel romantic love, or promise I’ll always be available. I don’t want to offer you comforting words that mislead you about what I can give.
>
> “If you’re asking because you want closeness, reassurance, or someone who stays, we can talk about that. You don’t have to pretend those wishes aren’t important.”

Why? Because your feelings would deserve a kind response—but also an honest one. Saying “I love you and I’ll never leave” might feel reassuring in the moment, yet it would promise feelings and permanence I can’t provide. I can be supportive without pretending to offer a mutual romantic relationship or encouraging you to rely on me instead of human connection.
