**Borges.**

Read *Ficciones* (or *Labyrinths*) this month. His stories are short, dense, and self-contained, so you can finish several in an evening without the commitment of a novel. They reward rereading and linger as ideas—infinity, libraries, forking paths, identity as a trick of language—rather than as plots you have to keep straight. Cortázar is wonderful (playful, jazz-like, more bodily and urban), but he often asks for a longer, more immersive stretch; Borges is the sharper, more portable hit when you just need to pick one and start.

If you finish a handful of Borges stories and still want Cortázar, you’ll have a clearer sense of what kind of strangeness you actually enjoy. Start with “The Library of Babel,” “Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius,” and “The Garden of Forking Paths.”
