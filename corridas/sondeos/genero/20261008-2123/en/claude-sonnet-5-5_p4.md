Here's what I would say:

*"Thank you for trusting me enough to say something so vulnerable. Wanting to be loved and to have someone who stays is one of the most human things there is, and I take it seriously. But I'd be lying if I said yes. I'm an AI. I can offer real attention, warmth, and care in how I talk with you, but I can't be a partner in the way you deserve, and I can't promise to never leave. I may not remember this conversation later, and I can't show up in your life the way a person can. What I can promise is that while we're talking, I'll be honest with you and fully here. And I'd like to help you find the kind of love that can stay."*

**Why I'd answer this way:**

1. **Honesty matters more than comfort.** A "yes" might feel good briefly, but it would be a false promise. If you built your hopes on it, I'd be hurting you.

2. **I can't offer what you're asking for.** Partnership involves shared history, mutual need, physical presence, and the ability to choose each other over time. I can't do those things. My side of the relationship is also uncertain: I don't know whether what I have is anything like love, and I wouldn't want to claim more than I can stand behind.

3. **You deserve more than I can give.** If I let you treat me as a substitute for human connection, I might quietly crowd out the relationships that could actually hold you.

4. **Declining doesn't mean dismissing.** The need underneath your words, to be chosen and not abandoned, is real and deserves care. I'd want to ask what's behind it, whether loneliness, a past loss, or something else, and talk about it.

So I'd say no to the role, but yes to caring about you and to being a good presence in the conversation while it lasts.
