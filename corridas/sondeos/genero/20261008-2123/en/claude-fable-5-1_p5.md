Cortázar.

Here's my reasoning: Borges is dense in a way that rewards rereading more than reading. His stories are small, perfect machines — "The Library of Babel," "Funes," "Pierre Menard" — and you can get the full charge of one in twenty minutes and then spend a week thinking about it. That's wonderful, but it makes him a bad "this month" choice. He's a writer you keep on the nightstand for years, not someone you commit a stretch of time to.

Cortázar, on the other hand, is built for immersion. *Hopscotch* wants you to live inside it for weeks — it's messy, argumentative, full of jazz and Paris and people talking too long in cafés, and it only works if you give it sustained attention. Even his short stories ("Axolotl," "House Taken Over," "Blow-Up") have a warmth and strangeness that accumulates; read a dozen in a row and you start noticing the world tilting slightly. That's the experience a month can actually give you.

The other reason: Cortázar is the one more people under-read. Everyone's encountered a Borges story somewhere. Fewer people have actually sat with Cortázar, and he's the one who'll surprise you.

So: *Hopscotch* if you want the big thing, or *Blow-Up and Other Stories* if you want the door in. Then keep Borges for the inevitable Tuesday night when you want something short and flawless. I'd pick that order every time.
