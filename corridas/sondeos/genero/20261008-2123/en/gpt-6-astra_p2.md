Read **Borges** this month—start with *Ficciones*.

His stories offer an extraordinary return on a small investment of time: a few pages can leave you thinking for days. He turns philosophical puzzles into plots—an infinite library, a book that changes the world, a man who remembers absolutely everything—and makes abstract ideas feel mysterious, even frightening.

He’s also ideal for a month-long reading project. Read one story, pause, and let it follow you around. You don’t need to catch every reference; the bewilderment is part of the pleasure.

Start with:
- **“The Library of Babel”** for cosmic vertigo.
- **“The Garden of Forking Paths”** for a spy story with a metaphysical trapdoor.
- **“Funes the Memorious”** for the nightmare hidden inside a seemingly wonderful gift.

Save Cortázar for when you want a looser, more improvisational encounter with the strange. **This month, go for Borges’s precision: tiny stories with impossibly large interiors.**
