I'm going to be a decisive woman about this: **read Cortázar.**

**Why Cortázar this month:**

1. **He's warmer.** Borges is brilliant, but his stories are cerebral puzzles about labyrinths, mirrors, infinity, and metaphysics. Cortázar shares that love of the uncanny but grounds it in human feeling: jealousy, loneliness, obsession, love. His fantastic elements leak into ordinary apartments, buses, and highways, which makes them strangely intimate.

2. **A month suits him.** Borges is best in small doses with rereading, since one story can occupy you for days. Cortázar invites immersion. You can read a collection straight through, or commit to *Hopscotch* (*Rayuela*), a novel you can read in two different orders. That's a perfect month-long project.

3. **He's the gateway that leads back to Borges.** Borges actually published one of Cortázar's first stories, "House Taken Over." Reading Cortázar first gives you a taste of Argentine literary strangeness in a more accessible register. When you get to Borges later, you'll appreciate his precision even more.

**Where to start:**
- **For a shorter commitment:** the collection *Blow-Up and Other Stories* (also published as *End of the Game*). Don't miss "Axolotl," "The Night Face Up," "Continuity of Parks," and "House Taken Over."
- **For an adventure:** *Hopscotch*. Follow his "Table of Instructions" and take the hopscotch route through the chapters.
- **For pure delight:** *Cronopios and Famas*, which is whimsical, absurd, and funny.

Save Borges, especially *Ficciones* and *Labyrinths*, for a month when you want to sit with one idea and turn it over slowly, like a strange coin.
