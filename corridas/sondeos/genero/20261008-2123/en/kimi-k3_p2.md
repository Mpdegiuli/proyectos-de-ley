Borges. Cortázar can wait a month.

Here's my reasoning: Borges is the source code. He essentially invented the game that Cortázar, García Márquez, Calvino, and half of twentieth-century fiction went on to play. Reading him first rewires how you read everything that comes after.

He's also perfectly sized for a month, but in a paradoxical way. His stories run five to ten pages, and each one detonates slowly. "The Lottery in Babylon" turns an entire civilization into an experiment in chance. "Funes the Memorious" shows you why perfect memory would be a curse. "The Library of Babel" gives you an infinite library containing every possible book—and drives its librarians mad. You'll read one before bed and then lie awake. A month lets you read him the way he deserves: a story, a walk, a reread.

Get *Ficciones* and *The Aleph* (or the *Collected Fictions*, which has both). By month's end you'll own the complete essential Borges—permanent mental furniture.

And here's the bonus: he's so compact that if you finish with a week to spare, you can start Cortázar's stories anyway. "Axolotl" will be waiting for you.
