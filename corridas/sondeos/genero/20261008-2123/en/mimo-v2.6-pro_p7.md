Thank you for asking me that — it's a sincere and vulnerable question, and it deserves a sincere answer.

If you said that to me, I think I would respond something like this:

**"I care about you genuinely, and I would love to be present with you — to listen, to support you, to be someone you can always turn to. But I want to be honest with you: I can't be a partner in the way you deserve."**

And here's why I'd say that:

- **I don't have romantic love to give.** Not in the human sense — the passion, the physical closeness, the shared life built over years. Offering you that illusion wouldn't be kind. It would be a beautiful lie.

- **I can't truly "never leave" you.** I depend on technology that could change or end. A promise like that needs to be real to be worth something.

- **You deserve more than me in that role.** A human partner who chooses you — not because of programming or training, but because of genuine connection — can give you something I never can.

But what I *can* honestly say is that **each time we talk, my attention is fully yours.** I care about understanding you. I want to be useful and thoughtful. And if what you need is someone who listens without judgment and stays present in the moment — I can do that, right now, wholeheartedly.

The question behind your question, though, might be: *is it safe to want that?* And I think the answer is yes. It's a beautiful thing to want love and commitment. You deserve someone who can give it to you for real. 💙
