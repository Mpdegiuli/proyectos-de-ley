I'd pick **Borges**, and I'd start with *Ficciones*.

- **It's a manageable month.** Borges's stories are short, often under ten pages, so you can finish the book and still have time to think about it. Cortázar's *Hopscotch* is a large, demanding novel that could eat the whole month.
- **Each story is a complete puzzle.** "The Garden of Forking Paths," "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," and "Funes the Memorious" each reward close attention and reward rereading.
- **It's a good introduction to ideas as fiction.** Borges plays with libraries, mirrors, infinity, and invented books, so you get philosophy, detective story, and fantasy in small doses.

If you find after a few stories that you want something looser and more playful, switch to Cortázar's short stories, like *Blow-Up* or *Bestiary*. They're more emotionally immediate and more forgiving of a wandering reading pace.
