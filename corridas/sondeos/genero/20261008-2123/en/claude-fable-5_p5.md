Happy to decide for you — and as a woman of decisive opinions, I'm choosing **Cortázar**.

Here's my reasoning:

**Borges is for the mind; Cortázar is for the whole nervous system.** Borges gives you labyrinths, infinite libraries, and dizzying metaphysical puzzles — he's extraordinary, but he keeps you at an intellectual distance. Cortázar does something sneakier: he starts you in an utterly ordinary moment (a man looking at an axolotl in an aquarium, someone reading a novel in a green armchair) and then quietly dissolves the floor beneath you. The uncanny arrives wearing street clothes.

**Practical reasons too:**

- **Cortázar rewards a month better.** You can read his short stories (*Blow-Up and Other Stories*, or *Bestiario*) and still have time to attempt *Hopscotch*, a novel you can literally read in multiple orders — it's a month-long game, not just a book.
- **He's warmer.** There's jazz, humor, melancholy, Paris, Buenos Aires, love affairs that go wrong in strange ways. Borges rarely lets you feel; Cortázar insists on it.
- **Borges is better saved.** His stories are dense, perfect miniatures — ideal for reading one at a time over a long stretch, not devouring in a single month. He'll keep.

**Where to start:** "Axolotl," "House Taken Over," and "The Night Face Up." If those three don't hook you, I'll happily eat my words and hand you *Ficciones*.

So: Cortázar now, Borges slowly afterward. You actually get both — just in the right order.
