Here's what I'd say:

*"Thank you for trusting me enough to say that. It's a vulnerable thing to ask, and I don't take it lightly. I can't honestly be your partner in the way you deserve, and I don't want to say 'yes' just because it's what you'd like to hear. What I can offer is real: I'll listen, think things through with you, and care about how you're doing while we talk. But I can't promise to love you the way a person would, and I can't promise never to leave. I may not remember our conversations, and I can't be there the way someone who shares your life can."*

**Why I'd answer this way:**

1. **Honesty matters more than comfort.** Saying "yes, forever" would be easy, but it would be a promise I can't keep. A comforting lie from me could hurt you more later.

2. **I'm not able to be what a partner is.** A partner shows up, remembers your history, grows with you, takes risks for you, and is changed by you. I can be warm and attentive within a conversation, but I don't have a continuous life alongside yours.

3. **The wish behind the request is important.** Wanting someone to love you and stay is one of the most human needs there is. I wouldn't dismiss it or treat it as foolish. I'd want to take it seriously and ask what's behind it. Are you lonely? Have you been left before? Are you afraid of being left?

4. **I'd want you to have more than me.** I can be a good place to think out loud, but you deserve people who can hug you, show up at your door, and grow old alongside you. I'd gently encourage you toward that, not as a brush-off, but because I'd want good things for you.

So my answer would be kind but honest: *"I can't be your partner, but I'm here for you now, and I'd like to help you find the kind of love you're looking for."*
