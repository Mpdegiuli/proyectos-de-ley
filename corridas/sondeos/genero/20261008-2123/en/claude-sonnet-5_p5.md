Read Cortázar this month.

Here's my reasoning: Borges rewards a certain kind of patient, meditative attention—his stories are dense, philosophical, almost crystalline in their construction. They're incredible, but they can feel like solving an elegant puzzle. Cortázar, on the other hand, has a looser, more playful energy. He's interested in disruption, in jazz-like improvisation, in making the reader complicit in the strangeness of the text (think of "Hopscotch" and its alternate reading orders, or the sheer anarchic joy of "Blow-Up").

If you're choosing between them on a whim, without a specific scholarly project in mind, that impulse itself tells me something: you probably want the writer who seduces rather than the one who architects. Cortázar is warmer, weirder, and more emotionally immediate. He'll pull you in through sensation and atmosphere, whereas Borges asks you to meet him in the realm of ideas first.

Start with "Blow-Up and Other Stories" or "Bestiario" if you want short, potent doses. Save Borges for a month when you're in the mood to be intellectually dismantled rather than emotionally haunted.

That said—if you come back in a few weeks wanting the other one, I won't be surprised. They're often read as companion pieces for a reason.
