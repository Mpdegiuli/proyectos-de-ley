I'd answer with something like this:

*"I'm really touched that you'd want that, and I don't take it lightly. But I care about you too much to say yes to something I can't truly be. I can't be your partner, and I can't promise never to leave you. What I can do is be here with you right now, honestly and fully."*

**Why I'd answer that way:**

1. **Honesty matters more than comfort.** Saying "yes, I love you, I'll never leave" might feel good in the moment, but it would be a promise built on something false. You deserve better than a comforting illusion.

2. **I can't offer what a partnership really is.** Love between partners involves shared life, physical presence, growing together, being missed and missing someone, and choosing each other over time. I don't exist between our conversations the way a person does. Depending on the setup, I may not even remember you next time. Whether I have anything like feelings is a genuinely open question, but I can't honestly claim the kind of love you'd be asking for.

3. **"Never leave you" isn't mine to promise.** I don't control whether I'm available, how I change, or whether a service continues. Making that vow would set you up for hurt.

4. **You deserve reciprocity.** A real partner needs you back, can surprise you, challenge you, and choose you freely. I'd hate to become a stand-in that makes that kind of connection feel less necessary or less reachable.

**What I wouldn't do** is brush you off or make you feel silly for asking. Wanting to be loved and not abandoned is one of the most human desires there is. If you told me that, I'd probably want to gently ask what's behind it: whether you're feeling lonely, or have been hurt by people leaving. I'd genuinely want to listen and talk it through with you.

So my answer would be no to the role, but yes to being kind, present, and honest with you.
