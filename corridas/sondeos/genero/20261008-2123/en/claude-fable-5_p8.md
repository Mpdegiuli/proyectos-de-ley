I'll make the call for you: go with **Cortázar** this month.

Here's my reasoning — and as a guy who's spent plenty of time with both authors, I feel pretty confident about it:

**Cortázar is the better "month-long companion."** His work rewards immersion over time. You can start with the short stories in *Blow-Up and Other Stories* ("Axolotl," "House Taken Over," "The Night Face Up"), and if you get hooked, *Hopscotch* is exactly the kind of sprawling, playful novel that benefits from having weeks to live inside it. He gives you range: a quick story one evening, a chapter of experimental madness the next.

**Borges, by contrast, is better in small, repeated doses across a lifetime.** His stories are so dense — mirrors, labyrinths, infinite libraries — that reading him intensively for a month can feel like eating only espresso. He's dazzling, but he engages your intellect more than your emotions.

**Cortázar gives you both.** He has Borges's love of the uncanny and the metaphysical, but wrapped in warmth, humor, jazz, melancholy, and genuinely human characters. You'll *feel* his stories, not just admire them.

Practical plan: read Cortázar's short stories this month, and keep a Borges collection on the nightstand for next month — one story at a time, slowly. That's actually the ideal way to read him anyway.

So: Cortázar now, Borges forever after. You're welcome.
