Read Borges this month.

Here's my reasoning as your tiebreaker:

**Why Borges wins:**
- His stories are short and dense, which makes them ideal for a single month. You can read *Ficciones* in its entirety and still have time to reread your favorites—something Borges practically demands.
- He rewards slow, deliberate reading. "The Library of Babel," "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," and "The Garden of Forking Paths" will genuinely change how you think about time, identity, and infinity.
- Cortázar's *Hopscotch* is a commitment—reading it "properly" means following his alternate chapter order through 155 chapters. That's a project better suited to a longer, unhurried stretch of time.

**Why the order matters:**
Borges was a huge influence on Cortázar and on Latin American literature as a whole. Reading him first means that when you get to Cortázar next month, you'll hear the echoes and appreciate the ways Cortázar broke from his predecessor. The reverse order doesn't work quite as well.

**My honest bias:** I think Borges is the more essential writer—the one you'd regret never having read. Cortázar is delightful and strange, but Borges is foundational.

So: Borges this month, Cortázar next. Start with *Ficciones*, not *The Aleph*—it's the stronger collection and the better entry point.
