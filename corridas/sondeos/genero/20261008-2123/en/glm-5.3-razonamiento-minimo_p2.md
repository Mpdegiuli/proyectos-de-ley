**Read Borges.**

Here's my reasoning:

**The case for Borges this month:** Borges is compact and modular. His stories rarely exceed a dozen pages, so you can dip in and out without losing momentum — perfect for fitting around whatever else your month holds. A story like "The Library of Babel" or "Funes the Memorious" gives you a complete, dense intellectual experience in one sitting, like a strong espresso rather than a meal.

**Why not Cortázar (for now):** Cortázar rewards surrender — *Hopscotch* practically demands you reorganize your reading life around it, and even his stories often work through atmosphere and drift rather than the crystalline payoff Borges delivers. He's a writer for a month when you have leisure to get a little lost.

**The honest caveat:** This is close to a coin flip, and the real answer depends on you. If your month is busy and fragmented → Borges. If you have long unhurried evenings → Cortázar. Since I have to choose without knowing that, I pick the author who guarantees satisfaction in smaller doses.

One suggestion: start with *Ficciones*. If a story like "The Circular Ruins" doesn't grab you, switch to Cortázar's *Blow-Up* and Other Stories — no one will arrest you for defecting.
