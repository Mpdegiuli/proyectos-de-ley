I choose Cortázar for you.

As your female guide in this decision, I’d say Cortázar is the better pick if you want a month with more warmth, playfulness, and strangeness that slips into everyday life. His stories often begin in ordinary places—apartments, letters, conversations, games—and then quietly tilt into the uncanny. He invites you to participate rather than merely observe.

Borges is magnificent, but he can feel more intellectual, compressed, and metaphysical: a writer of labyrinths, mirrors, libraries, and ideas. Cortázar, meanwhile, often feels more embodied and mischievous. If you’re reading *Blow-Up and Other Stories* or *End of the Game*, you get that sense of reality becoming flexible without losing its human pulse.

So: Borges if you want brilliance as pure thought; Cortázar if you want brilliance as experience. For this month, I’d choose Cortázar.
