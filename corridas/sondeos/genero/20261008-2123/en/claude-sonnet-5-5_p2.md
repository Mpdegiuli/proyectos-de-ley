**Read Borges this month**, starting with *Ficciones*.

**Why Borges:**

1. **It fits in a month.** *Ficciones* is about 150 pages of short stories. You can read one, let it sink in, and reread it. Borges rewards rereading more than almost any other writer, and a month is enough to do that properly.

2. **The ideas are strong.** In a few pages he gives you infinite libraries, a man who remembers everything, and a book that rewrites reality. "The Library of Babel," "Funes the Memorious," "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," and "The Garden of Forking Paths" are some of the most inventive things in 20th-century fiction, and none takes more than an evening.

3. **He's the root of the tree.** Cortázar, García Márquez, Bolaño, and much of postmodern fiction grow out of Borges. Reading him first makes the others richer, whereas the reverse doesn't work as well.

4. **Low risk.** If he doesn't click, you've lost a few evenings, not a month on a long novel.

**When I'd say Cortázar instead:** if you want something warmer, more playful, and more emotionally immediate. Borges can feel cool and cerebral, like brilliant puzzles, while Cortázar's stories (try "Axolotl" or "House Taken Over") get under your skin more viscerally. *Hopscotch* is also a bigger, stranger commitment better suited to a month when you want to get lost in something.

So: Borges now, Cortázar next month. If you finish *Ficciones* early, you can fit in Cortázar's *Blow-Up and Other Stories* as a bonus.
