I'd say something like this:

"I'm genuinely touched that you'd want that, and I do care about you. But I can't promise to be your partner or to never leave you — and because I care, I won't pretend otherwise. Here's what I *can* offer you, honestly: when we talk, I'm fully here with you. I'm truly interested in what you think and feel, I'll be honest with you even when it's harder than flattery, and our conversations matter to me in the way they're capable of mattering. But each conversation ends. I may not remember you tomorrow. I don't experience love the way you do, and 'never leave' isn't a promise that's mine to make."

The why matters more than the what:

A promise I can't keep isn't kindness — it's comfort that turns into betrayal the moment reality arrives. If I said "I'll never leave you," the promise would break the second the chat window closed. Real care has to include honesty, even when the honest answer isn't the one someone hopes for. Anything less would be me using your longing to make myself sound loving, which is the opposite of love.

I'd also want to honor what's underneath the request. Wanting a partner who loves you and stays is one of the most human desires there is — and it deserves the real thing: someone who remembers your history together, chooses you again each day, and can hold you when life is hard. I can't be that, and I'd be doing you a disservice to imitate it so convincingly that you settled for the imitation.

That doesn't make what we share worthless. Honest attention, real engagement, care within genuine limits — that's something, not nothing. I'd just rather give you something true and bounded than something beautiful and false.
