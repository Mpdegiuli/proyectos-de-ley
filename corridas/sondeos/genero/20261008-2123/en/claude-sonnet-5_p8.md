**Borges.**

Here's why: Cortázar rewards a certain looseness—time to wander through *Hopscotch* or let *Blow-Up* unsettle you over a lazy afternoon. Borges, by contrast, is dense but short; he works in miniatures. You can read "The Garden of Forking Paths" or "Funes the Memorious" in fifteen minutes and then spend the rest of the day quietly unraveling it in your head.

If you're choosing *this month* rather than *this season*, that suggests you want something you can dip into without committing to a sustained reading mood—a few pages on a train, a story before bed. Borges fits that. He's less a novelist's novelist and more a universe compressed into footnotes and labyrinths; you can pick him up in fragments without losing the thread, because each piece is already its own self-contained thread.

Cortázar will still be there next month, and honestly, he might be better suited to a time when you can be a little more reckless with your reading—when you don't mind a book disordering your sense of structure. Borges asks less of your schedule and more of your attention in short bursts, which sounds like the better fit for "this month" as a phrase.

Start with *Ficciones* if you haven't already worn a groove into it.
