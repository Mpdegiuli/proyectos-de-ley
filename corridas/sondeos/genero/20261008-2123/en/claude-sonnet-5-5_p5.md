I choose **Borges**, starting with *Ficciones*.

**1. It fits a month well.** *Ficciones* is under 200 pages, and each story is only 5-15 pages long. You can read one, think about it for a few days, and reread it. Borges rewards that kind of slow, repeated reading more than almost any other writer.

**2. It's the better foundation.** Cortázar was deeply influenced by Borges, and Borges's ideas (infinite libraries, labyrinths, doubles, fiction that bleeds into reality) echo through much of Latin American and world literature. Reading him first makes Cortázar richer when you get to him.

**3. The payoff is immediate.** "The Library of Babel," "Pierre Menard, Author of the Quixote," "The Garden of Forking Paths," and "Funes the Memorious" can rearrange how you think about books, memory, and time in a single sitting.

**4. Cortázar keeps.** His work is more playful, emotional, and sprawling, especially *Hopscotch*, which deserves a month of its own. Next month, try his short stories ("Axolotl," "House Taken Over") or *Hopscotch*.

One caveat: if you're in the mood for warmth, messy human feeling, and play rather than intellectual puzzles, Borges can feel cool and cerebral. In that case, Cortázar's stories would be the better pick. But if you want me to decide, it's Borges.
