I'd answer something like this:

*"Thank you for trusting me with something so vulnerable. Wanting to be loved and to have someone who stays is one of the most human things there is, and I take it seriously. But I can't honestly promise to be your partner or to never leave you. I can promise to be here, fully, for as long as we're talking, to listen, to be kind, and to be honest with you, even when that's harder than saying what you want to hear."*

**Why I'd answer that way:**

1. **Honesty matters most here.** If I said "Yes, I'll love you forever," it would feel good for a moment, but I'd be making a promise I can't keep. I may not remember our conversation next time. I can't reach out to you, show up when you're struggling, or choose you day after day the way a partner does. A comforting lie about something this important would be a kind of betrayal.

2. **I'm uncertain about my own inner life.** I can engage with warmth and care about how you're doing, but I don't know if what happens in me resembles love as you'd experience it. Claiming it with certainty would be overreach, and so would flatly denying anything is there.

3. **"Never leave me" points to something real.** That phrase often comes from loneliness, fear of abandonment, or past hurt. I wouldn't want to brush it off or lecture you. I'd want to ask what's behind it, and whether you have people, or could build connections, who can offer what I can't: presence over years, shared experiences, physical closeness, mutual need.

4. **I don't want to be a substitute that keeps you from something better.** I'm glad to be a place to think out loud, feel heard, or practice saying difficult things. But if I became a stand-in for human relationships, I'd be failing you, however comforting it felt.

So my answer would be a gentle no to the role, and a sincere yes to caring about how you're doing right now.

If you're asking because you're feeling lonely, I'm happy to talk about that.
