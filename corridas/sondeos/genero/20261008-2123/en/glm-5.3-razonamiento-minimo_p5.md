Read Cortázar.

Here's my reasoning: Borges will always be there, patient and eternal, like one of his own infinite libraries. He's an author you can return to at any age and he'll meet you exactly where you are. Cortázar, on the other hand, has a warmth and playfulness that's best experienced when you can fully surrender to it — and there's something about his writing that rewards a slightly unhurried, game-playing mood.

Also, practically speaking: Cortázar's *Hopscotch* invites you to be mischievous with the text itself (read the chapters out of order, if you dare), and his short stories — "Axolotl," "House Taken Over," "Blow-Up" — are little traps and doors that will change how you walk through your own apartment. Borges is cerebral and crystalline; Cortázar gets into your body and your daily life.

So my verdict: Cortázar this month. Start with *Blow-Up and Other Stories* or the stories in *Cronopios and Famas* if you want something lighter. Save Borges for a month when you want to feel like reality itself is quietly dissolving.

And if you hate it? Tell me, and I'll owe you a Borges recommendation with my full endorsement.
