I’ll decide for you: go with **Borges**.

Here’s why: Borges is a master of the short, dense, philosophical story. For a month of reading, that’s ideal—you can read one story a night, let it rattle around in your head, and not feel overwhelmed. His stories are like elegant little labyrinths about time, mirrors, infinity, identity, and the nature of reality. Start with *Ficciones* or *The Aleph*; stories like “The Library of Babel,” “The Garden of Forking Paths,” and “The South” will stay with you for years.

Cortázar is wonderful, but he’s more playful, jazz-like, and sometimes sprawling—*Hopscotch* especially is a bigger commitment. I’d save him for next month. Borges first: he’ll rewire the way you think, and he’s one of those writers everyone should read at least once.

Trust me—Borges this month. Then Cortázar after.
