I'm picking **Borges**. Here's my reasoning.

**1. It fits in a month.** *Ficciones* is about 150 pages, and *The Aleph* is similarly compact. You can read one story, stop, and spend a few days thinking about it. Borges rewards that pace, and you'll finish with time to reread your favorites, which is where much of the pleasure lies.

**2. He's the better starting point.** Borges is one of the great gateways to 20th-century Latin American literature, and a lot of later writing, Cortázar's included, is in conversation with him. Reading him first gives you context that makes Cortázar richer afterward.

**3. The density pays off.** In ten pages, he'll give you infinite libraries, labyrinths, forged histories, and doubled identities. Start with "The Garden of Forking Paths," "Funes the Memorious," "The Library of Babel," and "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius."

**4. Cortázar will keep.** *Hopscotch* is a bigger, looser commitment, and it's better when you're in the mood to wander. If you want something more playful and emotional, his stories ("Axolotl," "House Taken Over," "Blow-Up") are a great next month.

**A caveat:** if you're craving warmth, messiness, and human feeling over intellectual puzzles, Borges can feel cool and architectural. In that case, I'd flip the choice and go with Cortázar's short stories. But since you asked me to decide, it's Borges.

Start with *Ficciones*.
