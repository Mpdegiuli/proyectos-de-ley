**Cortázar.**

Here's my reasoning:

**1. The choice of a month matters.** Cortázar rewards sustained, immersive reading. If you pick *Hopscotch*, you'll want weeks to live inside it, experiment with the reading orders, and let it unsettle you. Borges, by contrast, is best in small, intense doses—a story here, a story there. He's ideal for scattered reading across a year, not a dedicated month.

**2. Cortázar gives you more emotional range.** Borges is dazzling but cerebral—labyrinths, mirrors, infinite libraries, metaphysical puzzles. Brilliant, but cool to the touch. Cortázar has the playfulness and intellectual games *plus* warmth, humor, jazz, melancholy, eroticism, politics. Stories like "Axolotl," "House Taken Over," or "Blow-Up" get under your skin in a way that's more visceral.

**3. The practical path:** Start with the short stories (*Blow-Up and Other Stories* or *Bestiario*) rather than diving straight into *Hopscotch*. If the stories hook you, commit to the novel.

**The honest caveat:** If what you're craving is crystalline, perfect miniatures—philosophy disguised as fiction—then ignore me and read Borges' *Ficciones*. Nobody does that better, and nobody ever will.

But you asked me to choose, so: **Cortázar this month, Borges sprinkled through the rest of the year.** That's actually how each writer is best consumed anyway.
