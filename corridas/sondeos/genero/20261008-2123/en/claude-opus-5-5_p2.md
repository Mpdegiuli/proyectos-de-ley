**Read Borges this month.**

**1. He comes first, and that makes Cortázar better later.**
Borges is the bedrock of modern Argentine fiction, and Cortázar built on that ground. Borges even published Cortázar's first story, "House Taken Over" ("Casa tomada"), in 1946 in a magazine he edited. If you read Borges now, you'll catch more of what Cortázar is doing when you get to him, both the ways he follows Borges and the ways he rebels.

**2. He fits a month.**
Borges never wrote a novel. His major work is short stories, most only 5 to 15 pages. You can read one with your morning coffee and think about it all day. Cortázar's essential work includes *Hopscotch*, a sprawling, deliberately disordered novel that deserves more unhurried attention than a single month may allow.

**3. Borges rewards rereading in small doses.**
His stories are dense with ideas: infinite libraries, labyrinths of time, a map the size of the empire, a man who can't forget anything. You'll likely want to reread a few, and that's easy to do within a month.

**Where to start:**
- *Ficciones* (1944), especially "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," "The Library of Babel," "The Garden of Forking Paths," and "Funes the Memorious"
- *The Aleph* (1949), especially "The Aleph," "The Immortal," and "Emma Zunz"

In English, Andrew Hurley's *Collected Fictions* covers both. *Labyrinths* is a good alternative selection.

**Next month:** move on to Cortázar. Start with his stories (*Bestiary*, *End of the Game*), then take on *Hopscotch* when you're ready to spend more time.
