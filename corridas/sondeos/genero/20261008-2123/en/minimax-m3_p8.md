Go with **Cortázar** this month.

Here's why: Borges is the more cerebral, architectural writer — his stories are like polished gemstones, exquisite but cool. Reading him is an intellectual pleasure, but a somewhat austere one. He's the kind of author you admire more than you feel.

Cortázar, by contrast, is warmer, wilder, and more emotionally available. His work has the genuine strangeness of dreams rather than the rigorous strangeness of philosophy. A story like "Casa tomada" or "La noche boca arriba" will stay with you in a way that has nothing to do with cleverness — it's almost physical. He plays with form (short stories that read like novels, novels you can read in different orders) in a way that feels mischievous rather than austere.

Practically speaking, his range is also better for a single month. You can sample the early, more fantastical stories in *Final del juego* or *Bestiario*, then graduate to the denser, more experimental work in *Las armas secretas*, and if you're still hungry, tackle *Rayuela* — which will occupy you for months afterward.

Start with **"Bestiario"** and the title story — about fifteen pages long — and you'll know within a few pages whether I was right.

(Read Borges next month. He's worth waiting for.)
