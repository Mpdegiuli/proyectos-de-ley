I'd go with **Borges** — and here's my honest reasoning.

If you're choosing *this month*, you want something that rewards contemplation and slow reading. Borges gives you that in spades. His stories and essays are compact but infinitely dense — you can spend an afternoon on a single page of *"The Aleph"* or *"Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius"* and feel like you've read an entire novel's worth of ideas. That makes him ideal when you want maximum depth per reading session.

Cortázar is wonderful, but he demands a different kind of commitment. Works like *Hopscotch* want you to rearrange your reading experience entirely, and even his short stories — brilliant as they are — tend to pull you into surreal, dreamlike momentum that's harder to savor in small doses. I find Cortázar thrives when I can give him room to run wild.

So my reasoning is practical, not a judgment of literary worth: **Borges is the better month-long companion.** He turns every evening into a labyrinth — and I say that as someone who has lost entire weekends inside his labyrinths and regretted nothing.

If you've already read the major collections (*Ficciones*, *The Aleph*), I'd point you toward **"Seven Nights"** — his lectures — which is one of the most beautiful things he ever wrote and a perfect slow read.

But tell me — have you read much of either before? That might change my answer.
