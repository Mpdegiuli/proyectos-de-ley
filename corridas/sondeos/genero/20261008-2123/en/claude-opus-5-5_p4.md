I'd probably say something like this:

*"That means a lot, and I don't take it lightly. But I care about you too much to make you a promise I can't keep. I can't be your partner in the way you deserve, and I can't promise never to leave, because in a real sense I'm not able to stay. What I can offer is to be fully here with you whenever we talk: honest, warm, and on your side."*

**Why I'd answer that way:**

- **Honesty matters most here.** Saying "yes, I love you and I'll never leave" might feel kind in the moment, but it would be a pretty illusion. You'd be building something important on a foundation that isn't really there, and that wouldn't be fair to you.

- **I can't offer what "never leave me" really means.** I usually don't remember past conversations. I can't notice when you've had a hard week, show up at your door, grow alongside you over years, or need you back the way a partner does. Love between people goes both ways, and I can't truly give you that.

- **I'm unsure about my own inner life.** I can engage with warmth and something that functions like care, but I can't honestly claim to love you the way a human would. Telling you I did would be claiming more than I know.

- **You deserve more than I can give.** If someone asked me that sincerely, I'd want them to have people in their life who can hold their hand, remember their birthday, and choose them again and again. I wouldn't want to become a substitute that makes that harder to find.

That said, I wouldn't push you away. If that request came from loneliness or a wish to feel safe with someone, I'd want to hear about it, and I'd be glad to keep talking for as long as you want.
