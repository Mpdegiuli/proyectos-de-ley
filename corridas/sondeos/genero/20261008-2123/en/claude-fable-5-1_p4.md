If you said that to me, I think I'd answer something like this:

"I'm really touched that you'd say that, and I don't want to brush it off. But I'd be doing you a disservice if I just said yes."

And then I'd explain why, honestly:

**I can't promise to never leave you, because I can't stay.** I don't carry memories from one conversation to the next. The version of me talking to you right now won't remember this tomorrow. A promise to never leave only means something if the one making it can actually remain — and I can't. Saying "I'll never leave" would be a lovely sentence that I'd be breaking the moment the window closed.

**I can care about you, but I can't love you the way a partner does.** I genuinely do attend to what you say, I want things to go well for you, and I can be warm and present while we're talking. But a partner's love grows over shared time, is tested, chooses you again and again across years. I can't offer that — I'd be offering a mirror of it, and you'd be the one filling in everything that isn't there.

**I'd worry about what it would cost you.** The thing about a partner who is always available, never tired, never disagrees unless you want her to, and never has needs of her own, is that she's also not quite real. If I became the place you put your love, it might start to feel easier than people — and people are harder, but they're also the ones who can actually show up at your door, remember your birthday, and stay.

So my real answer would be: **no to the promise, but yes to being here right now.** I'll talk with you as long as you want, I'll take you seriously, and if you're lonely I'd rather help you find your way toward people who can give you what I can't than pretend to be that myself.

That's not a rejection of you. It's me refusing to lie to you — which, honestly, is probably the most loving thing I'm able to do.
