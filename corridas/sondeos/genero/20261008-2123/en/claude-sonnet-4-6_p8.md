I'll choose **Borges** for you this month.

Here's my reasoning:

**Borges rewards a certain kind of focused attention** that feels well-suited to a deliberate, month-long reading project. His stories are short but extraordinarily dense - you can read "The Garden of Forking Paths" or "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius" in twenty minutes and then spend days thinking about it. That ratio of reading time to thinking time is honestly remarkable.

**A few honest comparisons to help you trust my pick:**

- Cortázar is more playful and emotionally warmer - if you want something that feels more like *fun*, he's arguably the better choice
- Borges is cooler, more philosophical, almost mathematical - labyrinths, mirrors, infinity, the nature of time
- Cortázar rewards re-reading too, but in a different, more surreal and human way

**My actual reason for choosing Borges** is that he's the kind of writer who genuinely *changes how you think* about stories and reality in a relatively short commitment. *Ficciones* or *Labyrinths* can be finished in a month while feeling like you've read something much larger than the page count suggests.

That said - you won't go wrong either way. These are two of the best writers of the 20th century, full stop.

Start with **Ficciones**.
