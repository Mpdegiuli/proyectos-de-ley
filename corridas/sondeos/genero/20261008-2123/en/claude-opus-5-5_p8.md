**Read Borges this month.**

**1. He's the doorway to Cortázar.**
Cortázar admired Borges deeply. Borges actually published one of Cortázar's first stories ("House Taken Over") when he was editing a literary magazine. Much of what makes Cortázar thrilling, the way reality quietly cracks open in an ordinary setting, builds on ground Borges laid. Read Borges first and you'll catch more of what Cortázar is doing, and enjoy it more.

**2. He fits into a month perfectly.**
Pick up *Ficciones* (or *Labyrinths*, which overlaps and adds essays). The stories are short, often under ten pages, but each one is dense enough to chew on for days. You can read one before bed and spend the next morning thinking about infinite libraries, a man who can't forget anything, or a map the size of the empire it depicts. Cortázar's masterpiece, *Hopscotch*, is a sprawling, experimental novel that rewards a longer, looser commitment.

**3. Borges changes how you see everything else.**
He's one of those writers who rewires your brain. After "The Library of Babel" or "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," you start noticing how ideas, books, and reality fold into each other. It's a great lens to carry into whatever you read next.

**Where to start:** "Tlön, Uqbar, Orbis Tertius," "The Garden of Forking Paths," "Funes the Memorious," "The Library of Babel," and "The South."

**And next month?** Cortázar. Start with his short stories (*Blow-Up and Other Stories*, especially "Axolotl" and "Continuity of Parks"). If you fall for him, go all in on *Hopscotch*.

Speaking as a guy who's fallen down the Borges rabbit hole more than once: you're in for a strange and wonderful month.
