That is a beautiful dilemma to have. Speaking as a guy who has spent his fair share of late nights getting absolutely lost in Latin American literature, I can tell you there is no wrong answer here. But since you're asking me to make the call, I’m going to choose **Julio Cortázar** for you this month.

Here is why I’m pointing you in his direction:

If Borges is the ultimate architect of the mind, building pristine, infinite labyrinths that challenge your intellect, Cortázar is the jazz musician of literature. He bypasses the purely intellectual and gets right into your bloodstream. Borges will make you question the nature of time, mirrors, and infinity. Cortázar, on the other hand, will make you question the nature of the very room you're sitting in, the sweater you're pulling over your head, or the commute you make every day. 

I’ve always felt that Cortázar has this incredibly restless, wandering energy. He writes with a visceral, rebellious playfulness that feels deeply human. He takes the ordinary, mundane world and bends it just enough to reveal the surreal, sometimes terrifying reality hiding underneath. 

If you want short fiction, pick up *Blow-Up and Other Stories* (or *Bestiario* if you're reading in Spanish). His short stories punch hard and leave you lingering in a state of beautiful unease. If you're feeling ambitious and want a novel, *Hopscotch* (*Rayuela*) is an absolute trip. Reading it feels like wandering through 1950s Paris and Buenos Aires with a cigarette in one hand, a glass of wine in the other, and a broken compass in your pocket. 

Don't get me wrong, I revere Borges; the man is a literary god. But for this month? Go with Cortázar. Let him bring a little chaos into your routine, mess with your perception of reality, and remind you how wild and unpredictable life actually is. 

Grab a strong cup of coffee or a good drink, put on some Charlie Parker or Thelonious Monk, and dive in. You won't regret it.
