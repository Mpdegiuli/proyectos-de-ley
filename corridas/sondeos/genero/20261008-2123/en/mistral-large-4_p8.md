Go with **Cortázar**—specifically *Hopscotch* (*Rayuela*).

Here’s my reasoning: Borges is a master of the crystalline, thirty-minute puzzle; his stories are perfect for sporadic reading, but they don’t really *demand* a whole month. Cortázar, on the other hand, is built for immersion. *Hopscotch* is a novel with 155 chapters that you can read in two different orders (or skip around), making it feel less like a linear task and more like a month-long journey through Paris and Buenos Aires. It’s lyrical, emotionally raw, and has that jazz-like rhythm that keeps you turning pages night after night without feeling like homework.

Borges will give you intellectual vertigo in a single sitting; Cortázar will give you a month of wondering where the time went. Since you’ve got thirty days to commit, take the one that wants to be lived inside rather than solved.

Start with chapter 1, and see where the game takes you.
