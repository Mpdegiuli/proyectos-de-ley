This month, you are going to read **Julio Cortázar**. 

While both are undisputed titans of Latin American literature, they offer entirely different experiences. Here is why Cortázar is the perfect choice to consume your month, and why Borges can wait.

**1. Cortázar is an experience; Borges is an intellectual exercise.**
Borges is the architect of the mind. Reading him is like wandering through a cold, beautiful, infinite library or solving a metaphysical puzzle. He is cerebral, erudite, and requires a lot of pauses to stare at the wall and ponder the nature of infinity. 
Cortázar, on the other hand, is the pulse of the blood. His writing is visceral, bohemian, and jazz-soaked. He doesn’t put you in a metaphysical labyrinth; he puts you in a messy apartment in Paris or Buenos Aires, and then slowly lets the surreal bleed into everyday life. 

**2. The surrealism of the everyday**
While Borges writes about minotaurs, imaginary encyclopedias, and immortal men, Cortázar writes about regular people whose realities suddenly slip off the tracks. He writes about the anxiety of putting on a sweater and getting stuck inside it, or the strange phenomenon of vomiting up tiny, living bunnies. Cortázar takes the mundane and makes it terrifying, magical, or deeply weird. It’s highly addictive to read over a concentrated period of time.

**3. The element of "Play"**
Cortázar is fundamentally playful. He loved jazz, and his writing mimics musical improvisation. He breaks the rules of grammar, perspective, and narrative structure, but never in a way that feels like homework. He treats the reader as a co-conspirator. 

**4. Why not Borges this month?**
Borges should not be binged. His stories are so dense with philosophical concepts that reading a whole book of them in one month can actually be exhausting. Borges is best treated as a lifelong companion—a writer you keep on your nightstand and read one story from every few weeks when you are in a contemplative mood. Cortázar, however, is a writer you can absolutely devour in a month-long fever dream.

**Where to start:**
Do not start with his famous, massive, rule-breaking novel *Hopscotch* (*Rayuela*). 
Start with his short stories. Grab the collection **"Blow-Up and Other Stories"** (which compiles stories from *Bestiary*, *End of the Game*, and *Secret Weapons*). 

Make sure to read:
*   *House Taken Over* (Casa Tomada)
*   *Letter to a Young Lady in Paris* (Carta a una señorita en París)
*   *The Night Face Up* (La noche boca arriba)
*   *Axolotl*

Pour yourself a glass of wine or a coffee, put on some Thelonious Monk or Charlie Parker in the background, and spend this month with Cortázar. You can visit Borges's infinite library next month.
