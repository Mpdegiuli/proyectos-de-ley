Read **Borges**.

Here's why: Borges is the better entry point and requires less commitment. His stories are short—often just 10-15 pages—so you can finish one in a sitting and genuinely *finish* something. With Cortázar, especially *Hopscotch*, you're signing up for a marathon that demands patience and re-reading.

Borges also hits harder on first contact. A story like "The Library of Babel" or "The Garden of Forking Paths" will grab you immediately with a brilliant premise, then unsettle you with its implications. You don't need to acclimate to his style.

Save Cortázar for when you have:
- More time to commit
- Energy for playfulness and digression (which is kind of the point)
- A willingness to read non-linearly if you choose

**That said**: if you're feeling restless and experimental rather than wanting something perfectly crafted, flip this advice. Cortázar rewards the right mood.

What kind of reading are you in the mood for—something dense and haunting, or something that plays with form?
