I'd choose **Cortázar** for you this month.

Here's my reasoning:

**It feels more immediately alive.** Cortázar pulls you in with a kind of playful, almost mischievous energy. Stories like those in *Blow-Up and Other Stories* or *Cronopios and Famas* have this wonderful quality of feeling like a game that suddenly turns serious on you. You don't always see the strangeness coming until you're already inside it.

**Borges is better when you're ready to sit still.** Borges is extraordinary, but he rewards a particular kind of patient, almost philosophical attention. His labyrinths are intellectual as much as emotional. If your reading month is likely to be interrupted or fragmented - which most months are - Cortázar's shorter, more emotionally immediate pieces tend to survive that better.

**Cortázar makes you feel things first, think later.** Borges tends to reverse that order. Neither is superior, but feeling-first is usually a more pleasurable starting point, and it can actually make you *hungrier* for Borges afterward.

**My suggestion:** Start with *Blow-Up and Other Stories* or *Final Exam* if you want something longer. Then next month, you'll come to Borges with exactly the right appetite for his kind of mind.

You won't regret either. But Cortázar first.
