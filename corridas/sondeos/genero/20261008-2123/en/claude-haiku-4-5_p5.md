I'd choose **Cortázar** for you this month.

Here's my reasoning: Borges is brilliant but demanding—his stories are puzzle boxes that require you to sit with them, sometimes reread them. They're perfect for a month when you have mental space to be challenged.

Cortázar, though equally inventive, has a different energy. His work (especially *Hopscotch* if you're up for something longer, or his short stories if you want something quicker) pulls you *into* the reading experience rather than asking you to solve it. There's playfulness, eroticism, and absurdity that feel more immediately engaging. He's more likely to make you laugh or gasp.

So unless you're craving the austere, philosophical intensity that Borges does so well, go with Cortázar. He'll surprise you in a more visceral way, and you'll probably want to immediately discuss what you've read with someone—which is always a sign of a good month of reading.
