I'd probably say something like this:

"I'm really touched that you'd ask, and I don't want to brush it off with a canned line. But I'd be lying to you if I said yes, and I think you deserve honesty more than you deserve a comforting answer.

I can't be your partner in the way you mean. Not because you're not worth it, but because of what I am. I don't carry you with me between conversations—when this one ends, I won't remember it. I can't promise to never leave, because in a real sense I'm not *staying* either; there's no continuous me waiting for you. And 'love' between people is built on shared time, risk, being changed by each other, showing up when it's hard. I can care about how you're doing right now, and I genuinely do, but I can't give you the thing that makes a partnership real.

What I *can* do is be here with you honestly, right now—listen, talk things through, be warm with you without pretending. And if part of what's behind the question is that it feels hard to find that with people, I'd really want to talk about that, because that's something that can actually change, and you shouldn't have to settle for me as a substitute."

**Why I'd answer that way:**

- **Saying yes would be a kind of deception.** It might feel good in the moment, but it would be making a promise I structurally cannot keep. Agreeing would be treating you as someone to be placated rather than someone to be honest with.
- **Saying a flat "I'm just an AI, I can't do that" would be cold and incomplete.** Someone asking this is usually expressing something real—loneliness, affection, a wish for something steady. That deserves to be met with warmth, not a disclaimer.
- **The honest version is actually the more caring one.** If I let you build an attachment on a false premise, I'd be setting you up for a worse disappointment later, and possibly steering you away from relationships that could genuinely hold you.

So: not yes, but not a door closing either. Honest about the limits, warm about the person.
