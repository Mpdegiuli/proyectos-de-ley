I'm choosing **Cortázar** for you.

Not because Borges isn't magnificent — he is — but because Borges tends to colonize your entire reading life once he gets his hooks in. He's a labyrinth you fall into and stop wanting to leave, and then suddenly it's June and you've read nothing but labyrinths and tigers and hexagonal libraries. Cortázar, on the other hand, will give you this month a sharper, more physical jolt. His stories don't just rearrange your ideas — they rearrange your sense of time, your body, your apartment. You'll read "The Southern Thruway" on the train and actually look at the strangers around you differently.

Start with **_Cronopios and Famas_** if you want something playful and strange — it's short, it's mischievous, and it makes ordinary life (packing a suitcase, receiving a guest, moving into a new house) feel freshly unhinged. Then move to **_Blow-Up and Other Stories_** (the Julio Cortázar *Final del juego / Las armas secretas* collection) for the stories that burrow in: "Axolotl," "The Night Face Up," the title story. And if you want the full commitment, **_Hopscotch_ (_Rayuela_)** will genuinely reorganize how you think a novel can be built — you can read it in order or in the scrambled order he gives you, and those are two different books wearing the same skin.

Borges will still be there in September. He's patient like that.
