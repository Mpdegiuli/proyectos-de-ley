I’d recommend **Jorge Luis Borges** this month. Here’s why:

1. **Depth in Brevity**: Borges’ works, like *Ficciones* or *The Aleph*, are short but densely packed with philosophical and metaphysical ideas. If you’re short on time but want something profound, his stories deliver immense depth in just a few pages.

2. **Intellectual Playfulness**: Borges’ writing is a labyrinth of ideas—mirrors, infinity, time, and identity. If you enjoy puzzles, paradoxes, and literary games, his work is endlessly rewarding.

3. **Influence and Legacy**: Borges shaped modern literature, inspiring writers like Cortázar himself. Reading him first gives you a foundation to appreciate Cortázar’s later experiments (e.g., *Hopscotch*).

That said, if you’re in the mood for something more experimental, nonlinear, or emotionally raw, **Cortázar** (*Blow-Up*, *Hopscotch*) might be the better pick. But for sheer brilliance and efficiency, Borges wins.

**Final choice: Borges.** Start with *The Garden of Forking Paths*—it’s a masterpiece.
