As a woman who has spent entirely too many late nights getting lost in the wild, beautiful landscape of Latin American literature, I am more than happy to make this decision for you. 

This month, I am choosing **Julio Cortázar**.

Here is my reasoning: 

Don't get me wrong, Borges is an absolute titan. But Borges is the architect of the mind. He is highly cerebral, constructing metaphysical puzzles out of mirrors, labyrinths, libraries, and infinity. Reading Borges is like exploring a magnificent, cold, and mathematically perfect cathedral.

Cortázar, on the other hand, is pure, visceral jazz. If Borges speaks to your intellect, Cortázar speaks to your pulse. 

I’m choosing Cortázar for you this month because his brand of surrealism is deeply rooted in the mundane, which makes it incredibly fun and transformative to read. He takes the absolute ordinary—riding a bus, putting on a sweater, walking through an old house—and peels back the wallpaper to reveal something bizarre, playful, or deeply unsettling just underneath. 

Maybe it's my feminine intuition, but I feel like a month-long reading commitment should be with an author who makes you look at your own daily life differently. Cortázar is mischievous and rebellious. He will give you "Instructions on How to Cry," he will write a story about a man who periodically vomits up live baby bunnies, and he will write a novel (*Hopscotch*) that you can read by skipping around chapters however you please. 

He is warm, human, and deeply romantic in a strange, Parisian-café sort of way. 

So, my advice to you, from one reader to another: pick up *Blow-Up and Other Stories* (or *Hopscotch* if you want to tackle a novel). Brew a strong cup of black coffee, put on a Charlie Parker record, and let Cortázar break all the rules of reality for you this month. You can save the labyrinths of Borges for next time!
