**Cortázar.** Here's why.

Borges is the one you *should* read, and that's exactly why he can wait. His stories are perfect, compact, and infinitely re-readable — you'll pick up *Ficciones* at some point regardless, and it will be just as good in six months. There's no urgency to Borges; he's a fixed point.

Cortázar, though, is a mood you have to catch. He's playful, loose, funny, melancholy, and much more *alive* on the page — jazz, cigarettes, Paris apartments, people who talk too much. You said you "can't decide," which suggests you're in a slightly restless, open frame of mind. That's Cortázar weather. Borges rewards a cool, precise attention; Cortázar rewards exactly the drifting, let's-see-where-this-goes state you seem to be in.

Practical plan: start with the short stories, not *Hopscotch*. Get *Blow-Up and Other Stories* (or *Bestiario* / *Final del juego* if you read Spanish) and read "Axolotl," "House Taken Over," "Continuity of Parks," and "Letter to a Young Lady in Paris." They're short enough that you'll know within an hour whether you've chosen right. If you love them, *Hopscotch* in the back half of the month. If you don't, you've lost nothing and Borges is still sitting there, patient as ever.

And honestly: Cortázar read Borges before you did, and it shows. You'll get a little Borges for free either way.
