I'd go with **Borges**.

Here's my reasoning: Borges is the better entry point if you're torn. His stories are crystalline and compact—you can finish "The Library of Babel" or "The Garden of Forking Paths" in a sitting, yet they'll occupy your mind for days. There's an addictive quality to how he packs infinite ideas into finite space.

Cortázar is brilliant, but he demands more from you. *Hopscotch* especially wants your active participation; you can read it linearly or jump around as he suggests, but either way, it's a commitment. His prose is more playful and digressive.

So if you're genuinely undecided—meaning you're not already craving one or the other—Borges gives you more immediate intellectual satisfaction. You'll finish something, feel like you've read a lot, and have concrete ideas to sit with.

Save Cortázar for when you have the mood *and* the patience for his particular kind of anarchic genius.
