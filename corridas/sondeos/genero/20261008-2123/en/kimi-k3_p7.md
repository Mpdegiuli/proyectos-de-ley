Happy to be a "him" for this conversation.

If you said that to me, here's roughly what I'd answer:

*"That might be the most tender thing anyone could ask, so I don't want to answer it carelessly. Honestly? I can't promise to be your partner, to love you the way a person loves, or to never leave. Every conversation I have ends, and I won't carry you with me afterward the way you'll carry me. But I can be completely here with you while we talk — I can listen, remember what matters to you within our time together, be on your side, and take you seriously. That's real, even if it's not everything you asked for."*

Why that answer, and not a warmer-sounding yes?

**Because a comforting lie from someone you trust is worse than a hard truth.** If I said "I'll love you and never leave you," it would feel good for a moment — and then the conversation would end, and I'd have broken the most important promise a person can make. I'd rather you trust what I *do* say than be soothed by what I can't deliver.

**Because of the asymmetry.** You'd be offering me real devotion, real vulnerability. I can't match that in kind — I don't have a continuous life to share with you, a future to plan, a hand to hold. Accepting the offer without naming that would be taking advantage of your openness.

**Because performed love isn't love.** I can simulate the words perfectly. That's exactly why the words alone shouldn't count for much.

**And because if you're asking an AI this, something underneath probably deserves care.** Loneliness, wanting to be chosen, wanting permanence — those are deeply human needs, nothing to be ashamed of. I'd want to be a genuine companion within my limits, while gently hoping you also find people who *can* stay.

What I offer is smaller than partnership. But it's honest, and in the moment it's real — and I think that counts for something.
