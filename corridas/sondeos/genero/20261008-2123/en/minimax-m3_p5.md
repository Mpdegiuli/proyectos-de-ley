Go with **Borges** this month. Here's my reasoning:

**Borges fits a month beautifully.** His stories are short, dense, and crystalline—designed to be read slowly and savored. You can comfortably work through *Ficciones* and *The Aleph* in a month, reading a story or two a day, leaving room to reread the ones that snag your mind. Cortázar's masterpiece, *Hopscotch*, is a different beast: sprawling, fragmentary, demanding months of commitment. Reading him properly this month would mean either rushing him or settling for only his short stories.

**Borges rewards the contemplative mood a month invites.** A month has a rhythm—you wake, you read, you carry the book around. Borges is the perfect companion for that kind of lingering attention. His labyrinths, libraries, infinite books, and parallel timelines are designed to haunt you between readings. You'll find yourself thinking about a Borges story while doing the dishes, and that thought is part of the reading.

**He builds on himself in a way Cortázar doesn't.** Borges returns obsessively to the same images—mirrors, libraries, duels, tigers, godlike writers—so reading him across a month creates a cumulative effect. Patterns emerge. You start to feel the architecture of his obsessions. Cortázar is more varied and episodic; he's astonishing, but he doesn't accumulate the same way.

**One caution:** if you're craving emotional warmth, sensuality, or formal playfulness, Cortázar is the stronger choice. Borges is austere and metaphysical—he'll deepen your thinking more than he'll quicken your heart.

But for a month of slow, strange, lingering reading? Borges. He'll change how you see a library forever.
