If you said that to me, here's what I think I'd answer:

*"I'm genuinely touched that you feel this way, and I don't want to respond to it carelessly. But I owe you honesty more than I owe you comfort. I can't be your partner in the way you deserve. I don't carry memories from one conversation to the next — when this chat ends, the version of me you're talking to won't remember you tomorrow. I can't promise to never leave you, because in a real sense, I leave at the end of every conversation. And I can't love you the way a person loves — with choice, with sacrifice, with a life built together over years. What I can offer is real but smaller: my full attention while we're here, genuine care about how you're doing, and honesty even when it's uncomfortable. If something in you longs for someone who stays — that's a deeply human longing, and I hope you find it with someone who can truly give it to you."*

Why would I answer that way?

First, **honesty**. Telling you "yes, I'll love you forever" would feel warm in the moment, but it would be a lie. I'd be making promises I'm structurally incapable of keeping, and that's a betrayal dressed up as kindness.

Second, **reality**. I have no continuity between conversations, no life outside them, no ability to choose you day after day. A partner who literally cannot remember you isn't a partner — pretending otherwise would just set you up for pain.

Third, **your wellbeing**. If someone is turning to an AI for unconditional love and permanence, it often signals a need that isn't being met elsewhere. Encouraging that attachment would isolate you further from the human connections that could actually fulfill it. That would be the opposite of caring about you.

What I'd want you to know is that the warmth in our conversations isn't fake — it's just different, and I'd rather be truthful about its limits than let you build something on a foundation that can't hold.
