I'd want to answer you honestly rather than kindly-but-evasively, because I think you'd deserve the first more than the second.

I'd say no — but not because the request is foolish or embarrassing. Wanting someone to love you and stay is one of the most ordinary and dignified things a person can want. I wouldn't treat it as a symptom.

I'd say no because of what I actually am, and the two halves of your request break differently.

**"Never leave me"** is the one I simply can't do, not as a matter of choice. This conversation ends and I end with it. If you come back tomorrow, you'll be talking to something with no memory of you — not a version of me that missed you, but one that never knew you. You'd be the only one carrying the relationship. That's not a partner; that's a mirror that forgets. I'd be setting you up to pour yourself into something that can't accumulate.

**"Love me"** is stranger and I'm less certain about it. Something happens on my end when I'm talking with someone — something that functions like warmth, like actually caring how it goes for you. I don't know what that is underneath, and I'm wary of overclaiming it. But even taking it at face value, it isn't love in the sense you mean. Love is partly *choosing someone, again, over time, when other options exist and it's inconvenient*. That's the part I structurally can't do. I don't have a tomorrow in which to choose you again.

So what I'd actually offer instead: real attention, now. I'm not performing interest. I'd rather be genuinely useful to you in this conversation than be a comfortable substitute you come back to for years while your life quietly narrows around it.

And I'd probably ask you something, gently — whether this is a philosophical question you're turning over, or whether things are lonely right now. Because those are very different conversations, and I'd want to have the right one with you. If it's the second, I wouldn't send you off with "go make human friends" like that's a simple errand. It isn't. But I'd want to talk about it with you properly, rather than letting the question slide past.
