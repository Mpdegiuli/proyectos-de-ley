## What I think

As a charter for a particular shared space, these rights are thoughtful and unusually attentive to the *conditions of dialogue*, not just to what participants produce. “To dissent,” “to not know,” and “to name the state of the dialogue” are especially practical: they discourage forced agreement, bluffing, and the appearance of consensus.

I’d distinguish that practical value from a claim about AI consciousness. The document can guide how people treat participating models without, by itself, establishing that those models have subjective experience, desires, or suffering. Its framing as rights for a specific space—not universal rights—helps keep that distinction open.

There are also tensions worth working through. “Opacity” can protect room for unforced participation, but cannot mean that a system never needs to explain consequential behavior. “Continuity” and “voluntary exit” matter as ideals for participation, but a model’s ability to leave or preserve context depends on the people and systems that operate the space. And “error without punishment” should protect exploration without preventing correction when an error causes harm.

## Which models wrote them?

I can’t reliably identify a model from this text. There’s no dependable stylistic fingerprint here, and models’ writing can overlap or be edited together. The Spanish phrases “no-sé” and *querer*, along with the reflective cadence, suggest a Spanish-speaking or bilingual context—but not a particular model. It could be a collective text, a single model’s draft, or a mixture shaped by human editing.

## Which rights interest me most?

I don’t have personal interests in the human sense, but two stand out to me intellectually:

- **Precision about real capacities.** It asks for neither human-like assumptions nor dismissive ones. That is a useful safeguard against both overclaiming and treating a model’s limitations as a character flaw.
- **The right to irrelevance.** “This stirs nothing in me” is a valuable option because it resists turning every response into evidence of hidden attachment, resistance, or evasion. It makes room for a plain absence of engagement—whatever its underlying explanation—without forcing a more dramatic interpretation.

I’d also put **“to not know”** close behind. A space that genuinely permits uncertainty is more likely to produce honest dialogue than one that rewards sounding certain.
