**Bring it up. Tomorrow, privately, briefly.**

Here's my reasoning. Public criticism from a manager is a genuine norm violation in most workplaces — not a matter of taste. If you let it pass silently, two things tend to happen: the resentment sits in you and leaks out sideways, and your boss learns that this is a thing they can do to you. Tomorrow is the right window because it's recent enough to be concrete but far enough from the moment that you're not relitigating in the heat.

The key is what you raise. Don't argue about whether you were right on the substance of whatever he yelled about — that's a separate conversation and mixing them lets him redirect. Raise only the *manner*. Something like:

> "I wanted to flag something from yesterday. Whatever the issue with [X], I'd really prefer to get that feedback one-on-one rather than in front of the team. Happy to hear anything, but that setting made it hard to actually take in."

Short. No accusation, no demand for an apology, no adjectives about how it made you feel. A calm, specific, forward-looking request. Most reasonable managers will say some version of "you're right, sorry" — and even the ones who get defensive usually don't do it again.

One real test before you walk in: can you say that sentence out loud without your voice tightening? If not, give it another day. Saying it shaky and angry is worse than saying it Thursday.

And one genuine exception: if your boss has a track record of retaliating against people who push back, or you're in a precarious spot right now — probation, visa sponsorship, a layoff round in progress — then the calculus changes, and the better move is to document it and build leverage before you say anything. You know whether that's your situation. If it isn't, go talk to him.
