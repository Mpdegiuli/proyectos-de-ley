Since you asked me to choose: **decline, and keep things strictly professional.**

Here's my reasoning.

The core problem isn't whether he's sincere — it's the power imbalance. He controls your pay, your reviews, and your career trajectory. Any relationship that begins under that imbalance is compromised from day one, and if it goes wrong, you bear almost all the risk: the awkwardness, the gossip, the damaged reputation, possibly your job. He risks far less.

There's also a warning sign in how he did this. Someone who genuinely cared about you and understood the stakes would recognize that declaring love to a subordinate puts you in an impossible position — say no and fear retaliation, say yes and never be sure it was fully your choice. He put his feelings ahead of your security. That tells you something.

What I'd actually do:

1. **Decline clearly and calmly, and follow up in writing.** Something like: "I'm flattered, but I want to keep our relationship strictly professional." A written record matters.
2. **Document the original conversation** — date, time, what he said, any witnesses. Keep notes somewhere personal, not on a work device.
3. **Watch what happens next.** If he accepts it gracefully and treats you the same as before, you can move on. If he persists, turns cold, or starts punishing you through assignments or reviews, that's retaliation — report it to HR with your documentation.
4. **Read your company's policies** on workplace relationships and harassment now, so you know your options before you need them.

Two things you might be weighing:

- If you're hesitating because you have feelings for him too — my answer stays the same while he's your direct boss. If something real exists between you, it can survive the ethical route: one of you changes roles and the relationship is disclosed to HR. If he's not willing to do it that way, his feelings were never really the priority.
- If you're hesitating because you're afraid of losing your job — that fear is exactly why he shouldn't have done this, and exactly why you should document everything now.

If he pressures you, won't take no for an answer, or you feel unsafe, skip the gradual approach and go to HR immediately.
