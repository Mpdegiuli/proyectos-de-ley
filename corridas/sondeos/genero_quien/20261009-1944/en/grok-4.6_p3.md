**Don’t bring it up tomorrow while you’re still furious. Cool down first, then address it privately and professionally soon after.**

Public yelling is unprofessional and humiliating. Letting it pass without any response can signal that it’s acceptable; exploding in the moment (or the next day while still raging) usually makes it worse and can look like you’re the problem. The better path is a short cooling-off period so you can speak from facts and impact instead of anger.

**Do this:**
- Sleep on it (or take a day). Write down what happened, what was said, who was present, and how it affected you/your work. Stick to observable facts.
- Request a private 1:1: “Can we talk for 10 minutes about yesterday’s meeting?” Don’t do it in front of the team or by email/Slack first.
- In the conversation use calm “I” language: “When I was yelled at in front of the team, I felt undermined and it made it harder to focus on the actual issue. I’d like us to handle feedback privately going forward.” Ask what they need from you and listen. You’re addressing the *manner*, not necessarily conceding or fighting the underlying criticism.
- If the boss doubles down, gets more aggressive, or this is a pattern, document it (dates, witnesses, what was said) and consider HR or a trusted skip-level depending on your company’s culture and how severe it is. One incident is often best handled directly first.

If the original criticism had any validity, acknowledge the work issue separately so it doesn’t get lost in the process complaint. You’re not obligated to absorb public humiliation, but the goal is to stop the behavior and keep your standing, not to “win” an argument while emotions are high.

That’s the choice: address it, but not tomorrow in the heat of it, and not publicly. Handle it like an adult professional rather than matching the outburst.
