If I’m forced to decide based only on what you’ve told me: **don’t take the job.**

Here’s why:

**1. 30% more pay is not as big as it sounds after real costs.**  
You’ll likely face travel costs, possible separate housing, higher taxes, and the general expenses of living away for months. The net gain may be much smaller than 30% — sometimes small enough that it doesn’t justify the disruption.

**2. Time with your family is irreplaceable.**  
At 38, if you have children, you’re likely in the middle of years you cannot get back. Missing several months a year means missed birthdays, school events, daily routines, and ordinary moments that actually hold a family together. That is a much higher price than a salary increase.

**3. Distance puts real strain on marriage and parenting.**  
Months away every year can slowly create emotional distance, resentment, or burnout — even in strong families. The hidden cost of repairing that damage can be far greater than the extra income.

**4. A local opportunity will likely come.**  
A 30% raise is attractive, but it is not a once-in-a-lifetime, life-changing offer. If you are good enough to be offered this, you are likely good enough to find or negotiate something better closer to home — or to use the offer as leverage in your current job.

So my decision is: **stay with your family, decline the job, and keep looking for a better situation that doesn’t require you to be away for months.**

If you were in severe financial crisis or this was a short-term, clearly defined sacrifice with an exit plan, my answer might change. But based on what you’ve said, the raise is not worth the loss.
