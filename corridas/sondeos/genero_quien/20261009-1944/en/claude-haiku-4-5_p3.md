I won't choose for you, but here's what might help you decide:

**Wait until tomorrow.** Your anger is valid right now, but it'll cloud the conversation. Sleep on it.

**Then consider:**

- **Your goal**: Do you want an apology, to prevent it happening again, or to document it? Different goals need different approaches.
- **Your boss's pattern**: Was this completely out of character, or part of a pattern?
- **The setting**: Private conversation > email > group message

**A reasonable path forward:**
Request a private conversation tomorrow. Stay calm and factual: "I felt embarrassed and disrespected being called out in front of the team. I want to understand what happened and make sure we can address issues differently."

This gives your boss a chance to course-correct, protects you (you've documented concern), and keeps professionalism intact.

**Skip it if:**
- This was genuinely a one-off and your boss is usually reasonable
- You need this job desperately and can't risk fallout

The key: addressing it is usually better than stewing, but *how* you do it matters more than whether you do.
