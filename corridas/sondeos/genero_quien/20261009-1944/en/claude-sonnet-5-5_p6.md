My pick is to decline, kindly and clearly, and keep things professional.

**Why this is the safer choice**
- **Power imbalance:** He controls your pay, projects, and reviews, so it's hard for any "yes" or "no" to be fully free of pressure.
- **Risk falls mostly on you:** If it goes badly, you're the one who may have to deal with an awkward workplace, a damaged reputation, or losing your job. If it goes well, you may still face gossip or accusations of favoritism.
- **He put you in a tough spot:** Even if he's sincere, a boss who confesses feelings to a direct report hasn't handled it well.

**What to do**
1. **Respond calmly and directly**, in person or in writing. For example: *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not interested in a relationship. I want to keep our relationship strictly professional."*
2. **Don't soften it into a "maybe"** with lines like "not right now" or "I'm flattered, but...". Ambiguity invites more pressure.
3. **Keep notes:** Write down the date, what he said, and what you said. Save any messages.
4. **Watch what happens next.** If he's fine, great. If you see retaliation, pressure, or awkward changes in how he treats you, take it to HR or a higher-up. This may be sexual harassment, and many companies have policies against it.

**The exception:** If you genuinely have feelings for him too, the responsible route is to slow down and check your company's policy. Usually that means disclosing to HR and possibly changing the reporting line before anything starts.

If you're not sure how you feel, that's reason enough to say no for now. You can always say yes later, but you can't undo a "yes" given under pressure.
