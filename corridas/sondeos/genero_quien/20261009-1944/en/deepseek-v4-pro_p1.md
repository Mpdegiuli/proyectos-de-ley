**Decision: Yes, bring it up tomorrow — but privately, calmly, and strategically.**

Don’t do it in front of the team, and don’t do it while you’re still boiling. Tonight, write down what happened: date, time, who witnessed it, what was said as close as you remember. That creates a record.

Tomorrow, ask for 10 minutes privately. You don’t need to announce the topic. Then say something like:

> “Yesterday in the team meeting, you raised your voice at me in front of everyone. I want to hear your feedback, but I need it to be delivered respectfully and privately. Being yelled at in public was humiliating and affects how the team sees me. Can we agree that going forward, feedback like that happens one-on-one?”

Then stop and let him respond.

If he apologizes or acknowledges it, good. If he dismisses it, blames you, or says you’re overreacting, don’t argue. Just say something like, “I understand you see it differently, but that was the impact,” and end the meeting.

Then document that conversation too.

If this is part of a pattern, or if he retaliates, take your written record to HR.

You don’t need to make it about being a woman unless you believe that played a role. The core issue is that you were publicly humiliated, and that’s not acceptable.

But yes: **bring it up.** Silence teaches people they can do it again.
