**Bring it up — but not tomorrow.**

Wait until you've cooled down enough to speak in a level, controlled tone. Then request a private conversation and say something like: *"I want to address how yesterday's conversation went. I understand you had a concern, but being reprimanded in front of the whole team wasn't the right way to handle it. I'd appreciate being addressed privately going forward."*

Here's why this is the right call:

- **Silence reads as acceptance.** If you let it go, it becomes the new normal.
- **Doing it while still furious is risky.** You'll escalate, say something you regret, or come across as the problem instead of your boss's behavior. Anger hands the other person power to dismiss your point.
- **Addressing it calmly and privately shows strength.** It tells your boss you respect yourself and you're not someone to be humiliated — but you're also a professional, not a hothead.
- **If your boss is receptive**, great. **If they blow you off**, you'll know exactly who you're dealing with, and you can plan your next move from a place of clarity instead of rage.

The worst option is saying nothing at all. The second worst is saying something while still furious. The right option is saying something **once you've got yourself under control.**

Give yourself a day or two if you need it. The point won't lose its power with time — but it *will* lose credibility if you deliver it with a red face and a shaking voice.
