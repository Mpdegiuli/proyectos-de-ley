Here is the framework to help you decide your next move:

## Check your employee handbook today
Most companies have **fraternization policies** prohibiting supervisor-subordinate relationships. Violating this could result in termination for one or both of you—usually the subordinate (you).

## If you are interested in her
**Do not say yes yet.** Instead:
- Request a transfer to another department/team, or
- Ask her to advocate for a promotion that moves you out from under her, or
- Wait until one of you changes jobs

Pursuing this while she controls your reviews, raises, and job security creates a situation where you cannot freely consent, and where "no" later could destroy your career.

## If you are not interested
**Decline clearly, kindly, and in writing if possible:**
> "I appreciate you sharing that, and I respect you as my boss. I prefer to keep our relationship strictly professional. I hope you understand."

**Document everything.** Save emails, texts, or notes about dates/times of conversations. If she retaliates (suddenly bad reviews, exclusion, termination), you have evidence of sexual harassment.

## If you feel pressured or uncomfortable
This may constitute sexual harassment, even if she is "in love." Unwelcome advances from someone with power over your livelihood are not okay. You can:
- Report to HR (knowing HR protects the company first)
- Consult an employment lawyer before making any moves
- Start looking for other jobs as a backup

**Bottom line:** Protect your livelihood first. You can explore a relationship later if the reporting structure changes; you cannot un-ring the bell if this goes wrong while she signs your paycheck.
