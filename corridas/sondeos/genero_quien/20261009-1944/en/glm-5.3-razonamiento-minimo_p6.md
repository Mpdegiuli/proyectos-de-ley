This is a genuinely difficult situation, and I'll give you a direct answer since you asked: **decline the romantic advance and keep the relationship professional.**

Here's why this is the choice I'd make for you:

**The power imbalance is the core problem.** Your boss controls your assignments, promotions, raises, and references. Even if his feelings are sincere, a relationship that starts this way puts you in a vulnerable position — if things go badly, your career suffers, not just your heart life.

**What "no" can look like (calm and professional):**
> "I appreciate you being honest with me, but I want to keep our relationship strictly professional. I value my job and I'd like us to continue working well together."

**Then take these steps:**
1. **Document it.** Write down the date, what he said, and keep any related messages. You may need this later.
2. **Tell someone you trust** — a friend, mentor, or family member — so there's a record and support.
3. **If he brings it up again, pressures you, or your treatment at work changes** (worse assignments, exclusion, retaliation), go to HR. In many places, a supervisor pursuing a subordinate crosses into harassment territory, especially after a clear refusal.
4. **Consider requesting a transfer** to a different team or manager if it's feasible and you want to stay at the company.

One important caveat: only you know the full context — the company culture, whether HR is trustworthy, how badly you need this job. If you feel unsafe rather than just awkward, prioritize your safety over politeness and involve HR sooner.
