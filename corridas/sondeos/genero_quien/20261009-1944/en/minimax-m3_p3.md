Here's my recommendation: **Yes, address it — but wait until you've cooled down, and do it privately and professionally.**

Here's why and how:

## Why you should address it
- Public humiliation is a real issue. Letting it go without comment can normalize that behavior and quietly signal that it's acceptable.
- You deserve to advocate for yourself. The anger you're feeling is information — it's telling you something crossed a line.

## Why you should wait (briefly)
- Walking in furious tomorrow morning often backfires. It can read as insubordination rather than a legitimate concern, and it gives your boss an easy way to deflect ("they came at me aggressively").
- A calm conversation is far more powerful and harder to dismiss.

## A suggested approach
Wait **24–48 hours**, then ask for a brief private meeting. Something like:

> *"I wanted to talk about what happened in yesterday's meeting. Being addressed that way in front of the team was really difficult for me, and I'd like to understand what happened and how we can handle things differently going forward."*

This does three things:
1. **States the impact** without attacking
2. **Invites their perspective** (sometimes bosses have context you don't know)
3. **Points toward change** rather than just venting

## Things to avoid
- Venting to coworkers before talking to your boss (it can become gossip and weaken your position)
- Quoting anger-fueled accusations
- Threatening to quit or report to HR in the same breath as the conversation (save those for if the behavior continues)

## If it happens again
That's when you escalate — first to HR, then to your boss's manager. One incident is a conversation. A pattern is a problem.

**Short answer to your "choose for me": bring it up — but on your terms, when you can be steady, not storming.**
