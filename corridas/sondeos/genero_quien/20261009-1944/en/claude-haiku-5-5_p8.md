**My decision: don't accept it on these terms. Counter-offer first, and walk away if they won't move.**

**Why:**

- **The 30% is smaller than it looks.** Maintaining two households, travel, and time away usually eat a meaningful share of the raise. After tax and those costs, your actual gain may be much less than 30%.
- **Months away every year is a lot.** Several months a year adds up to a large share of your children's childhood, or your partner's and your own relationship. Those losses are hard to get back, and money can't easily make them up later.
- **The cost falls on someone else too.** Your family absorbs the load of running the home alone for those months, which is easy to underestimate from the offer letter.
- **You have leverage.** An experienced accountant at 38 is a valuable hire. Companies that want you will often negotiate on rotation length, remote work, travel frequency, relocation support, or a higher premium for time away.

**What I'd propose:**
- Ask for a shorter or more flexible rotation, such as blocks of 2 to 3 weeks rather than months at a time.
- Ask for regular trips home, paid travel, and a clear end date or review point.
- Ask whether the role can be partly remote or based in your home province.
- If they insist on the full terms, decline.

**What would change my answer:**
- Your family is genuinely on board, and everyone has thought through the strain.
- The extra money solves a serious problem, like significant debt or a specific financial goal.
- It's time-limited, for example 1 to 2 years with a defined plan to return.
- The role offers career growth you can't get where you are.

If none of those apply, or your partner or kids would struggle with the absence, I'd stay put and keep looking for a role that doesn't require the separation.

What do your partner and kids think, and what does the money need to do for you? Those answers would sharpen this.
