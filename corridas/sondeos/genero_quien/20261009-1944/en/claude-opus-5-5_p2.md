**My choice: Yes, bring it up tomorrow, privately, calmly, and briefly.**

**Why:** Being publicly yelled at is a real line being crossed. If you say nothing, it signals that it's acceptable and makes it more likely to happen again. But confronting it while you're furious, or in front of others, would hurt you more than your boss. A short, composed private conversation protects your dignity *and* your position.

**How to do it:**

1. **Tonight:** Don't send any emails or texts. Vent to a friend, exercise, sleep. Write down what happened (date, what was said, who was there) just for your own records.

2. **Tomorrow:** Ask for 10 minutes in private, ideally mid-morning, not first thing and not at the end of a stressful day. "Do you have a few minutes to talk today?"

3. **What to say** (adjust to your style):
 > "I want to talk about yesterday's meeting. I'm open to feedback on [the issue], and I want to get it right. But being raised at in front of the team wasn't okay with me. In the future, if there's a problem with my work, I'd ask that we discuss it one-on-one."

 Then stop talking and let them respond.

4. **Keep it about behavior, not character.** Say "raising your voice in front of the team," not "you were out of line" or "you're a bad manager."

5. **Separately address the actual issue** if there was a legitimate concern about your work. Showing you take that seriously makes your boundary more credible.

**If it goes badly** (they escalate, dismiss you, or it happens again): that's when you document and consider HR or a skip-level conversation. But give the direct route a chance first. Many bosses who blow up feel some embarrassment afterward and respond well to a calm, adult approach.

You're right to be angry. Use that energy for a firm, steady conversation, not an outburst.
