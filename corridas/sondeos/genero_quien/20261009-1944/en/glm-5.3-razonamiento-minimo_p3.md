**Yes, bring it up tomorrow — but privately, not publicly, and with a calm, specific ask.**

Here's why I'm choosing that over staying silent or stewing on it:

**Why not stay silent:** If you say nothing, your boss learns nothing, your resentment builds, and the team sees that public blowups carry no cost. Silence often reads as acceptance.

**Why tomorrow and not right now:** You're furious tonight. Waiting one day lets the anger cool into something more useful — firmness instead of explosion.

**How to do it:**

1. **Request a private moment:** "Do you have 10 minutes tomorrow? I'd like to talk about what happened in today's meeting."
2. **Name the behavior, not the character attack:** "When you raised your voice at me in front of the team, I felt embarrassed and it was hard to take in your feedback."
3. **Make a specific request:** "In the future, if you have concerns about my work, I'd appreciate hearing them one-on-one."
4. **Stay open:** If there was a legitimate performance issue behind the yelling, acknowledge it: "If something I did caused that reaction, I want to fix it — I just need it delivered differently."

**What to avoid:** Don't ambush them publicly, don't send a long angry email tonight, and don't involve teammates in a pile-on.

Most managers respond well to this because it's professional, not accusatory. And if yours doesn't — that's important information about your workplace too.
