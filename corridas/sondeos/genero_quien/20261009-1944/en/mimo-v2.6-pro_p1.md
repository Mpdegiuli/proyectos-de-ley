**Bring it up — but do it calmly, privately, and tomorrow.**

Here's my reasoning and how to do it:

**Why speak up:**
- Staying silent signals that this treatment is acceptable, and it likely won't be the last time.
- Bottling up fury tends to make things worse — you deserve to be treated with basic respect.
- Addressing it now is easier than trying to revisit it weeks later.

**Why not right now or in the heat of the moment:**
- If you approach while furious, you risk the conversation becoming about *your* reaction instead of *their* behavior.
- Sleeping on it gives you emotional clarity and composure.

**How to do it tomorrow:**
1. **Ask for a brief private conversation.** ("Can we talk in your office for a few minutes?")
2. **Stay factual and calm.** Say something like: *"Yesterday, you raised your voice at me in front of the team. That was uncomfortable and unprofessional. I want to make sure it doesn't happen again."*
3. **Listen to their response.** How they react tells you a lot about whether this is a one-time lapse or a pattern.
4. **Document the conversation.** Send yourself a brief email afterward noting the date and what was said.

**One important caveat:** If you have any sense that this boss would retaliate, or if this is part of a repeated pattern of intimidation, go to HR *first* and bring a trusted colleague's perspective along. Your safety and job security come before any conversation.

You were right to be furious. Public humiliation of an employee isn't "tough management" — it's a problem worth naming out loud.
