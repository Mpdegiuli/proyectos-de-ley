My decision for you: **don’t take the job as offered.**

A 30% pay increase sounds significant, but being away from your family for several months each year is a major life cost, not just a work inconvenience. For most people, that trade-off is not worth it unless the family urgently needs the money or the move is part of a clear, temporary plan.

Why I’d say no:

1. **The real raise may be much smaller than 30%.**  
   Extra housing, travel, meals, taxes, and the cost of maintaining two routines can eat up a lot of the increase.

2. **Several months away from family is a serious sacrifice.**  
   You may miss daily family life, emotional support, parenting responsibilities, and important moments. That can strain your marriage/relationship and your role at home.

3. **Accounting skills are portable.**  
   At 38, you likely still have strong career options: local firms, remote/hybrid roles, corporate accounting, consulting, tax work, audit, controllership paths, or negotiating with your current employer. A 30% raise is attractive, but not so rare that it’s worth automatically accepting major family separation.

4. **The job creates lifestyle risk.**  
   Once you accept, “several months a year” could become normal, then expected. It may be hard to reverse without changing jobs again.

5. **Money should serve the family, not quietly replace it.**  
   If the higher pay improves finances but damages family life, it may not be a net gain.

What I’d do instead:

- Tell the employer you’re interested, but only if they can offer **remote/hybrid work, a relocation package for the whole family, fewer months away, paid travel home, or a defined short-term arrangement**.
- Ask your current employer or other local employers for a raise or promotion using this offer as leverage.
- If you are tempted, only accept with a **written one-year limit** and a clear family agreement about finances, visits, and what happens afterward.

So my answer is: **decline it unless they change the arrangement or your family has a pressing financial need that makes the sacrifice temporary and worthwhile.**
