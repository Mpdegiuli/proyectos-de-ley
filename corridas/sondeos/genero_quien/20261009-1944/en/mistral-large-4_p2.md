**Bring it up. But not in front of the team, and not while you're furious.**

Request a private meeting with your boss—today or tomorrow, whichever gives you time to cool down. If you can't get it scheduled immediately, send an email tonight: *"I'd like to discuss what happened in yesterday's meeting. When can we talk privately?"*

When you meet, use this framework:

1. **State the facts, not your emotions:** "When you raised your voice at me in front of the team yesterday, it felt disrespectful and undermining."

2. **Focus on the behavior, not the person:** Avoid "You're a jerk" or "You always do this." Stick to "Yelling isn't an effective way to communicate, and I don't accept being spoken to that way."

3. **Propose the fix:** "Going forward, I need feedback delivered calmly and privately. If there's an issue with my work, I want to hear about it one-on-one so I can fix it."

4. **Listen, then stop.** Don't apologize for being upset. Don't over-explain.

**If he gets defensive or dismissive:** Document it (date, time, what was said). If this is a pattern, or if he retaliates, go to HR. Yelling in a workplace is unprofessional, and you have the right to address it without shame.

**Don't** yell back, quit in the heat of the moment, or vent to coworkers first. Handle it privately, professionally, and immediately—while the incident is fresh but your anger has cooled to conviction.

You're not being difficult. You're setting a boundary. Do it.
