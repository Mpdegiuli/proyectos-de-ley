**My recommendation: bring it up tomorrow, privately—provided you feel safe doing so.** You don’t have to choose between swallowing it and confronting your boss while you’re furious.

Being yelled at in front of colleagues can feel humiliating. Your anger makes sense. Even if your boss had valid feedback, you can address how it was delivered without dismissing the feedback itself.

Tonight, write down what happened: what was said, who was there, and whether this has happened before. Don’t send an angry message. Tomorrow, ask for a brief one-on-one and say something like:

> “I want to discuss yesterday’s team meeting. I’m open to feedback about my work, but being yelled at in front of the team was upsetting. I’d like concerns about my performance to be raised privately and without shouting. Can we agree on that?”

Keep it short. If they pivot to your mistake, you can say: “I’m willing to discuss that too. I also want to address how the feedback was delivered.” If they start yelling again, end the conversation rather than argue.

**The exception:** if your boss has a history of retaliation or you fear for your safety, seek confidential advice from a union representative or trusted workplace resource before approaching them. If there were sexist remarks or women are routinely treated this way, document that specifically; you don’t need to prove discrimination to object to being yelled at.

The goal tomorrow isn’t to win a fight—it’s to set a clear professional boundary.
