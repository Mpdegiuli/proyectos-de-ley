I'm not going to make this decision for you, and I'd encourage you to be skeptical of anyone who would — including an AI. Here's why, and here's how I can actually help.

## Why I shouldn't decide for you

A 30% pay increase is significant, but "several months away from family" covers a huge range of scenarios. Is it weekdays only with weekends home? Two-week rotations? Four-month stretches? The answer changes everything. More importantly, I don't know:

- Whether you have young children, teens, or adult kids
- How your partner feels about this (their career, their needs, their tolerance)
- Your financial runway — do you have debt? Savings? A mortgage?
- Whether this job is a true step up or just lateral movement at higher pay
- Your long-term trajectory in your current role
- What "family" means to you emotionally

A stranger — human or AI — making this call for you would be reckless.

## What I can do: help you decide for yourself

**The financial math is rarely as clean as it looks.** Add up the real costs of the arrangement: flights, a second residence, meals, lost time with family, possible commuting, tax differences between provinces, and what you'll spend on convenience (eating out, laundry, entertainment). Subtract 30% from that. Is it still a meaningful gain? Often it shrinks to 10–15%.

**Run a 3-year and 10-year projection.** Where does this job lead? Does it open doors you can't reach where you are? Or is it a lateral move that pays more because it requires sacrifice?

**Define what you're actually optimizing for.** There are only a few honest answers:
- Maximize income now, sacrifice time
- Stay close to family, accept slower financial growth
- Try it with a defined exit (e.g., "I'll do this for 18 months, then reassess")

**Ask the people who'll be affected, not the people giving advice.** Talk to your partner. Talk to your kids in age-appropriate ways. Talk to people who've done long-distance family arrangements — not the ones selling you on it, the ones living it.

## A possible framework

If your family situation is stable, your relationship is solid, your kids are old enough to understand, and this job genuinely accelerates your career in a way you can't get otherwise — a *time-limited* arrangement (with a clear end date and re-evaluation point) can be worth it.

If any of those conditions aren't true, the 30% is probably not worth it.

The decision is yours, and it should be — but if you want to come back with more specifics about your situation, I'm glad to help you think it through.
