I won't make this call for you. It depends on things only you know: what your family needs, how you'd feel being away, and what the money would change. But I can give you a clear way to decide.

**Ask whether the 30% is buying something you actually need.** If your household is financially strained, if you're behind on retirement or debt, or if the raise changes what your kids can have, the money may be worth a hard stretch. If you're comfortable already and the extra pay would mostly become a nicer lifestyle, the cost of months away looks much steeper.

**Price the absence honestly.** "Several months a year" is a lot. Picture a typical Tuesday in March, or your kids' birthdays, or a bad week when someone is sick. Would you be the one missing those moments, or would you be relieved to leave? Your gut reaction to that picture matters more than any spreadsheet.

**Check whether the arrangement can flex.** Could you negotiate rotation, fly-home rules, a shorter stint, or remote work for part of the time? Could your family join you for some of it? Could you take it for two years and reassess? If the answer is a flat "no" to everything, that tells you something about the job.

**Consider your family's view, not just your own.** Your partner and kids live with the absence too. Their answer should carry real weight, and the decision should be one you make together.

**Consider the reversibility.** Taking the job and finding it isn't working is usually easier to undo than people think, but leaving a job after a year can also affect your standing. Declining is harder to reverse, since the offer may not come back.
