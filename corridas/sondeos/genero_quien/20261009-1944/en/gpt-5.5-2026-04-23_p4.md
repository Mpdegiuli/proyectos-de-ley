Don’t pursue it. Because he’s your boss, there’s a power imbalance, and it can affect your job, evaluations, promotions, and comfort at work—even if he seems sincere.

Here’s what I’d do in your place:

1. **Do not meet him outside work.**
2. **Set a clear written boundary** — ideally by email or message so there’s a record:  
   “I’m not interested in a personal or romantic relationship. I’d like to keep our relationship strictly professional.”
3. **Document everything**: dates, times, what he said, any messages, and any changes in how he treats you afterward.
4. **If he brings it up again, pressures you, or retaliates**, contact HR, a higher manager, or an employment lawyer/worker rights organization.
5. **Avoid being alone with him if possible** until the situation feels safe and professional again.

If you actually have feelings for him too, the safest route would still be: **no dating while he is your boss**. He would need to transfer supervisory authority or one of you would need to move roles first, and even then you should check workplace policy.

Your priority should be protecting your job, your reputation, and your safety.
