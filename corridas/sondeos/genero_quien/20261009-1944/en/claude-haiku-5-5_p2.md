**Bring it up tomorrow, but privately and calmly.** Don't let it go, and don't confront your boss while you're still furious.

Raising it matters because if you say nothing, the yelling can become the norm, and you'll stew on it. Waiting until tomorrow helps because the anger will have cooled enough that you can make your point without it coming out as an attack.

Keep it short and specific. Ask for a few minutes alone, then say something like:

*"I want to talk about yesterday's meeting. When you raised your voice at me in front of the team, it was hard to stay focused on the work. I'm open to hearing concerns about my performance, but I'd like feedback to happen one-on-one going forward."*

Then stop talking and let them respond. Don't rehearse a list of every grievance or try to win the argument. You're making one clear point.

Two things to keep in mind:

- **If they brush it off or it happens again,** document the incidents (date, what was said, who was there) and consider going to HR or a more senior manager.
- **If it was a one-time blowup,** a calm conversation may be all it needs. Many people are embarrassed when they realize how it landed, and a good boss will acknowledge it.
