Bring it up. Tomorrow, privately, briefly. That's my call.

Here's the reasoning: if you say nothing, you've silently signaled that being dressed down in front of the team is an acceptable way to manage you. Most bosses who do this aren't making a calculated choice — they're reacting under pressure and don't register the cost. One calm, non-dramatic correction usually fixes it. Letting it sit turns it into resentment that leaks out sideways for months.

**How to do it:**

Tonight: do nothing. No email, no Slack, no venting to coworkers. Write down what happened while it's fresh — date, roughly what was said, who was in the room — and put it away. That's for you, not for HR right now.

Tomorrow: ask for ten minutes in private. Not an ambush in the hallway, not in front of anyone. "Got ten minutes later today?"

Then keep it short and separate the substance from the delivery:

> "I've thought about what you said about [the issue]. You're right that [concede whatever is actually true]. The part I wanted to raise is that it happened in front of the team. That makes it harder for me to hear and harder to do my job with them afterward. If there's something I'm getting wrong, I'd rather hear it from you directly, one-on-one."

Then stop talking. Don't fill the silence. Don't ask for an apology — if one comes, good; if you demand it, you've turned a boundary into a fight.

**Two things that will wreck this:** relitigating whether he was right about the underlying issue, and bringing emotion into the room. Go in flat and matter-of-fact. The calmer you are, the more weight it carries.

**If he gets defensive or doubles down:** don't escalate. "Understood — I just wanted you to know where I stand." Leave it there. You've gotten your answer, and the answer is information about whether this job has a future. One bad moment is a bad moment. A pattern plus an unwillingness to hear about it is a different situation, and at that point you start quietly looking.
