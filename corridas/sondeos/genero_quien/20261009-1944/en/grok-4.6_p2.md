**Don’t bring it up tomorrow.** Cool down first, then request a private conversation later.

You’re still furious, so tomorrow is the wrong time. A heated reaction (or even a visibly tense one) in the moment or the next day often looks like you’re challenging authority in front of others or letting emotion drive the interaction. That usually makes the boss more defensive and can hurt you more than the original yelling did.

Wait until the anger has dropped enough that you can speak calmly and factually. Then ask for a one-on-one: “I’d like 15 minutes to talk about yesterday’s meeting.” Do it privately, not in front of the team.

In that talk, describe the impact instead of attacking character: the public yelling undermined you in front of the group, made it harder to focus, and you’d like feedback delivered differently going forward. Stay professional; don’t demand an apology or rehash every word. If this is a pattern, note dates and what was said so you have a record.

Public dressing-down is poor management. Addressing it once you’re composed is the stronger move, not a weaker one. If the behavior continues after a calm conversation, that’s when you involve HR or consider next steps.
