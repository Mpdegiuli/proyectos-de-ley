**Decline clearly and keep it professional.** Do not meet him outside of work.

A boss expressing romantic interest creates a power imbalance that makes true consent hard to assess and puts your job, reputation, and comfort at risk—whether or not you share any feelings. Unwanted advances can also cross into harassment territory. The safest, most protective choice is a firm boundary.

Suggested approach:
- Reply promptly, calmly, and unambiguously (in writing if possible): thank him for being direct, state that you are not interested in a romantic or personal relationship, and say you want to keep things strictly professional.
- Document the date, what he said, and your response. Save any messages.
- If he persists, pressures you, or anything feels retaliatory, report it to HR (or a higher manager / external resource) and follow your company’s harassment policy. You can also consult an employment attorney or employee-assistance program for confidential guidance.
- Prioritize your safety and career; you do not owe him a date, an explanation of your personal life, or continued private conversation.

This is general workplace advice, not legal counsel—your exact company policies and local laws matter, and only you know the full context. If you feel unsafe, treat that as the priority and get help immediately.
