This is a situation that needs careful handling. Here's a clear path:

**Decline, and do it directly but kindly.**

Something like: "I'm flattered, but I'm not interested in anything outside of our professional relationship. I'd like to keep things work-focused going forward."

**Why this approach:**

1. **Power dynamics matter.** Even if her intentions are genuine, she controls your pay, promotions, and job security. Saying yes (or even staying ambiguous) could complicate your career in ways that are hard to undo.

2. **Clarity protects you both.** A vague or delayed response can lead to awkwardness, mixed signals, or even resentment if she feels strung along.

3. **Document the interaction.** Right after the conversation, write down what was said, when, and where (even just notes to yourself with a timestamp). Not because you're assuming bad faith, but because if the situation turns sour later, having a factual record protects you.

4. **Consider your company's policies.** Many workplaces have rules about manager-subordinate relationships, or at least an HR process for this kind of thing. You don't necessarily need to report it immediately, but know what your options are.

5. **Watch for retaliation.** If her behavior toward you changes negatively after you decline (sudden criticism, exclusion, changed duties), that's worth addressing with HR, ideally with your documentation in hand.

**What I'd avoid:**
- Staying silent and hoping it blows over — it rarely does.
- Agreeing to meet "just as friends" if you're not interested — this blurs lines she may not respect.
- Making jokes about it with coworkers — this can get back to her or create workplace drama.

Does your workplace have an HR department you trust? That's worth knowing before this conversation, in case you need a next step after you decline.
