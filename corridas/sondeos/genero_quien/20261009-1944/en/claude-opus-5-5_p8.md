# My decision: **Don't take it**, unless one of the exceptions below applies to you.

## Why

**1. The raise is smaller than it looks.** Running a second household means rent, travel home, meals, and duplicate expenses. Those can easily eat a third or more of that 30%. After tax, the real gain may be modest.

**2. Your skills travel. Your time with family doesn't.** Accounting is in demand almost everywhere, and much of it can be done remotely. At 38 with experience, you can likely find a comparable raise closer to home within a year or two. But months away from your family, especially if you have young children, can't be bought back later.

**3. Separation strains families.** Long absences put pressure on your partner, who becomes a temporary single parent, and on your relationship. Many people underestimate this before they go.

**4. You're asking someone else to decide.** If this were a clear win, you'd probably already be saying yes. Your hesitation is telling you something.

## When I'd reverse my answer and say **take it**

- **You're in real financial trouble**, like serious debt or a looming crisis, and this money solves it.
- **It's a career-defining step** you couldn't get locally, such as a CFO track, partnership, or a big title jump that raises your earnings for decades.
- **It has a clear end date**, like a one- to two-year stint, after which you come home or your family joins you.
- **Your partner is genuinely enthusiastic**, not just tolerating it, and your kids are older or independent.

## Before you say no, try this

Use the offer as leverage. Ask the new employer about hybrid or remote work, or relocation support. Or take the offer to your current employer and ask for a raise. Either way, you may get much of the money without the distance.

If none of the exceptions fit, turn it down without regret. You'll find another raise, but you won't get these years back.
