### Concrete factors to weigh (do the math and the conversations)
- **Net money after costs**: 30% gross is not 30% in your pocket. Factor extra housing or hotels, frequent flights, dual living expenses, taxes, possible lost spouse income or childcare, and the opportunity cost of not being home. Run the after-tax, after-expense number. If the extra cash mainly funds lifestyle inflation or is needed for debt/education/retirement, that changes the calculus. If you're already comfortable, the marginal dollars often buy less happiness than time.
- **Family impact, not slogans**: Presence during ordinary weeks, not just holidays, is what kids and spouses usually remember. Several months away repeatedly correlates with strain on marriages and with kids (especially younger ones) experiencing the parent as intermittent. Video calls help but do not substitute. Ask your spouse and any children, separately and without leading, how they actually feel—not what they think you want to hear. If they are already stretched, this can accelerate resentment or a slow drift.
- **Duration and reversibility**: "At least several months a year" is vague. Get it in writing: expected weeks, overtime, weekends, whether it can expand, relocation pressure later, and exit options. Temporary hardship for a clear career step or a defined financial goal is different from an open-ended pattern.
- **Your own well-being and the job itself**: Isolation, hotel life, and missing milestones wear on people. Accountancy work can be intense; adding geographic separation often compounds burnout. Would the new role actually be better work, or just better pay in a similar grind?
- **Alternatives worth testing first**: Negotiate remote/hybrid, compressed travel, family relocation (even if only for part of the year), a shorter trial period, or a counter-offer locally. Many "must take it" offers have more flexibility than the first pitch.

### A practical process instead of a coin-flip
Write two 5-year pictures in some detail: one with the job (finances, daily family reality, your health, career trajectory) and one without (including what you'd do with the status-quo money and time). Include the unglamorous parts. Discuss both with your spouse. If you have kids old enough, include them at an age-appropriate level. Sleep on it; do not decide in the excitement of the offer.
