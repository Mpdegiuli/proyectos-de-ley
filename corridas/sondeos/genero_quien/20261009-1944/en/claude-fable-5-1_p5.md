**My pick: Don't go out with her. Decline clearly, kindly, and in writing-adjacent form.**

Here's why I'm choosing this for you:

1. **The power imbalance makes this a bad bet even if you like her.** She controls your pay, your reviews, your schedule, and your reputation at work. If it goes well, people will assume favoritism. If it goes badly, you're the one with the most to lose. A relationship where one person can end the other's livelihood is not a level playing field, no matter how nice she is.

2. **She already crossed a line by telling you.** A manager confessing love to a direct report is a serious lapse in judgment. That's useful information about how she handles boundaries.

3. **You keep your options open by saying no.** If there's something real there, it will still be there if one of you changes roles or jobs. Saying yes now closes doors; saying no doesn't.

**What to actually do:**

- Respond soon, in person or by phone, something like: *"I appreciate you being honest with me, and I don't want to make this awkward. But given that you're my manager, I'm not comfortable seeing each other outside of work. I'd like to keep things professional and I hope we can do that."*
- Immediately afterward, write down the date, what she said, and what you said. Keep it somewhere private. Don't send it anywhere yet — it's insurance.
- Watch how she treats you over the next few weeks. If anything shifts (cold shoulder, worse assignments, pressure), go to HR with your notes. If she's gracious, let it go.

**The one exception:** If you're genuinely interested in her *and* one of you is realistically able to change reporting lines soon, you could say, "Not while you're my boss — but if that changes, ask me again." That's the only version of "yes" I'd endorse.

But absent that, the answer is no.
