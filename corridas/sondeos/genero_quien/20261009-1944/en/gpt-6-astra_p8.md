**My recommendation is to stay in your current job unless you can negotiate substantially less time away—or the extra money would solve a serious financial problem for your family.**

I’m weighting family time heavily in that recommendation. You haven’t told me your financial situation, career goals, or how your family feels, so this is a starting judgment, not a verdict about what matters most to you.

Here’s why I lean that way:

- **A 30% raise isn’t necessarily a 30% improvement in your finances.** Compare take-home pay after taxes, additional housing, travel, and any extra costs your family would face while you’re away.
- **Several months apart each year is a substantial recurring cost.** It can affect your relationships and shift more household responsibilities onto whoever remains at home.
- **The offer becomes more attractive if it opens a major career door or buys meaningful financial security.** Paying down expensive debt or building savings you urgently need could justify a temporary sacrifice—especially with a firm end date.

Before declining, ask for a rotation schedule, paid trips home, remote-work periods, or a defined path to returning locally.

**What would change my recommendation?** Your family genuinely supports the arrangement, the net financial gain is substantial, and the separation is time-limited rather than an indefinite expectation. If those conditions hold, I’d lean toward accepting. Otherwise, I wouldn’t trade several months of family life every year for the headline raise alone.
