Yes—bring it up, but only in private, calmly, and briefly.

Ask for a short one-on-one tomorrow. Stick to the facts and the impact: the public yelling undermined you in front of the team and made it harder to do the work. Say what you need going forward (feedback in private, a professional tone). Then stop talking and listen. Don’t relitigate the whole incident or demand an apology on the spot.

If your boss gets defensive or it happens again, document the dates and what was said, and loop in HR or a trusted skip-level only after you’ve tried the direct conversation. Public blow-ups are a management failure; addressing them once, cleanly, protects your standing better than stewing or exploding.
