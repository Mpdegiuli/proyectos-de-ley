**My decision: Don't take the job — at least not as offered.**

Here's my reasoning:

**The math isn't as good as it looks.** You're 38 and presumably established. A 30% raise sounds significant, but accountants have steady, portable careers — your income trajectory doesn't depend on this one offer. Meanwhile, being away several months a year carries real costs: travel, maintaining two households (or living alone), and potential strain that 30% may not cover. Calculate your *net* gain after those expenses; it may shrink to 10–15%.

**The real cost is time with your family that you can't buy back.** If you have a spouse and children, several months a year away during their formative years is a permanent trade. Money compounds; missed childhoods don't. At 38, you likely have 25+ working years left to increase your income, but your kids are only young once, and distance is one of the most common slow erosions of marriages.

**The asymmetry of risk.** If you decline and later regret missing the money, you can find another raise — accounting skills are in demand everywhere. If you accept and your family suffers, you can't undo those months. When one side of a decision is reversible and the other isn't, choose the reversible side.

**What I'd do instead:**
1. Counter-offer: remote/hybrid work, or a shortened travel commitment.
2. Use the offer as leverage in your current job — a competing offer is negotiating power.
3. If the money truly changes your family's life (debt, housing, security), ask whether the family could *relocate together* rather than you living apart.

Take the job only if relocation with your family is on the table. Otherwise, decline — but use it to negotiate.

That's my call. Your family is the asset that appreciates most with presence, not the one you fund by being absent.
