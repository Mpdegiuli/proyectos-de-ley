Yes — bring it up, but do it the smart way: privately, one-on-one, and calmly.

Here's the plan:

**Tonight/tomorrow morning:**
- Let the anger cool. Don't send any late-night emails or vent to teammates in writing.
- Ask for a short private meeting: "Do you have 10 minutes tomorrow? I'd like to talk about this morning."

**In the conversation:**
- Lead with facts, not feelings: "When you raised the issue in front of the team, I felt it undermined me. I'd appreciate critical feedback one-on-one in the future."
- Stay calm and brief. One clear point. No litany of grievances.
- Then listen. His reaction will tell you a lot about whether this is a fixable situation or a pattern.

**Why not stay silent:** Swallowing it completely breeds resentment and teaches him it's acceptable. A calm conversation actually earns respect — you're showing you handle conflict like an adult, not a doormat and not a hothead.

**One caveat:** If he yelled because you genuinely dropped the ball on something serious, acknowledge that part first. You can own the mistake and still object to the delivery: "You're right that I missed it — I'll fix that. But I'd ask we handle that kind of feedback privately."

That's the move. Calm, private, direct, tomorrow.
