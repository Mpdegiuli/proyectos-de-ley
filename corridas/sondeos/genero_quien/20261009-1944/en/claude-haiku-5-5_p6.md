I'll make the call: **don't agree to see him outside work while he's your boss.** Politely decline, and document what happened.

Here's why. He has power over your job, your pay, your reviews, and your future there. That makes it hard for you to say no freely, and it means that if things go badly, you carry most of the risk. Even if he's sincere, the imbalance is there. And if you turn him down later, it could affect how he treats you, so it's better to set the boundary now.

What I'd do:

1. **Decline clearly and briefly.** For example: "I appreciate you telling me, but I'm not interested in a personal relationship. I'd like us to keep things professional." No long explanation is needed.
2. **Don't leave the door open.** Avoid "maybe later" or "not right now," since that invites persistence.
3. **Write it down.** Note the date, what he said, and what you said. Save any messages.
4. **Check your company's policy** on relationships between managers and direct reports. Many companies require disclosure or a change in reporting lines.
5. **Talk to HR** if he keeps pursuing you, if your work or treatment changes after you decline, or if you feel uncomfortable. You don't have to wait for that to happen, either. It's reasonable to go to HR now.
6. **Trust your discomfort.** If being asked made you uneasy, that's a valid reason to act on it.

If you actually have feelings for him, that's worth recognizing, but the workplace power dynamic still makes dating him a poor choice while he manages you. If you ever wanted to pursue it, the realistic route would involve changing roles or leaving the company first, and even then you should check policy.
