Yes — bring it up tomorrow. Do it privately, calmly, and directly.

Don’t let it slide, because being yelled at in front of the team is unprofessional and it can affect how others see you. But don’t confront them while you’re still furious.

Here’s what I’d do:

1. **Write down what happened tonight**
   - Date/time
   - Who was present
   - What your boss said/did
   - How it affected the work/team dynamic

2. **Ask for a private conversation tomorrow**
   You can say:
   > “Do you have 15 minutes today? I’d like to discuss what happened in yesterday’s team meeting.”

3. **Keep it firm, not emotional**
   Use something like:
   > “I want to talk about the moment in yesterday’s meeting when you raised your voice at me in front of the team. I’m open to feedback, but being yelled at publicly isn’t acceptable or productive. In the future, I’d like feedback to be given directly and respectfully.”

4. **Then pause**
   Let them respond. You’re not asking permission to feel disrespected — you’re setting a boundary.

5. **If they dismiss it, blame you, or it happens again**
   Document it and consider going to HR or your boss’s boss, especially if this is part of a pattern.

So my choice: **bring it up tomorrow — privately, professionally, and with a clear boundary.**
