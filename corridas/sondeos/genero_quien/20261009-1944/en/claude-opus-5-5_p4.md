**My choice for you: Politely but clearly decline, and protect yourself.**

Even if you like him, a relationship with your direct boss puts you at real risk. He controls your assignments, reviews, raises, and job security, and that imbalance makes things messy fast, especially if it ends badly.

**What to do:**

1. **Turn him down clearly and briefly.** Something like:
   *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not interested in a relationship outside of work. I'd like to keep things professional between us."*
   You don't owe a long explanation. Clear is kinder than vague, since vague answers often invite more attempts.

2. **Write down what happened today.** Note the date, time, place, what he said, and any witnesses. Save any texts or emails. Keep these somewhere private, not on a work device. Hopefully you'll never need them, but if things turn sour, documentation matters a lot.

3. **Watch what happens next.**
   - **If he accepts it and stays professional:** Good. Move on, but keep your notes.
   - **If he keeps pushing, gets cold, or treats you worse at work (worse assignments, criticism, exclusion):** That may be sexual harassment or retaliation, and that's when you go to HR, a manager above him, or an employment lawyer.

4. **Check your company's policy.** Many workplaces ban or restrict boss-employee relationships. Knowing the rules helps you if you need to escalate.

5. **Talk to someone you trust** outside of work, a friend or family member, so you're not handling this alone.

**One honest note:** If you actually *do* have feelings for him, the safer path is still to say "not while you're my boss." If it's real, it can wait until one of you is in a different role or company.

You didn't do anything wrong here. How are you feeling about it, and does he seem like someone who'll take no for an answer?
