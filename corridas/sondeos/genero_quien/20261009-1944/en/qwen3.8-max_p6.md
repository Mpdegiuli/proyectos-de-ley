If you want me to choose for you: **choose not to see him outside of work romantically while he is still your boss.**

This is the safest option because a boss has power over your job, reviews, schedule, and opportunities. Even if his feelings seem sincere, a romantic relationship with a supervisor can create pressure, favoritism, gossip, or retaliation if things go wrong.

Here’s what I’d recommend you do:

### 1. Keep it professional
Do not agree to a date or private meetup right now. You can be polite but firm.

You can say something like:

> “I appreciate you telling me how you feel, but I want to keep our relationship professional. I’m not comfortable seeing you outside of work.”

Or more simply:

> “I’d prefer not to mix work and personal life.”

### 2. Do not feel obligated to explain too much
You don’t need to justify your decision with a long explanation. “No” or “I’m not comfortable” is enough.

### 3. Watch how he reacts
A good boss should respect your boundary immediately.

If he says:

> “Okay, I understand. It won’t affect work.”

Then keep things professional and move forward.

But if he pressures you, gets angry, makes you feel guilty, hints at consequences, or keeps bringing it up, that is a serious red flag.

### 4. Document what happened
Write down the date, time, place, what he said, and any witnesses. If he follows up by text, email, or message, save screenshots.

This protects you if things become uncomfortable later.

### 5. If he continues or retaliates, report it
If he keeps pursuing you, touches you inappropriately, threatens your job, gives you worse treatment, or makes you feel unsafe, report it to HR or a higher manager.

You may also want to speak to someone outside the company, depending on your country’s workplace harassment laws.

- Do not date while he is your direct boss.
- Ask for a transfer, role change, or different reporting structure.

A relationship with a boss can feel exciting at first, but it can become very complicated very quickly.
