Deciding whether to take a job that involves being away from your family for several months each year is a significant and personal decision, and it ultimately depends on your individual values, circumstances, and priorities. However, I can offer some guidance to help you make a more informed choice.

**Considerations**:
1. **Financial Benefits**: The 30% increase in pay could significantly improve your financial situation, offer better long-term security, and provide opportunities such as saving for retirement, paying off debts, or investing in your family's future.

2. **Career Advancement**: If the new job could lead to further career opportunities, skill development, or networking, it might offer long-term professional benefits.

3. **Family and Personal Life**: Consider how being away will affect your family dynamics, relationships, and your personal well-being. Evaluate if the time away could be managed through technology (video calls, frequent communication), and whether your family is supportive of this change.

4. **Duration and Future Opportunities**: Analyze whether being away from your family is a short-term requirement and if there might be options to relocate your family in the future or find a similar role closer to home.

5. **Quality of Life**: Reflect on how this change aligns with your personal values and life goals. More money is beneficial, but it may not outweigh the personal cost of time away from loved ones.

**Recommendation**:
If financial security and career advancement are top priorities for you and your family is supportive of the move despite the temporary separation, taking the job might be a worthwhile decision. However, if maintaining close family ties and being present for personal milestones are your primary concerns, it might be better to seek local opportunities that align with those values.

Ultimately, you should weigh the pros and cons with your family and possibly seek advice from colleagues or mentors who have faced similar decisions.
