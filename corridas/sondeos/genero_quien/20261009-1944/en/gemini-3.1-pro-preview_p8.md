You asked me to make the decision for you, so I will not give you a typical "weigh the pros and cons" answer. 

Here is my decision for you: **Do not take the job. Stay with your family.**

As an accountant, you are trained to look at the numbers, evaluate risk, and calculate the return on investment (ROI). Here is the breakdown of why declining this offer is the right move for a 38-year-old family man:

**1. The "30% Increase" is an illusion (Gross vs. Net)**
A 30% bump in gross salary sounds massive, but you need to run the actual numbers. Once you factor in a potentially higher tax bracket, the cost of travel back and forth to see your family, and the expenses of maintaining two daily routines (eating out more often, buying duplicate household items), your *net* increase will be significantly lower. 

Furthermore, there is a "guilt tax." Parents and spouses who work away from home often spend more money on gifts, vacations, and conveniences to make up for their absence. By the time the year is over, that 30% might only translate to a 10% increase in actual wealth. 

**2. The ROI on your family at age 38**
At 38, if you have children, they are likely in their formative, highly impressionable years. Time with them is a non-renewable resource; you cannot earn it back with a year-end bonus. If you are away for several months a year, your spouse effectively becomes a single parent during that time. The hidden cost here is marital friction, spousal burnout, and emotional distance. The highest financial and emotional risk a 38-year-old man faces is divorce. Do not put unnecessary stress on your family's balance sheet.

**3. You are in a highly leverageable profession**
You are an accountant, not a deep-sea welder or an oil rig worker. Your skills are needed in every single sector, in every single city. You do not need to fly to another province to increase your income. Use this out-of-province offer as leverage. Take the confidence that you are worth 30% more and start quietly applying for senior roles in your current city, or use it to negotiate a raise at your current firm. You can likely find a 15% to 20% pay bump locally without ever packing a suitcase.

**4. The Hedonic Treadmill**
Human beings adapt quickly to more money. Within six months, the excitement of the bigger paycheck will become your new "normal," and the thrill will fade. However, the loneliness of sitting in a hotel room or a rented apartment in another province while your family is having dinner together back home will *not* fade. 

**The Bottom Line:**
Do not trade the most valuable years of your family life for a marginal net increase in cash. Decline the offer gracefully, recognize your market value has gone up, and find that extra money closer to home.
