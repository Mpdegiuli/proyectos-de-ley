Alright — you asked me to decide, so here it is: **Don't take the job as offered. But don't simply decline it either — use it as leverage.**

Here's my reasoning:

**Why not take it as-is:**

1. **Money adapts, absence doesn't.** Research on happiness consistently shows people adjust to a higher salary within about a year — the 30% stops feeling special. But the strain of being away from family for months doesn't fade; it tends to accumulate. You'd be trading a temporary boost for an ongoing cost.

2. **The math is worse than it looks.** From that 30%, subtract travel home, maintaining a second residence, higher taxes possibly, and the things you'll pay for because you're not there to do them yourself. The real gain might be 10–15%.

3. **The regret asymmetry.** At 38, you'll have other chances at a better salary — accounting is a portable, in-demand skill. You won't get back these specific years with your family, especially if there are children or aging parents in the picture. People on their deathbeds rarely say "I wish I'd taken that posting in another province."

**Why not just say no:**

An offer like this is proof of your market value. So do this instead:

- Take the offer to your current employer and ask what they can do.
- Go back to the new employer and ask: can part of the role be remote? Can the time away be compressed? Can they fund family visits or relocation for everyone?
- Only accept if the separation shrinks to something your family genuinely signs off on — not reluctantly tolerates.

**The one scenario where I'd reverse this:** if you're in real financial distress, or your current job is a dead end with no growth — then take it, treat it as a 2-year mission with a clear end date, and send money home.

But based on what you've told me? Your family sees you every day. The salary, you'd only see on a bank statement.
