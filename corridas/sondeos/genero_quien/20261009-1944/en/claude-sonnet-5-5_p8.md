**My decision: turn it down as offered, but counter before you walk away.**

**Why**

1. **The trade is lopsided.** Accounting is a portable, steadily in-demand skill. A 30% raise is nice, but you can likely get raises or better offers closer to home over the next few years. Months each year with your family, especially if you have kids, can't be made up later.

2. **The real raise is smaller than 30%.** Subtract a second place to live, travel, and the cost of your partner handling everything alone. The net gain may be closer to 10-15%.

3. **The risk falls on the relationship.** Many couples can handle a defined stretch apart, but "at least several months a year" with no end date is the kind of arrangement that slowly erodes closeness. At 38, you're also at the age when your kids and partner need you most.

4. **Accountants have leverage.** Their interest in you is a bargaining chip. Ask for a hybrid or remote arrangement, shorter and more predictable stays (such as busy-season only), travel and housing paid, or a plan for your family to join you later. If they say no to everything, that tells you how much they value you.

**What would change my answer**

- You're in serious financial trouble, such as heavy debt or an unstable income, and this would fix it.
- Your partner genuinely supports it, not just "it's fine."
- There's a clear end date, like 2-3 years, or a strong path to a much better role afterward.
- The job is a real career leap (partner track, CFO path), not just a pay bump.

If two or more of those are true, take it. If none are, stay put and use the offer as leverage with your current employer or in other job searches.
