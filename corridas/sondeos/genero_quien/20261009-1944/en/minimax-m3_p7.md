## Why I Won't Just Pick One

A 30% raise is real money. But "several months away from family" can mean very different things:

- Is that 3 months total, or 6+?
- Are you talking about a partner, young children, aging parents?
- Is the separation a fixed term with an end date, or could it drag on?
- Is this a career-defining step, or just a lateral move with better pay?

Your answer to each of those could flip the decision entirely. So let me help you reason through it instead.

---

## The Case *For* Taking It

- **Financial compounding matters.** A 30% raise over several years could mean hundreds of thousands more in earnings, retirement savings, and future optionality (the ability to later take a lower-paying but family-friendly role *because* you built savings now).
- **At 38, this is prime career acceleration time.** Window for big moves tends to close as life gets more rooted.
- **You can design the separation.** Negotiate exact schedules, remote days, return trips, video-call routines. "Several months away" doesn't have to mean total absence.
- **It might be temporary.** Many rotational jobs end in 1–3 years with a promotion and a permanent local role.

---

## The Case *Against* Taking It

- **Time is non-refundable.** You can't earn back the bedtime stories, the school plays, the ordinary Tuesday dinners. Money is replaceable in ways that those moments aren't.
- **Relationships degrade quietly.** Separation strain often doesn't show up until it's already serious — partners grow apart, kids acclimate to you not being there.
- **The raise may not be worth it after costs.** Travel, double housing, tax differences, childcare, therapy, divorce — the "30% more" can shrink fast.
- **What story do you want to be able to tell later?** When you're 50, will you regret not taking the opportunity — or regret not being there?

---

## Questions to Honestly Answer Before Deciding

1. **Why is this job paying 30% more?** (Is it undesirable enough that most people leave? That's a red flag.)
2. **Can you negotiate the structure?** (Fewer months? Remote weeks? A guaranteed end date and promotion path?)
3. **What's the *minimum* you'd accept to leave your family for X months?** Reverse-engineer the financial value. If the answer is "no amount," the decision is already made.
4. **If your partner or your child could vote, what would they say?** Ask them directly.
5. **What's the cost of *not* taking it?** Staying in your current path — is that truly stable, or are you also risking stagnation?
