# My recommendation: Decline politely but firmly.

Here's why this is the safest and most responsible choice:

## The risks are serious and real

- **Power imbalance:** She has authority over your job, pay, and career. Even if things go well, the dynamic is inherently unequal. If things go poorly, *you* are the one who is vulnerable — not her.
- **Professional consequences:** If it ends badly, you could face retaliation, a hostile work environment, or be forced to leave your job entirely.
- **Legal/HR exposure:** If the relationship is discovered (or if it goes south), it could trigger investigations. As the subordinate, you could end up in a very uncomfortable position regardless of what actually happened.
- **No dating is without conflict:** Disagreements happen. Now imagine those conflicts leaking into your professional life with someone who controls your livelihood.

## How to handle it

- Be **respectful and brief**: *"I appreciate the honesty, but I don't think it would be appropriate for either of us to pursue this."*
- **Document** the conversation — date, time, what was said (keep this for yourself).
- **Do not discuss it with coworkers.**
- If she doesn't accept your "no" or retaliates at work, go to **HR or consult an employment attorney.**

I know this may not be the answer you were hoping for, but protecting your career and your well-being has to come first. This situation is already a professional boundary violation on her part — a subordinate dating their boss rarely ends well for the subordinate.
