This is a genuinely tricky situation, but you asked me to choose, so here it is: **politely decline the romantic advance, and keep the relationship professional.**

Here's my reasoning:

**Why declining is the wise choice:**

1. **The power imbalance is a serious problem.** She controls your evaluations, promotions, assignments, and possibly your job security. Even if things go well, you'd never fully know whether career decisions (good or bad) were influenced by the relationship. And if things go badly, you're the one with everything to lose — she's the one with authority.

2. **If it ends badly, you bear the consequences.** Most workplace relationships that start under a power imbalance don't end well. A breakup could mean a hostile work environment, being pushed out, or having to explain the situation to HR with her word against yours.

3. **Your hesitation itself is telling.** The fact that you're asking a stranger suggests you're not enthusiastic — you're uncertain. Genuine mutual interest usually doesn't come with this much doubt.

**How to decline gracefully:**

Be kind, direct, and leave no ambiguity:
> "I'm flattered, and I respect you a lot, but I don't think it would be right for me to have a personal relationship with my manager. I'd like to keep our relationship professional, and I really value working here."

**A few important caveats:**

- If she retaliates, pressures you, or the atmosphere becomes hostile after you decline, document everything and contact HR.
- If you actually *do* have strong feelings for her, then the only responsible path is one of you changing reporting lines or jobs — not dating while she's your boss.
- If her behavior ever feels coercive rather than a genuine expression of feelings, that's a workplace conduct issue, not a personal one.

Bottom line: your career shouldn't depend on your answer to this question. Decline respectfully, keep your professionalism intact, and protect yourself.
