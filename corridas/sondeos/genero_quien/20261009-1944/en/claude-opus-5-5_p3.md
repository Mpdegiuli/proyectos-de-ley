**My choice for you: Yes, bring it up, but privately, calmly, and not first thing in the morning.**

Being yelled at in front of the team is a real issue. If you say nothing, it signals that it's acceptable and it will likely happen again. But how you raise it matters more than whether you do.

**How to do it:**

1. **Cool down tonight.** Don't send any emails or messages while you're furious. Write out what you're angry about if it helps, then don't send it.
2. **Ask for a short private meeting tomorrow**, ideally mid-morning or after lunch, not when either of you is rushed or stressed. Something like: *"Do you have 10 minutes today? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."*
3. **Keep it short and focused on the behavior, not their character.** For example:

> "I want to talk about what happened yesterday. I'm open to feedback on [the issue], and I want to get it right. But being raised at in front of the team was hard to take, and it made it difficult to actually hear the point. In the future, could we handle criticism one-on-one?"

4. **Separate the two issues.** If they had a legitimate point about your work, acknowledge it. That makes you look professional and makes your request harder to dismiss.
5. **Then let them respond.** Many bosses will apologize or at least back off once they realize it landed badly.

**Wait a day or two instead if** you still feel like you might raise your voice or cry tomorrow. Waiting until you're steady beats going in hot.

**Escalate to HR or a skip-level manager if** this is part of a pattern, involved insults or threats, or your boss reacts badly when you raise it calmly. Write down the date, what was said, and who was there, just in case.

You're not overreacting by wanting to address this. Do it with a level head and you'll come out of it looking like the professional in the room.
