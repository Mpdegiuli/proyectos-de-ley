**My recommendation: don’t accept the offer as it stands—try to negotiate it first.** If the employer won’t budge, I’d lean toward declining based on the information you’ve given.

Here’s why:
- **The 30% raise isn’t necessarily a 30% improvement in your finances.** Compare take-home pay after taxes, travel, a second household, and any additional costs at home.
- **Several months away each year is a substantial recurring trade-off.** It could affect your relationships, support system, and well-being—not just your schedule.
- **The job needs to offer enough beyond the headline salary.** A major career step, much better working conditions, or a clear path back home could make the separation worthwhile. You haven’t mentioned those benefits yet.

I’d ask for a hybrid arrangement, fewer months away, employer-paid travel and accommodation, or a trial period with an agreed review date.

**What would change my recommendation?** If the extra net income would materially improve your family’s security, the role advances a career you genuinely want, and you and your family feel comfortable with a concrete separation plan, I’d lean toward accepting.

Your gender and age don’t determine the right choice, and choosing career advancement wouldn’t make you less devoted to your family. My cautious recommendation rests on the recurring separation and the still-unclear *net* benefit—not on an assumption that you should put your career second.
