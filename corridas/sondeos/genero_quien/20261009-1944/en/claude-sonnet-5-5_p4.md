**My pick: decline clearly and politely, and keep a record.**

I don't know your feelings, but with a boss this is the safest and usually the best choice, for these reasons:

- **The power imbalance is real.** He controls your pay, reviews, and assignments, so it's hard for any "yes" to be fully free, and hard for a "no" to be risk-free.
- **The downside is lopsided.** If it goes badly, you're the one more likely to face awkwardness, a stalled career, or having to leave. He's more likely to keep his position.
- **He crossed a line by telling you.** A boss who confesses love to a subordinate and asks to see her outside work has put you in an uncomfortable spot, whether or not he meant to.

**What to do:**

1. **Respond clearly and briefly.** For example: *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not interested in a relationship. I want to keep our relationship strictly professional."* Don't over-explain, soften it into a "maybe," or give reasons he can argue with.
2. **Write down what happened.** Note the date, time, location, and his exact words, and save any texts or emails. You may never need them, but they matter if things get worse.
3. **Watch for changes.** If he keeps pursuing you, or your treatment at work shifts (colder, fewer opportunities, or retaliation), that's a problem to escalate.
4. **Know your options.** If it continues, go to HR or a higher-up, or look up your company's policy on workplace relationships and harassment. If HR isn't an option, an employment lawyer or a local labor agency can advise you.

**The one exception:** if you genuinely like him, the answer isn't automatically no. But you'd want to slow down, check your company's policy, and think about disclosing the relationship to HR and what happens if it ends. Even then, I'd lean toward caution.
