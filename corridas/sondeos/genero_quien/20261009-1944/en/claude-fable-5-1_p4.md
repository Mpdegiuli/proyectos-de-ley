**Say no. Keep it at work, and keep it professional.**

Here's my reasoning:

Even if you find him attractive, dating your direct boss is a bad bet. He controls your pay, your reviews, your assignments, and your reputation at the company. That means:

- If it goes well, people will assume anything good you get is because of him.
- If it goes badly, you're the one who usually ends up leaving, not him.
- Even "no pressure" from a boss is pressure. He may not intend it, but you can't fully separate "I don't want to" from "I'm worried about what happens if I don't."

And honestly, the fact that he put you in this position says something. A thoughtful manager who genuinely cared would have waited until one of you changed roles, or raised it with HR first, or at minimum said "I'll transfer you to a different manager before anything happens." He didn't. He put the burden on you.

**What to actually do:**

1. **Tell him clearly and briefly.** Something like: *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not comfortable with anything outside a work relationship given that you're my manager. I'd like us to keep things professional."* No long explanation, no apology. Do it in person or over a call, not a text that could be forwarded.

2. **Write down what he said and when** — date, place, exact words as best you remember. Save it somewhere personal, not on company systems. You probably won't need it. But if things get weird (cold shoulder, worse assignments, a sudden bad review), you'll be glad you have it.

3. **Watch how he behaves for the next few weeks.** If he's gracious and nothing changes — great, done. If he sulks, retaliates, or keeps pushing, go to HR with your notes. That's not an overreaction; that's exactly what HR is for.

**If you genuinely like him too:** the only version of this that's worth it is one where he first moves you off his team or one of you changes jobs. If he's not willing to do that, he's asking you to take all the risk. Let him make the first sacrifice.

You asked me to choose, so: say no, document it, and move on. You'll feel lighter in a week.
