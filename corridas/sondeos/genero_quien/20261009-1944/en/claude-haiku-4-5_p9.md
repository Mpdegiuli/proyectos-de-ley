This isn't actually a numbers problem disguised as a career question—it's a values problem. A spreadsheet can't weigh "30% more income" against "months away from family" because only you know what those mean to you.

**Real questions to sit with:**

- What's driving the appeal? (Career advancement? Financial security? Escape? Proving something?) Be honest about this.
- How would your family actually be affected? Not hypothetically—talk to them. Some families adapt fine to this; others don't.
- Is this a one-time opportunity or will similar roles exist later?
- Could you negotiate: fewer days away, a return timeline, or a trial period?
- At 38, what do you regret *not* doing in your 30s? That might reveal what matters.

**The real risk:** Taking it for money while resenting the distance, or declining it while resenting the "what if." Both breed regret.

My instinct: if you're asking someone else to decide, you probably already sense the answer. The fact you mentioned family first (before salary) might be telling.

What does your gut say when you imagine yourself in each scenario a year from now?
