**Bring it up privately tomorrow (after you’ve cooled off), not in front of the team.** Public yelling is unprofessional; responding in kind or staying silent both tend to make it worse.

Cool down first. Fury makes people sound emotional rather than factual, which some bosses use to dismiss the complaint. Sleep on it, write a short private note of what happened (date, what was said, who was present), then request a one-on-one.

In the meeting, keep it brief and specific: “Yesterday in the team meeting you raised your voice at me in front of everyone. That was humiliating and unprofessional. I want to do good work, but I need feedback delivered privately and respectfully.” Stick to observable behavior, not character attacks. Ask what the actual performance issue was (if any) so you can address it.

If the boss doubles down, documents nothing, or it repeats, that’s when you loop in HR or a trusted skip-level with your notes. One incident doesn’t always require that leap, but a pattern does.

You’re not required to absorb public humiliation to keep the peace. Address it calmly and on the record; that’s the adult move that protects you without escalating the original scene.
