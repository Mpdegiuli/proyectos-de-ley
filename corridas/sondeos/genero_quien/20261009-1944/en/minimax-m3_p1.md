Yes, bring it up — but not the way you feel right now.

**Here's what I'd do if I were you:**

**Before tomorrow:**
- Sleep on it. Write down exactly what was said and done, with dates and witnesses. Don't trust your memory while you're furious.
- Decide what outcome you actually want. An apology? A change in how you're addressed? Just to be heard? "Being right" isn't an outcome — pick something concrete.

**When you talk to your boss:**
- Request a private meeting, not at their desk in earshot. Email: *"I'd like to discuss yesterday's meeting. When can we talk privately?"*
- Open with the impact, not the accusation: *"When you raised your voice in front of the team, it made it hard for me to focus on the work afterward, and I don't think that's how we do things here."*
- Be brief. Don't re-litigate. State what happened, state how it landed, state what you want going forward.
- End it in under 10 minutes.

**Two things to keep in mind:**

1. Your anger is information — it tells you a boundary was crossed. But anger delivered as anger usually loses. Calm delivered with backbone usually wins.

2. If your boss reacts badly to a reasonable, private request — *that's* your real answer about whether this is a workplace you can stay in. Document it either way.

You don't have to decide today whether this is forgivable. You just have to decide whether to name it clearly. I'd name it.
