Tonight, don’t send an angry message. Write down what happened while it’s fresh: what was said, who was present, how it affected you, and what you want going forward.

Tomorrow, ask for a short 1:1. Say something like:

> “I want to talk about what happened yesterday in the team meeting. When I was yelled at in front of everyone, I felt embarrassed and it made it harder for me to respond productively. If there’s an issue with my work, I’m open to feedback, but I’d like it handled privately and respectfully going forward.”

Then stop and let them respond.

Your goal is not to “win” the argument. Your goal is to set a boundary: feedback is okay; public yelling is not.

If your boss apologizes or responds reasonably, great. If they dismiss you, blame you, or this is part of a pattern, start documenting incidents and consider speaking with HR or another trusted manager.
