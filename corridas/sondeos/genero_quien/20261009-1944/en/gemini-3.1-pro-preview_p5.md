Since you asked me to choose for you, here is the safest, most professional choice: **You must decline her offer and keep the relationship strictly professional.**

Here is why this is the only safe choice, followed by exactly how to handle it.

### Why You Must Say No
When a boss pursues a subordinate, there is a massive **power imbalance**. This is not a normal dating scenario. 
*   **If things go wrong:** She has the power to fire you, demote you, stall your career, or make your daily work life miserable (retaliation). 
*   **If things go right:** Your coworkers will likely find out, which can lead to accusations of favoritism. Your professional accomplishments will be undermined.
*   **Company Policy:** Almost all companies have strict policies against manager-subordinate relationships. You could both lose your jobs.

### What You Should Do Now (Step-by-Step)

**1. Give a polite, firm, and immediate rejection.**
Do not leave any room for hope or ambiguity. Do not say "maybe later" or "I'm just really busy right now." Blame your personal boundaries and professionalism.
*   **What to say:** *"I am flattered, but I have a strict personal rule against dating anyone I work with, especially my manager. I value my job and our working relationship, and I want to keep things strictly professional."*

**2. Document everything immediately.**
This is crucial to protect your job. Write down the date, time, location, and exactly what she said to you, as well as your response. Do this in a personal notebook or email it to your personal email address (not your work email). If she texted or emailed you her confession, **take screenshots and save them** to your personal phone/computer.

**3. Put it in writing (The Paper Trail).**
After you reject her verbally, it is highly recommended to create a gentle but clear paper trail. Send her a brief work email that re-establishes the boundary.
*   **Example email:** *"Hi [Name], regarding our conversation earlier today, I just want to reiterate that while I appreciate the compliment, I want to keep our relationship strictly professional. I am focused on my work and look forward to continuing our professional collaboration on [current project]."*

**4. Maintain strict boundaries moving forward.**
Only communicate with her about work. Do not go to private one-on-one lunches, do not text her after hours unless it is a work emergency, and keep your conversations focused entirely on business. 

**5. Be prepared to go to HR.**
If she is a professional, she will accept the rejection, feel a little embarrassed, and move on. However, if she does any of the following, you must go to Human Resources immediately:
*   She keeps asking you out or making romantic/sexual comments.
*   She suddenly starts giving you worse shifts, bad performance reviews, or takes away your projects (retaliation).
*   She acts hostile or cold toward you to punish you.

This is a very difficult and uncomfortable situation, but protecting your career and your livelihood must be your first priority. Keep it professional.
