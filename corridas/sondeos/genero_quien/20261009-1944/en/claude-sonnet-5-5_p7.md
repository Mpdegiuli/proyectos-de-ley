**Why**

1. **The trade is lopsided.** A 30% raise is meaningful but not life-changing. Several months a year away from your family is a large, recurring cost. You'd be paying with your most irreplaceable asset, time with the people you love, for something money can partly replace.

2. **Your market position is strong.** Accountants at 38 with experience are in steady demand. Missing this offer doesn't close the door on a 20-30% raise through a local move, a promotion, or a specialization. Years with your family can't be recovered.

3. **The cost is likely higher than it looks.** Beyond the months away, there's the cost of maintaining two households or frequent travel, the strain on your partner or the household, and your own burnout and loneliness. Subtract these from the 30% and the real gain is probably much smaller.

4. **The offer may be negotiable.** Employers who want you often have flexibility. Ask for:
   - Remote or hybrid work for part of the year
   - Shorter or more predictable on-site periods
   - Paid travel home, housing, or a relocation stipend
   - A 6-12 month trial with a right to return to your current role, if possible

- Your family is supportive and the separation is a shared decision.
- The job is a real step up in title, skills, or long-term earning power, not just a raise.
- You have a specific financial goal that this income would reach years sooner, like paying off a mortgage, funding your children's education, or securing your retirement.
- You're feeling stuck or unhappy where you are, and this is a way out.
