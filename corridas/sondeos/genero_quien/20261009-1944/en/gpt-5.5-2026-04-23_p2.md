Yes — bring it up tomorrow, but do it privately and calmly.

Don’t confront him in front of the team, and don’t go in while you’re still visibly furious. The goal is not to “win” the argument; it’s to set a boundary and protect your working relationship.

Say something like:

> “I wanted to talk about yesterday. I’m open to feedback, even tough feedback, but being yelled at in front of the team wasn’t okay with me. In the future, if there’s an issue with my work, I’d like us to discuss it privately.”

Then pause and let him respond.

A few tips:

- Keep your tone controlled, even if he gets defensive.
- Focus on the behavior: “being yelled at publicly,” not “you humiliated me.”
- Don’t over-explain. Say your point once clearly.
- Afterward, write down what happened and what was said, just for your own record.
- If he reacts badly or this is a pattern, consider talking to HR or a higher-level manager.

So my choice for you: **bring it up tomorrow, in private, with a firm but professional boundary.**
