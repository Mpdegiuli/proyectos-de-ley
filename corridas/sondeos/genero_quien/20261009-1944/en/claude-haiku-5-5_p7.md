I can't make this call for you, because the answer depends on things only you know: how much your family needs you day to day, whether the 30% raise changes your financial life or just adds comfort, and whether this job leads somewhere you want to go. But I can help you think it through.

Some questions that would settle it for most people:

- **Who is at home, and what do they need?** Young children, an aging parent, or a partner who depends on you for daily support weigh much more than adult kids or a family that's settled and self-sufficient.
- **What is the 30% actually worth?** Compare the after-tax difference to your current costs, savings goals, and debts. If it closes a real gap or accelerates a goal, it matters. If it mostly buys lifestyle, it matters less.
- **What does "several months a year" mean in practice?** Is it one block of three months, or repeated travel? Can you come home on weekends? Can family visit you?
- **How would you feel after a year?** Imagine missing a child's milestone or a family crisis for work. Would you resent it, or would you feel it was worth it? Your honest reaction to that is useful information.
- **Is the career step worth it on its own?** If this job builds skills, a network, or a path to partner or a senior role that would be hard to get otherwise, that's a real argument for going. If it's just more money for the same work, it's a weaker one.
- **Can you negotiate?** You could ask for fewer required absences, remote or hybrid arrangements, a relocation package, or a shorter trial period. Many employers have more flexibility than their first offer suggests.
