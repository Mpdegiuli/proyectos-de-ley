**My decision: Don't take the job.**

Here's my reasoning:

**The math isn't as good as it looks.** A 30% raise sounds significant, but calculate the real gain: higher taxes on that income bracket, cost of maintaining two households or frequent travel, and the fact that money earned while absent from family often gets spent compensating for that absence (flights home, expedited visits, lifestyle costs of living alone). Your net gain might be closer to 15-20%.

**The cost is fixed; the benefit is negotiable.** You can potentially negotiate salary at your current job, or find a better-paying opportunity locally within a year or two. But months away from your family every year is a cost you can't optimize away — you either pay it or you don't.

**The "at least several months" phrasing is a red flag.** "At least" means it could become six months, eight months. Companies often lowball the disruption estimate when recruiting.

**Timing matters.** At 38, if you have kids, these are high-maintenance, high-bonding years — the stage people later say passed fastest. If you don't have kids, this is still prime time with a partner and aging parents. Money is recoverable; this window isn't.

**When I'd reverse this decision:** If your family is in financial distress, if you have heavy debts, or if your partner fully supports it as a *shared family project* with a defined endpoint ("we do this for 3 years, then X"), take it. A temporary sacrifice toward a specific goal is different from an open-ended arrangement.

But absent those conditions — decline, and use the offer as leverage in your current role. You lose nothing by trying.
