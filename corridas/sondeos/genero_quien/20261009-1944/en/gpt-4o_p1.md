I'm really sorry to hear about your experience. Whether or not to bring up the incident is a personal choice and depends on several factors, but here are a few things to consider to help you decide:

1. **Reflect on the Situation**: Consider why your boss might have yelled and whether there was any validity to their concerns, separate from their method of delivery. Think about the impact this had on you and your work environment.

2. **Assess Your Goal**: Determine what outcome you want from bringing it up. Are you looking for an apology, assurance it won't happen again, or a better working relationship?

3. **Consider the Timing**: Sometimes, allowing some time to pass can help you address the issue more calmly. Other times, addressing it promptly can be beneficial so it doesn't linger.

4. **Possible Outcomes**: Think about how your boss might react and whether you're prepared for various outcomes.

5. **Professional Guidance**: If you decide to bring it up, approach the conversation professionally. Focus on how the behavior affected you rather than making accusations.

6. **Support System**: Consider discussing it with a trusted colleague or HR for advice and support before proceeding.

Ultimately, the choice should align with your comfort level and the professional relationship you wish to maintain. If you feel confident and prepared, expressing how it felt for you could be constructive. If not, it might be beneficial to wait until you're more comfortable.
