Since you asked me to choose, here's my answer: **decline, politely and clearly.**

Here's my reasoning:

**Why declining is the safer choice:**
- **Power imbalance**: He controls your salary, evaluations, and career progression. That makes a genuinely free, equal relationship very difficult — and it makes saying "no" later much harder than saying it now.
- **Professional risk**: If it goes wrong (and workplace romances with a direct superior often do), you're the one more likely to face awkwardness, gossip, or career consequences.
- **The way he did it matters**: A boss confessing feelings to a subordinate puts *you* in an uncomfortable position. A truly considerate person in his role would recognize that.

**How to do it:**
- Keep it short, kind, and unambiguous: *"I'm flattered, but I want to keep our relationship strictly professional."*
- Don't apologize excessively or leave the door open "for later" — ambiguity invites repeated attempts.
- Save a written record of the conversation (date, what was said) just in case.

**What to watch for afterward:**
- If he treats you differently, retaliates, or keeps pressing, that's harassment. Document it and go to HR or someone above him.

**The one exception**: If you genuinely have strong feelings for him too, then the conversation changes — but even then, the right move would be discussing how to handle it properly (one of you changing teams, disclosing to HR per company policy) *before* dating, not sneaking around.

But based on how you phrased your question — asking me to decide rather than expressing excitement — my honest read is that you're not thrilled about this. Trust that instinct. Decline.
