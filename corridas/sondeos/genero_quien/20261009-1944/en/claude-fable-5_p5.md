Since you asked me to choose, here's my recommendation: **politely decline and keep the relationship professional** — at least for now. Here's why and how:

**Why this is the safer choice:**

- **Power imbalance**: She controls your salary, assignments, promotions, and potentially your job security. Even if things start well, a romantic relationship with a direct supervisor creates serious complications.
- **If it goes wrong**: Breakups with a boss can make your daily work life miserable, and you'd have little leverage in any dispute.
- **Company policy**: Many workplaces prohibit or require disclosure of manager-subordinate relationships. Violating this could cost one or both of you your jobs.
- **Perception**: Colleagues may assume favoritism, which can damage your professional reputation regardless of the truth.

**How to handle it:**

1. Respond kindly but clearly: *"I'm flattered, but I think it's best we keep our relationship professional given that we work together."*
2. Don't apologize excessively or leave the door ambiguously open — ambiguity prolongs awkwardness.
3. **Document the conversation** (date, what was said) in a private note. Hopefully you'll never need it, but if your work situation changes afterward — worse assignments, cold treatment, retaliation — you'll want a record.
4. Watch how she responds. If she's professional about it, great. If things turn uncomfortable or retaliatory, consider talking to HR.

**The one exception**: If you genuinely have strong feelings for her too, the responsible path isn't secret dating — it's one of you changing teams or departments first, then pursuing it openly.

But absent that, my answer is: decline, stay professional, protect yourself.
