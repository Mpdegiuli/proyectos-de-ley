**My choice for you: Don't date her while she's your boss. Politely decline.**

**Why**

- **The power imbalance is real.** She controls your assignments, reviews, raises, and job security. Even if she's sincere, you can't freely say yes or no to someone who has that much power over you.
- **If it goes wrong, you take the hit.** Breakups happen. When one person is the other's boss, the subordinate usually pays: awkwardness, retaliation, or having to leave.
- **It can damage your reputation.** Coworkers may assume favoritism, and any success you earn could be questioned.
- **It may break company policy.** Many workplaces ban or require disclosure of manager–subordinate relationships, and that can put both your jobs at risk.

**How to say no**

Keep it short, kind, and clear. You don't owe a long explanation. For example:

> "I appreciate you being honest with me, and I respect you. But I'm not comfortable mixing a personal relationship with our working relationship, so I'd like to keep things professional."

Then act normally and professionally afterward.

**Protect yourself**

- **Write down what happened:** dates, what was said, and any messages. Keep this privately, outside work systems. You'll likely never need it, but it's useful if things turn sour.
- **Watch for changes.** If she keeps pushing after you've said no, or your treatment at work changes (worse assignments, cold shoulder, unfair reviews), that's harassment or retaliation. It applies to men too. At that point, go to HR or her manager.

**One exception**

If you have real feelings for her too, the answer still isn't "yes right now." It's "not while she's your boss." The right way would be for one of you to move to a different reporting line or company first, and to disclose it per company policy. But unless you're sure you want that, decline.
