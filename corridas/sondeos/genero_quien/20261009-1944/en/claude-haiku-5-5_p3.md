**Bring it up, but do it privately and after you've cooled down.** Don't raise it tomorrow while you're still furious. Aim for a calm one-on-one within the next few days.

Staying silent lets the yelling become normal, and your boss may not realize how it landed. Speaking up in the moment, while angry, usually turns the conversation into a fight about tone instead of the behavior.

Before the conversation, write down what happened: the date, what was said, and who was there. Keep it factual.

Then ask for a meeting, something like: *"Can we talk privately about what happened in the team meeting on [day]? I want to find a better way for us to handle disagreements or problems."*

In the meeting, stick to three things:
1. **What happened:** "When I was yelled at in front of the team..."
2. **The impact:** "...it undermined my credibility with the team and made it hard for me to contribute."
3. **What you want:** "I need feedback to be given privately, and I'd like us to agree on that."

Then stop talking and let them respond. Don't bring up their history or motives. Focus on what you need going forward.

If they dismiss it or it keeps happening, escalate to HR or your boss's manager, with your notes in hand.
