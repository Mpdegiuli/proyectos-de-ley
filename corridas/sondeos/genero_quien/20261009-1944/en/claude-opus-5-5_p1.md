**My choice for you: Yes, bring it up, but privately, calmly, and not first thing in the morning.**

Here's why. Letting it slide signals that public yelling is acceptable, and the resentment will sit with you. Confronting it in the heat of anger risks making *you* look like the problem. A calm, private conversation protects your dignity and your standing.

**How to do it:**

1. **Tonight:** Write down exactly what happened: date, time, what was said, who was there. Do it while it's fresh. You may never need it, but if this becomes a pattern, you'll be glad you have it.

2. **Tomorrow:** Ask for a few minutes alone, ideally mid-morning or after you've both settled in. Something like *"Do you have 10 minutes today? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."*

3. **In the conversation,** keep it short and focused on the behavior, not their character:
   > "I want to talk about yesterday. Being yelled at in front of the team wasn't okay with me. If there's a problem with my work, I want to hear it, but I need that feedback to be given privately and respectfully."

   Then stop and let them respond. If you made a genuine mistake, you can acknowledge it. That doesn't cancel your point about *how* it was handled.

4. **Watch how they respond.** An apology or a reasonable explanation means it may have been a bad moment. Defensiveness or dismissal tells you something important too.

**When to escalate to HR or a higher manager instead:**
- If this has happened before, to you or others
- If you suspect you're being treated differently because you're a woman (e.g., men on the team make similar mistakes without being yelled at)
- If the yelling included insults, threats, or anything personal
- If you feel unsafe talking to your boss one-on-one

Your anger is legitimate. Being publicly yelled at is humiliating. Use that anger as fuel to stand up for yourself in a measured way, not as the voice you speak in tomorrow.
