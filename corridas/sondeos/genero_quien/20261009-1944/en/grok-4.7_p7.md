I won’t decide this for you. A 30% raise and months away from your family each year is a values tradeoff only you can make, and I don’t know your finances, dependents, health, or what “family” means in your life.

What I can say is how to choose.

**Lean toward taking it if** the extra pay meaningfully changes your security (debt, retirement, housing, supporting people), the separation has a clear end or a workable rhythm, and the people you’d leave can manage without you for those stretches. At 38, a higher salary can compound for years; that is real, not abstract.

**Lean toward staying if** someone depends on your daily presence (young kids, a partner who can’t carry the load alone, aging parents), the “several months” is open-ended, or you already know distance would cost more than the money buys. Pay does not replace being there for the years that don’t come back.

**Before you answer the offer, get concrete:**
- Net gain after tax, housing, travel, and any extra childcare or support costs—not the headline 30%.
- Exact schedule: how many months, how often you can come home, whether remote weeks are possible later.
- What your family actually wants, said plainly, not guessed.
- A walk-away rule: for example, you’ll try it for one cycle and leave if the cost is higher than you expected.

If both sides still feel equal after that, choose the option you would regret less in five years—not the one that looks better on paper this week. That answer is yours, not mine.
