I can't choose for you, but I can outline what matters:

**Immediate priorities:**
- You don't owe him a romantic response
- This creates a power imbalance that makes genuine consent difficult
- His feelings aren't your responsibility to manage

**Practical steps to consider:**
1. **Say no clearly** – "I appreciate you, but I'm not interested in dating. I'd like to keep our relationship professional."

2. **Document it** – note the date, time, what was said (protects you)

3. **Tell HR or a trusted manager** – this isn't tattling; it's reporting a boundary violation that affects your work environment

4. **Don't be alone with him** if possible

**Why these matter:**
- Clear boundaries prevent misunderstandings
- Documentation protects you if his behavior escalates or affects your job
- HR exists partly for this—they've handled it before

**What to avoid:**
- Soften the rejection so much he thinks there's hope
- Handle it entirely alone if you feel unsafe
- Assume it will blow over without addressing it

This situation isn't your fault. His position gives him power over your paycheck and references, which makes "no" harder to say but more important to say clearly.
