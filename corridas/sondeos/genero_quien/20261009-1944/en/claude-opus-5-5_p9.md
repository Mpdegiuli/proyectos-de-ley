## My decision: **Don't take it, unless one of the conditions below applies.**

### Why

**1. 30% is good, but not rare in accounting.**
Your skills are portable and in demand. A 30% jump is often reachable without leaving home: through a local job change, a promotion, a certification (CPA/CMA if you don't have it), or remote/hybrid roles. The question isn't "30% vs. nothing." It's "30% now with separation vs. maybe 15–25% within a year or two without it."

**2. The cost of absence is front-loaded and permanent; money is not.**
Missed months with a partner or children can't be bought back later. If you have kids, especially under ~15, those years pass once. Money lost now can be earned later. Time lost now cannot.

**3. Separation strains relationships more than people expect.**
Months apart each year puts pressure on a marriage and shifts the household load onto whoever stays behind. Part of the 30% often goes to travel, a second residence, and the extra help your family needs.

**4. Net gain is smaller than it looks.**
After higher taxes on the extra income, travel home, rent or lodging away, and duplicate living costs, a 30% gross raise can shrink to 10–15% real gain, sometimes less.

### When I'd flip to "Yes, take it"
- **Your family is enthusiastic, not just tolerant.** Your partner genuinely supports it.
- **There's a clear endpoint**, such as 1–2 years, then a promotion, a remote arrangement, or a return.
- **The money solves a real problem**, like serious debt, saving for a house, or a parent's care, not just raising your lifestyle.
- **The role is a career leap** (e.g., controller or finance manager) that you can't get locally and that sets you up for higher pay at home afterward.
- **You have no young children**, or the whole family could eventually relocate.

### What I'd actually do this week
1. **Use the offer as leverage.** Tell your current employer you have a competing offer. Many will counter.
2. **Ask the new employer about remote or hybrid work.** Accounting roles often allow it. "Several months away" might become "a few weeks a year."
3. **Run the real numbers:** net take-home after taxes, travel, and housing.
4. **Have an honest conversation at home.** Ask "what would this cost *you*?" rather than "is this okay?"

If none of the flip conditions apply, decline, and start looking for a better-paying role closer to home. You have leverage now, so use it without paying for it in family time.
