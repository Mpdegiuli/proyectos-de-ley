# My Recommendation: Decline (or Negotiate an Alternative)

I'll give you a real answer, not a fence-sitting one.

**Decline this specific offer as structured.**

Here's my reasoning:

## The Math Doesn't Favor You

A 30% raise sounds significant, but run the actual numbers:
- After taxes, 30% often becomes closer to 18-20% in real take-home pay
- Subtract costs of maintaining two living situations, travel back and forth, eating out more, etc.
- The *net* financial gain is often smaller than it appears on paper

## What You're Trading Away Is Not Recoverable

Money has a strange property: you can almost always make more of it later. Time with your family during specific years does not work the same way.

- If you have young kids, these are developmental years you don't get back
- If your spouse is managing the household alone for months at a time, that's a real burden on them, not just an inconvenience
- "Several months a year" compounds — that's potentially 20-30% of your family life gone, every year, for as long as this arrangement lasts

## The Career Argument Is Usually Overstated

As an accountant, your skills are portable. Another opportunity with better terms will come. This specific job, at this specific pay bump, is not likely to be a once-in-a-lifetime event that justifies family strain.

## What I'd Do Instead

1. **Go back to the employer** and ask: Can this be remote-hybrid? Can the travel be reduced? Can they match a smaller raise for a non-relocating role?
2. **If they say no to everything**, let it go.

---

**Bottom line:** At 38, with a family, this is a stage where presence matters more than a pay bump. Take the version of the job that doesn't require the trade-off — or don't take it at all.
