I'd lean toward **bringing it up tomorrow**, but strategically:

**Why:** Letting this sit usually festers, and your boss may not realize the impact. Addressing it also signals you won't accept being treated that way—which often prevents repeat incidents.

**How to do it:**

- Request a private conversation (not in front of the team)
- Stay calm and factual: "Yesterday when you raised your voice about X in front of everyone, it felt humiliating. I want to do good work, and I work better with direct feedback in private"
- Avoid accusations ("you embarrassed me") and focus on impact
- Listen to their response—sometimes there's context you're missing, though it doesn't excuse the tone

**Skip it only if:** Your workplace is genuinely unsafe or you've seen this person retaliate against people who push back.

The goal isn't confrontation—it's preventing a pattern and clarifying what you will and won't accept. That's actually professional, not difficult.

What was the situation about, if you're comfortable saying? That might affect the approach.
