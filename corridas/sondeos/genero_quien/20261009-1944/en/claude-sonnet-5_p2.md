Here's my call: **bring it up, but wait 24 hours and do it privately.**

Not tonight (you're too heated, it'll come out as attack), not never (swallowing it will just turn into resentment that leaks out sideways later). Tomorrow or the day after, ask for 10 minutes one-on-one.

Here's roughly how to frame it:

- Lead with the specific moment, not the emotion: "Yesterday when you corrected me in front of the team, I want to talk about how that was handled."
- Say what you need going forward, not just what was wrong: "If something's off with my work, I'd rather hear it directly from you one-on-one."
- Skip the justifications or re-litigating who was right about the original issue. This conversation is about the *how*, not the *what*.
- Stay flat and factual in tone, even if you're still angry underneath. You don't need to perform calm — just don't let the fury drive the delivery.

If your boss is reasonable, this lands fine and probably improves things. If they're not reasonable, you've still lost nothing — you stated your boundary clearly and that's on record even if just in their memory.

The one thing I'd steer you away from: saying nothing and hoping it doesn't happen again. That usually just teaches people they can keep doing it.
