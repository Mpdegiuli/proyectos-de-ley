**My recommendation: Don't take it** — unless one of the exceptions below applies to you.

Here's my reasoning:

**Why decline:**

1. **The math is less impressive than it looks.** A 30% raise shrinks fast once you subtract: maintaining two households (or frequent travel costs), flights/trips home, eating out more, possibly higher taxes, and the things your family will need to pay for that you currently do yourself (repairs, childcare help, etc.). Real net gain is often 10–15%, sometimes less.

2. **The hidden costs are asymmetric.** Money lost can be earned back. Time with your family — especially if you have young kids — cannot. At 38, you likely have 25+ working years left to grow your income. You do not have 25 years of your kids being kids, or of your relationship coasting without strain. Long-distance arrangements are a well-documented stressor on marriages.

3. **You're an accountant — your skills are portable and in demand everywhere.** This isn't a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity in a niche field. Another raise will come, possibly locally, possibly remote. A 30% bump is attainable through job-switching within your own region over 2–3 years in most markets.

**When I'd reverse my decision:**

- You're in genuine financial distress (debt, no savings) and this money solves a real problem, not just adds comfort.
- The role is a significant *career step* (e.g., controller/CFO track, Big 4 partner path) that you can't access locally — then it's an investment, not just a raise.
- The separation is clearly temporary with a defined end date (e.g., 12 months, then relocation together or remote work), and your partner is genuinely on board — not just resigned to it.
- Your family could realistically move with you within a year.

**One test before you say no:** Ask if they'd allow a hybrid arrangement (e.g., one week there, three remote) or relocation support for your family. Companies often flex more than the initial offer suggests. If they won't budge at all, that also tells you how they'll treat you as an employee.

But if it's purely "30% more money in exchange for months away from my family, indefinitely" — walk away. You'll rarely regret the raise you didn't take; people very often regret the years at home they missed.
