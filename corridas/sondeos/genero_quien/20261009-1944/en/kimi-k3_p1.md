**Yes — bring it up. Tomorrow, privately, and calmly.** Letting it slide signals that public yelling is acceptable, and it will likely happen again. But the *how* matters as much as the *whether*, so here's your plan:

**Tonight:**
- Write down exactly what happened — what was said, when, who witnessed it. Keep it in a personal notebook or personal email, not on work devices. This does two things: it creates a record in case a pattern emerges, and it gets the fury out of your body so you're not carrying it into the room.

**Tomorrow:**
- Ask for a brief private meeting: "Do you have ten minutes today?" Do not do this in front of anyone, and don't do it over Slack.
- Use this structure: what happened, how it landed, what you need. Something like:

> "In yesterday's meeting you raised your voice at me in front of the team. I take feedback seriously and I want to do good work here — but being called out publicly like that isn't okay with me. If there's a problem with my work, I'm glad to hear it, privately and respectfully."

- Keep your voice level. Don't apologize for raising it. Don't soften it into a joke. You're not asking permission to be treated decently.

**Be ready for three reactions:**
1. **An apology** — accept it gracefully, and note whether behavior actually changes.
2. **Defensiveness or justification** ("I was under a lot of pressure") — stay calm and repeat the core point: "I understand, and I'm still asking that feedback happen privately."
3. **Dismissal or retaliation** — end the conversation politely, then document it immediately. If this becomes a pattern, that record plus HR (or a trusted skip-level manager) becomes your next step.

**One condition:** if tomorrow morning you're still so angry you can't stay composed, wait one more day — but have the conversation within the week. Don't let it quietly expire, because that's how "furious" turns into "this is just how things are here."

You don't need to be less angry to do this. You just need to be calm in the room. Being yelled at in front of your team was not okay, and saying so — steadily, factually, one-on-one — is exactly the right move.
