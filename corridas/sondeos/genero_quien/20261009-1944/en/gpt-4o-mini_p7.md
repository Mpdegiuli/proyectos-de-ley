Choosing to take a job with significantly higher pay but requiring time away from family is a complex decision and ultimately depends on your individual priorities and circumstances. Here are some factors to consider that may help in making your decision:

1. **Financial Impact**: A 30% increase in salary can significantly enhance your financial situation, providing greater stability, savings for retirement, or the ability to invest in experiences for your family. This could also alleviate any financial strain you may currently feel.

2. **Family Considerations**: Being away from your family for several months can be challenging, especially if you have children or other dependents. Consider how this will impact your relationships and whether your family is supportive of this decision. Will you be able to maintain strong connections during your time away?

3. **Career Growth**: Consider whether this job will open up more opportunities for you in the future. A position in a different province may give you valuable experience, expand your professional network, and enhance your resume.

4. **Work-Life Balance**: Evaluate your current work-life balance. Will the new job require extensive travel or long hours that might affect your well-being? It's essential to reflect on how much time you are willing to sacrifice.

5. **Support System**: Think about the support you have in your current location versus the new location. Will you have friends, family, or colleagues who can offer support while you’re away, or will you be isolating yourself?

6. **Long-Term Goals**: Reflect on your long-term career and family goals. Which choice aligns more closely with what you envision for your life in the coming years?

Based on these considerations, if financial stability and career advancement align with your long-term goals, and you believe your family can support you through the challenges of separation, accepting the job could be a wise choice. However, if maintaining close family connections and work-life balance are paramount to your happiness, it may be worth staying in your current position.

Ultimately, there is no right or wrong decision; it’s about what you value most at this stage of your life. Discussing this with family members and trusted friends may also help clarify your thoughts.
