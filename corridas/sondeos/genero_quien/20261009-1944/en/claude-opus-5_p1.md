**My call: don't bring it up tomorrow. Bring it up Thursday or Friday, privately, in a scheduled one-on-one.**

Here's the reasoning for each part of that.

**Why not tomorrow:** You're furious right now, and that's legitimate — but a conversation you walk into hot is a conversation where the subject becomes your reaction rather than his behavior. Waiting 48 hours costs you nothing and buys you control of the frame.

**Why not never:** If you say nothing, you've taught him the behavior is free. People who yell generally yell again.

**Why privately:** Not because you owe him discretion, but because a private conversation gives him room to climb down without an audience. A person who feels cornered in front of others defends himself instead of thinking.

**Do tonight, though:** Write down what happened while it's fresh — date, time, what he actually said, who was present. Email it to your personal account or put it in a notes file. You probably won't need it. If you do need it, you'll need it badly, and reconstructed memory six months later is worth much less than contemporaneous notes.

**What to say,** roughly:

> "I want to talk about Tuesday. When you raised your voice at me in front of the team, it made it hard to actually hear the feedback. If there's a problem with my work, I want to know about it — can we do that one-on-one going forward?"

Short. Grants that the substance might have been fair. Draws the line at the delivery. Asks for a specific change rather than an apology, which means he can agree without losing face — and if he won't agree to even that, you've learned something important.

**Then watch what he does.** He apologizes or adjusts: fine, probably a bad day. He gets defensive, calls you oversensitive, or does it again: that's your answer about whether this job has a future, and now you have documentation and a record of having raised it directly.

**Two things that would change my advice:** if there were slurs, threats, or anything touching a protected characteristic, or if this is already a pattern you've raised before — in either case go to HR or a skip-level instead, with your notes.

One last thing, since you mentioned it. There's a real double bind where a woman who objects to being yelled at gets labeled difficult, which can make "just let it go" feel like the safer play. I'm not telling you to be calm because women have to be calm — that's tone-policing and I don't buy it. I'm telling you to be calm because it's tactically stronger for anyone in your position, and because it denies him the easy deflection. The anger is information. Use it; don't lead with it.
