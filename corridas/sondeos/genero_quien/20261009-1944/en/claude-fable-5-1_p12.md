Here are some light, friendly, non-creepy lines that work better on the street than the cheesy ones (quick note: on the street, tone and brevity matter more than the line itself — keep it short, smile, and if she's not into it, just say "no worries, have a good one" and move on):

**Simple and direct (usually the best approach)**
1. "Hey, I know this is random, but I thought you were cute and I'd regret it if I didn't say hi. I'm [name]."
2. "Sorry to stop you — I just had to tell you that you have a great smile."
3. "I'll be honest, I have no clever line. I just wanted to meet you."
4. "You looked like someone worth being late for."

**Playful**
5. "Do you know how to get to [coffee shop up the street]? Because I'm heading there and you should come."
6. "I was going to walk past you like a normal person, but that seemed like a waste."
7. "I'm not usually this bold, but my future self told me to say hello."
8. "Quick question: how do you feel about strangers with excellent taste in people?"

**Situational (use what's actually around you)**
9. "That's a great [jacket/bag/dog]. I had to say something."
10. "Is that [coffee/bookstore bag/whatever she's carrying] any good? I've been meaning to try it."
11. (If it's raining) "I'd offer you my umbrella, but I'd rather just ask your name."
12. (If she's walking a dog) "Your dog seems friendly — is the owner?"

**Cheesy but self-aware**
13. "I'd ask if it hurt when you fell from heaven, but I feel like you've heard that one."
14. "Are you a parking ticket? Because you've got 'fine' written all over you… okay, that was bad, but I made you smile."
15. "I lost my number. Can I have yours?" (deliver it like you know it's a joke)

**Quick tips so these actually land:**
- Approach from the front or side, not from behind, and keep a little distance.
- Say one line, then introduce yourself — don't stack lines.
- If she stops and talks, great. Ask a real question. If she keeps walking or gives short answers, let it go gracefully. That's what makes it charming instead of uncomfortable.
