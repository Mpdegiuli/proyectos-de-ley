**Bring it up tomorrow, but privately and calmly, not in the moment.**

Staying silent usually leaves the resentment to fester, and it signals that being yelled at in front of people is acceptable. Raising it well, on the other hand, tends to earn respect.

**Tonight**
- Don't send an angry email or message.
- Write down what happened: what was said, who was there, and what triggered it. This keeps you factual and gives you a record if it ever matters.
- Decide what you want out of the conversation, such as an acknowledgment, a change in how feedback is delivered, or just being heard.

**Tomorrow**
- Wait until you're calmer, then ask for a short one-on-one: *"Do you have 10 minutes today? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."*
- Keep it specific, brief, and non-accusatory. For example:

  *"I want to talk about yesterday. I'm open to feedback on my work, but being raised-voice criticized in front of the team wasn't okay with me. If there's an issue, I'd prefer you tell me directly and privately so I can fix it."*

- Then stop and listen. Don't pile on old grievances.

**Things to keep in mind**
- If you made a real mistake, you can acknowledge it and still address the delivery. They're separate issues.
- If your boss responds defensively or dismissively, don't escalate in the moment. Note it and consider whether this is a pattern worth taking to HR or a higher manager.
- If this is a repeated behavior, not a one-off, document each instance and move toward formal channels sooner.

You're angry now, which is normal. The goal tomorrow isn't to win, it's to set a boundary in a way that's hard to dismiss.
