Here's what I'd do: **decline, put it in writing, and start documenting.**

**1. Write down what happened tonight.** Date, time, location, who else was around, and his exact words as best you remember. Email it to your *personal* account, not work. Do this before details blur — contemporaneous notes carry real weight if this ever escalates.

**2. Decline clearly, in writing.** A short email or text creates a record and removes ambiguity. Something like:

> "I want to be straightforward — I'm not interested in anything outside of work, and I'd like to keep our relationship professional. I hope we can move past this without it affecting things."

Keep it warm but unmistakable. No "I'm flattered, but..." softeners that leave a door open, and no anger that gives him a grievance to point to later.

**3. Don't meet him outside work.** Not for coffee, not to "talk it through," not to let him down gently in person. Any private meeting can be reframed later, by him or by others.

**4. Watch for changes.** Fewer good assignments, exclusion from meetings, sudden criticism, a chilly tone, schedule changes. Document anything that shifts. Retaliation is often subtle and deniable in isolation — a pattern is what makes it visible.

**5. Hold HR in reserve, but know what it is.** HR exists to limit the company's liability. Sometimes that aligns with protecting you; sometimes it doesn't. If he's senior or the company is small, consider a free consultation with an employment attorney *before* you report, so you understand your position. If he escalates, persists, or retaliates, report it — and lead with your documentation.

**The one thing that changes this:** if you actually have feelings for him too. Even then, I'd still say no while he's your boss. The power imbalance means you'd carry nearly all the risk — to your reputation, your advancement, and your job if it ended badly. If it's real, it survives one of you changing roles.

You didn't do anything to cause this, and you're not responsible for managing his feelings. Protect yourself first.
