**Bring it up, but privately, and don't do it while you're still furious.**

**Tonight:** Write down exactly what happened: what was said, what triggered it, who was there. Vent to a friend or write an angry draft you won't send. Don't email your boss while you're angry.

**Tomorrow:** Don't confront your boss in the moment or in front of the team. Instead, ask for a short 1:1: "Do you have 15 minutes today or tomorrow? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting." Having the talk after a night's sleep, or later in the day, is fine. What matters is that you're calm when it happens.

**In the conversation:**
- Stick to specifics and your own experience: "When you raised your voice at me in front of the team, it was hard to take, and I'd rather get that kind of feedback privately."
- Acknowledge any legitimate issue they had, if there was one: "If there's a problem with my work, I want to fix it, but I'd like to hear it one-on-one."
- End with a clear request: "Can we agree to handle things like that privately going forward?"

**Why this is the right call:** Staying silent usually leaves the resentment to fester and signals that the behavior is acceptable. A calm, private conversation protects your dignity without escalating.

**One caveat:** If this is a pattern, or if your boss reacts badly, document it and consider going to HR or a trusted senior person. A one-off blowup is something to address directly, while repeated yelling is a bigger problem.

You're allowed to be angry. Just let the conversation come from the calm version of you.
