**Yes — bring it up. Tomorrow, privately, and calmly.** That's my call.

Here's the reasoning: staying silent teaches your boss that public blowups are acceptable, and you'll stew about it for weeks. Waiting longer than a day or two makes it awkward to raise. Tomorrow is the sweet spot — the incident is fresh, but you've slept on the anger.

How to do it:

- Ask for a few minutes **in private**. Not at your desk, not over Slack, not in front of anyone.
- Address the *delivery*, not whatever the yelling was about. Don't relitigate the underlying issue — that's a trap.
- Keep it to two or three sentences. Something like: *"I wanted to talk about yesterday. If there's a problem with my work, I genuinely want to hear it. But being yelled at in front of the team isn't okay with me — next time, please pull me aside."*
- Then stop talking and let them respond. Your goal isn't an apology; it's that it doesn't happen again.

Tonight: send nothing. Don't draft an angry email, don't vent to coworkers in writing. Do jot down what was said and who witnessed it, while it's fresh — you'll want that record if this ever becomes a pattern.

Two exceptions that would change my answer:

1. **If this is a pattern of behavior**, one calm conversation won't fix it. Start documenting and consider HR or an exit plan instead.
2. **If your boss is genuinely retaliatory** and you believe a private conversation would put your job at risk, that's real information — in that case, protect yourself first and document rather than confront.

But assuming this was a one-off from a boss who's normally reasonable: have the conversation. Most managers, approached calmly the next day, respond better than you'd expect — and even if yours doesn't, you'll have shown yourself you won't quietly absorb it.

Your anger is justified, by the way. Being dressed down in front of the whole team is humiliating, and it's a failure on their part, not yours.
