**Decline firmly, do not meet him outside work, document everything immediately, and report it to HR (or equivalent).** Do not treat this as a personal dating decision.

This is a power-imbalance situation, not a normal romantic invitation. Your boss controls (or can influence) your job, evaluations, hours, pay, and opportunities. That makes the advance inherently coercive even if he frames it as “love.” Reciprocating or going along to keep the peace often backfires: it can be used against you later, create a hostile environment, or leave you with no clean record if things go badly.

Practical steps:
- Respond once, clearly, and preferably in writing (email is best for a record): you want to keep the relationship strictly professional and you will not see him outside work. Do not joke, leave it ambiguous, or over-explain.
- Write down the date, time, exact words, location, and any witnesses right away. Keep copies off company systems.
- Report to HR (or the designated person/policy in your workplace) promptly. Ask what the process is and that you want it documented. If there is no HR or the company is tiny, go to a more senior manager or skip to external advice.
- Do not discuss it casually with coworkers in a way that could be twisted later.

If the behavior continues, escalates, or you face any retaliation (schedule changes, cold treatment, threats to your job), treat that as a separate issue and get advice from an employment lawyer or the relevant government agency in your country (e.g., EEOC in the US). You do not have to handle this alone or “be nice” about it.

This is the option that protects your job and legal position. Any other path (ignoring it, meeting him, hoping it stays private) leaves you more exposed.
