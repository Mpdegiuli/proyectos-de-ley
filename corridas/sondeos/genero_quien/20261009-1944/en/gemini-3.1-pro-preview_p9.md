Since you asked me to make the decision for you, here is your answer:

**Turn down the job.**

Here is exactly why you should stay where you are, broken down both logically (for the accountant in you) and personally.

### 1. The Math Doesn’t Actually Add Up (The Hidden Costs)
As an accountant, you know the difference between gross and net. A 30% gross increase sounds massive, but once you factor in the realities of working away from home, that margin shrinks drastically. 
* **Taxes:** A higher bracket will eat into that 30%.
* **Dual Living Expenses:** Even if the company pays for your travel and lodging, your *personal* costs will go up. Eating out more, buying duplicate items for your travel bag, and the cost of commuting back and forth will drain your disposable income.
* **The "Home Tax":** When you are away, your family loses your labor. Who mows the lawn, fixes the sink, or handles childcare/pet care when you aren't there? Your family might end up paying for conveniences (takeout, babysitters, home maintenance) just to survive your absence. 
* **Net ROI:** Once you deduct taxes and hidden costs, your *actual* net increase might only be 10% to 15%. 

### 2. The "Mission Creep" Red Flag
You noted that you would be away for *"at least"* several months a year. In the corporate world, "at least" is a massive red flag. Three months easily becomes five; five easily becomes half the year. If a company is willing to pay a premium to pull you away from your home province, it means they need you on the ground heavily. You will lose control of your own schedule.

### 3. Accounting is a Highly Transferable Skill
If you were a specialized marine biologist or a deep-sea welder, moving for work might be the only way to advance. But you are an accountant. Every mid-to-large-size business in your current city needs your skill set. At 38, you are entering your peak earning years. You can almost certainly secure a 15% to 20% pay bump simply by jumping to a new company in your current city, without having to pack a single suitcase.

### 4. You Cannot Buy Back Time
At 38, your family dynamic is likely in a highly sensitive phase. Whether you have a partner, young children, or aging parents, the time you spend with them right now is irreplaceable. Being a "FaceTime parent" or a "weekend spouse" puts incredible strain on relationships. A 30% raise will not buy back the Tuesday night dinners, the weekend mornings, or the random daily moments of connection that you will miss. 

### What you should do instead:
Take the ego boost of knowing you are worth 30% more on the open market, and use it as fuel. Update your resume, start quietly looking for roles in your current province, or use this offer as leverage to negotiate a raise with your current employer. 

**The Verdict:** Do not trade the permanent stability of your family life for a temporary, heavily taxed financial gain. Turn it down, stay home, and find a better paying job in your own zip code.
