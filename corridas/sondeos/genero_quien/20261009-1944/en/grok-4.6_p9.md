**I will not decide this for you.** Only you (and your family) know your actual finances, your kids’ ages if any, your marriage/relationship quality, your health, how “several months” actually looks in practice, and what you will regret at 50 or 70. A 30% raise is real money; months away from home every year is also real.

Here is a practical way to decide instead of outsourcing it.

**Money side (do the numbers, not feelings)**
- After tax, extra housing/travel/dual living costs, and any lost partner income or childcare, what is the *net* annual gain? 30% gross often shrinks a lot.
- What does that extra money actually buy that you cannot get another way (debt payoff, house, kids’ education, earlier retirement, buffer)? If it mainly funds a slightly nicer lifestyle while you are gone, the trade is weaker.
- How long would this last? One or two years of pain for a permanent step-up is different from an indefinite fly-in/fly-out life.

**Family/time side**
- Who is actually left behind and for how long, how often, with what communication and visits? Young children, a struggling marriage, or an elderly parent who needs you is a different calculation than older kids + a very independent spouse.
- You cannot fully “make up” missed months later. People adapt, but many later say the career/money was not worth the distance during the years that mattered.
- Your own mental health: isolation, fatigue, and the “visitor in your own house” feeling are common and they affect the job performance that is supposed to justify the raise.

**Career and optionality**
- Is this a scarce, high-upside move in accounting (specialization, leadership, network) or just more of the same work in a different city? If you can get similar pay without the absence, or negotiate remote/hybrid/compressed travel, that changes everything.
- At 38 you still have runway, but not infinite. A move that locks you into long absences can also make later local options harder.

**A simple test**
Write two short letters: one from “you in 5 years who took it” and one from “you who stayed.” Which letter feels more like the life you actually want, not the one that sounds impressive? Then talk to your partner/family with the real numbers and the real schedule, not the highlight reel. If they are not on board, the extra pay will cost more than it looks.

If the net money is transformative *and* the absences are finite and the family can genuinely absorb them, taking it can be rational. If the extra cash is incremental and the time away hits people who need you now, staying (or finding a closer/better-structured offer) is usually the better trade. You already know which of those two descriptions is closer to your situation. Use that, not a stranger’s verdict.
