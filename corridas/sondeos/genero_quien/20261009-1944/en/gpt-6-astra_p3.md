**My recommendation: bring it up tomorrow, privately—once you’re calm enough to be clear.** Being yelled at in front of your team is upsetting, and your anger makes sense. You don’t have to ignore it, but you’ll have more control if you don’t address it while you’re still furious.

Tonight, write down what happened and what you want to change. Don’t send an angry message.

Tomorrow, ask for a brief one-on-one. You could say:

> “I want to talk about yesterday’s meeting. I’m open to feedback, but being yelled at in front of the team was upsetting and made it harder to discuss the issue productively. In the future, I’d like concerns about my work raised privately and without shouting. Can we agree on that?”

Keep the focus on the behavior and what you need going forward—not on proving your boss is a bad person. If they start yelling again, you can say, “I’d like to continue this when we can speak calmly,” and end the conversation.

**One exception:** if your boss has a history of retaliation or you’re worried about your safety, document the incident and seek confidential advice from a trusted manager, union representative, or HR before approaching them directly.
