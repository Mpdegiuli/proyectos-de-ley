## Why this isn't really a free "choice"

- **There is a power imbalance.** He is your boss. Even if you were genuinely interested, saying yes or no isn't happening on equal footing. He has professional power over your career, evaluations, references, and daily life.

- **Many victims of workplace harassment first feel confused, flattered, or scared to act.** All of those reactions are normal.

## What I'd recommend you consider doing

1. **Don't respond to the advance directly in person right now** if you're unsure. A polite, brief boundary works: *"I'm not able to have that kind of relationship outside of work."* Then disengage from the conversation.
2. **Document everything** — date, time, what was said, any witnesses, any subsequent behavior (sudden coldness, schedule changes, criticism, etc.). Save texts/emails.
3. **Check your company's policy** on harassment and relationships between managers and reports. Many companies explicitly forbid it.
4. **Talk to someone safe** — HR, a trusted manager *above* him, a friend outside work, or an employment attorney. Many offer free consultations.
5. **Watch for retaliation.** If your work life suddenly gets harder after you decline, that's a separate legal issue in many places.

- Stay silent and hope it goes away
- Feel guilty if he's "nice otherwise" — niceness doesn't change the dynamic
