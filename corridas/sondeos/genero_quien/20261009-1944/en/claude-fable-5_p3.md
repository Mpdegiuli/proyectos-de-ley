I'll make the call: **Yes, bring it up tomorrow — but not while you're still furious, and do it privately.**

Here's why and how:

**Why you should address it:**
- Being yelled at publicly is a legitimate professional grievance, not an overreaction on your part
- Letting it slide often sets a precedent that this treatment is acceptable
- Unaddressed resentment tends to leak out in ways that hurt *you* (disengagement, passive-aggression, quitting in frustration later)

**Why tomorrow (not tonight, not next week):**
- Tonight you're too angry to be strategic
- Waiting too long makes it awkward and lets the moment calcify

**How to do it:**
1. **Request a private conversation**: "Do you have 10 minutes today? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."
2. **Stay factual, not emotional**: "When I was criticized in front of the team, it undermined me and made it harder for me to do my job. If there's a problem with my work, I'd appreciate hearing it one-on-one."
3. **Don't demand an apology** — ask for a change in behavior going forward. That's a request most bosses can say yes to without losing face.
4. **Listen too.** If there was a real issue behind the yelling, acknowledge it — it strengthens your position on the *delivery* being unacceptable.

**One caveat:** If your boss has a history of retaliation or you're in a precarious position (probation, layoffs looming), document the incident first and consider whether HR or a trusted mentor should be looped in before the conversation.

You've got this. Calm, private, specific — that's the formula.
