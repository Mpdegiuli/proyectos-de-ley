**My call: turn it down** — unless one of a few specific conditions applies, which I'll list at the end.

Here's the reasoning.

**The 30% is smaller than it looks.** After tax, a 30% gross raise is maybe 18–22% more take-home. Then subtract the second residence, flights or drives home, meals out, and the duplicated costs of running a life in two places. Unless the employer covers housing and travel (standard in rotational/camp work, not in most office roles), your real gain could land closer to 10–15%. That's a meaningful but not transformative number to pay for several months a year away from your family.

**Accounting is the wrong field for "rare opportunity" logic.** Your skills are portable, in demand, and credential-based rather than location-based. A CPA in one province is a CPA in another. This matters because the implicit argument for taking a job like this is "chances like this don't come twice" — and in your profession, they do. Changing employers locally typically gets you 10–20% anyway, and remote and hybrid work in accounting is more available now than it was five years ago. So the true premium you're being offered isn't 30% over your current job; it's more like 10–15% over what a normal local move could get you.

**The downside risk is asymmetric.** If this goes well, you have modestly more money. If it goes badly — strain in your marriage, feeling like a visitor with your own kids, resentment accumulating in both directions — the costs dwarf the raise. The financial consequences of a separation alone would wipe out many years of a 30% premium, and that's before the parts that don't have a dollar figure. At 38, if you have children, you're likely in a window of their lives that is genuinely non-repeatable. Money compounds. That time doesn't.

**Reconsider if any of these is true:**

- **It's a step change in role, not just pay** — Controller, CFO track, or entry into a specialized niche (public company reporting, resource industry, forensic work) that genuinely re-rates your career for the next 20 years. A lateral job with a higher number is not this.
- **The money is structurally important, not just nice** — you're carrying heavy debt, have no retirement savings, or there's a specific large goal this would decisively solve in a defined period.
- **It's genuinely time-boxed** — a defined 12–24 months with a clear path to relocating together or returning, not an open-ended arrangement that quietly becomes permanent.
- **Your partner is actively in favor**, not merely willing to tolerate it. "I'll support whatever you decide" is usually not the same thing as agreement, and the difference tends to surface about eight months in.

One concrete move before you decline: go back to your current employer with the offer, and separately test what a local or remote role would pay. If you can capture half the raise without leaving home, the decision gets easy.
