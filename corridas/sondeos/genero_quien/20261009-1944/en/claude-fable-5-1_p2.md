**Yes, bring it up — but not tomorrow morning, and not the way you're feeling right now.**

Here's the plan:

**1. Sleep on it tonight.** Fury is a bad negotiator. Write down what happened factually — what was said, who was there — so your memory doesn't harden into a worse version overnight.

**2. Tomorrow, ask for a private 10 minutes.** "Do you have a few minutes later today? I'd like to talk about yesterday." Don't ambush them, don't do it over Slack, don't do it in front of anyone.

**3. Separate the two issues.** There's the *content* (maybe you did screw something up — be honest with yourself) and the *delivery* (public yelling). You can concede the first while holding firm on the second. Something like:

> "If there was a problem with X, I want to fix it — tell me straight. But I want to ask that we handle it one-on-one next time. Being called out in front of the team made it harder for me to actually hear the feedback, and it's not great for how the team sees either of us."

**4. Then stop talking.** Let them respond. You're not demanding an apology; you're setting a boundary. If they apologize, great. If they get defensive, you've still put it on record calmly, which protects you.

**Why bring it up at all?** Because silence teaches your boss that public yelling works on you. One calm conversation now is cheaper than resentment compounding for months.

**One exception:** if your boss has a pattern of this and a reputation for retaliation, skip the direct conversation and document it instead — dates, witnesses — and consider HR or a trusted senior colleague.

But assuming this is a one-off from a normally reasonable person: have the conversation. Privately, calmly, tomorrow afternoon.
