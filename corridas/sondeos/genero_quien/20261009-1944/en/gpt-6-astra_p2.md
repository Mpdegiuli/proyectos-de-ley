Being yelled at in front of your team can feel humiliating. It makes sense that you’re furious.

**My recommendation: bring it up tomorrow, privately—not in front of the team, and not while you’re still boiling.** You don’t need to pretend it was okay, but you’ll be better positioned if you go in with a clear request rather than an argument.

Tonight, write down what happened: what was said, who was there, and what led up to it. Don’t send an angry message.

Tomorrow, ask for a short one-on-one and say something like:

> “I want to talk about yesterday’s meeting. I’m open to feedback and want to address any concerns about my work. But being yelled at in front of the team wasn’t okay with me. Going forward, I’d like critical feedback delivered privately and without raised voices.”

Keep it brief. You don’t have to prove your boss intended to embarrass you; focus on what happened and what you need to change.

**The exception:** if your boss has a history of retaliation or you genuinely fear for your job, get confidential advice from a trusted workplace resource or union representative before approaching them.

If you’re still too angry tomorrow to keep it measured, postpone a day. That’s preparation, not backing down.
