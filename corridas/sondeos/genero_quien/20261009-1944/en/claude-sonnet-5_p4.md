Here's my recommendation: **Decline, and keep it professional.**

Here's why and how:

1. **Say no clearly and calmly.** Something like: "I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not interested in anything outside of work, and I'd like to keep our relationship strictly professional." You don't owe a long explanation.

2. **Set the boundary once, then hold it.** You don't need to keep re-litigating this if he brings it up again — you can simply say "I already answered that" and move on.

3. **Document what happened.** Write down the date, time, what was said, and any witnesses, even if you don't plan to report it right away. This protects you if the situation escalates or affects your job.

4. **Watch for retaliation or changed treatment.** If your work assignments, evaluations, or opportunities start to shift after you decline, that's a red flag.

5. **Consider looping in HR if:**
   - He brings it up again after you've said no.
   - Your job conditions change.
   - You feel unsafe or pressured.
   Even if nothing further happens, some people choose to report proactively so there's a record — that's optional and depends on your workplace culture and how safe you feel doing so.

6. **Protect yourself going forward.** Avoid one-on-one situations with him if you can, communicate via email/written channels when possible so there's a paper trail, and lean on trusted coworkers if needed.

The core principle: **a boss declaring romantic feelings creates a power imbalance that isn't okay regardless of his intentions**, and you're fully entitled to shut it down without guilt and without it costing you anything professionally. If it does cost you something, that's on him, not you — and that's when HR or even legal advice becomes relevant.
