# My decision: Don't take it as offered. Negotiate first, and walk away if they won't budge.

**1. 30% is a good raise, but not a life-changing one.**
It's meaningful money, but for an experienced 38-year-old accountant, it's not unreachable elsewhere. Your skills are in demand nearly everywhere, and you could likely find a 15–25% raise closer to home within a year or two, without the separation. The time you'd lose with your family can't be earned back later.

**2. "At least several months" is a warning sign.**
That phrasing means the minimum is already significant and there's no ceiling. Arrangements like this tend to expand, not shrink, once you're committed.

**3. The hidden costs eat into that 30%.**
Travel, a second residence or temporary housing, possibly extra childcare or household help, and the strain on your partner or family all reduce the real gain. Run the actual numbers. The net raise may be closer to 10–20%.

**4. You asked someone else to decide.**
When an opportunity truly excites us, we usually look for permission, not a verdict. Your hesitation is information.

# What to do instead
- **Negotiate with the new employer:** Ask for partial remote work, a shorter on-site period, or a defined end date to the travel. Accounting work is often well suited to hybrid arrangements. If they agree, my answer changes to **yes**.
- **Use the offer as leverage:** Tell your current employer you have a competing offer. Many will counter.
- **Test the market:** If you're open to moving jobs, you're probably open to other offers too, including local ones.

# When I'd reverse my answer and say *take it*:
- You're under real financial pressure (debt, saving for a home, supporting someone) that this would solve.
- The role is a genuine career leap (bigger title, partner track, CFO path) that would pay off for decades, not just a bigger salary.
- Your family is fully on board, especially your partner if you have one, and your kids are older or well supported.
- You're feeling stuck where you are and want this change for yourself, not just the money.

If any of those strongly apply, tell me more and I'll reconsider. But based on what you've shared, the trade isn't worth it at these terms.
