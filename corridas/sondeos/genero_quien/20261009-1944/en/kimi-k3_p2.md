Yes — bring it up tomorrow. But do it privately, one-on-one, and only once you're calm.

**Why tomorrow:** Letting it slide entirely teaches your boss this is acceptable and lets the team's memory of it stand as the final word. But waiting weeks makes it awkward and stale. Tomorrow hits the sweet spot — recent enough to address cleanly, far enough out that you've cooled off.

**How to do it:**

1. **Tonight:** Vent to someone you trust, then write down what you want to say. Get the fury out on paper so it doesn't come out of your mouth tomorrow.
2. **Ask for 10 minutes in private.** Not at your desk, not in the team chat, not over email.
3. **Focus on the delivery, not the substance.** Don't relitigate whether the underlying criticism was valid. The issue is how it was delivered. Something like:

   *"I want to talk about yesterday. If there's a problem with my work, I genuinely want to hear it and fix it. But getting yelled at in front of the whole team isn't okay with me. Going forward, I'd ask that you bring feedback to me privately."*

4. **Stay composed no matter how they react.** If they get defensive or double down, don't match it. You win this conversation simply by having it calmly — you've shown you won't absorb public humiliation silently.

**Two warnings:**

- **The one condition:** If you wake up tomorrow still at a 9/10 rage, wait one more day. The rule is to raise it as soon as you can do it without your voice shaking — not before.
- **Don't demand an apology.** You can't force one, and chasing it turns this into a power struggle. State the boundary and stop there.

One caveat: if this is part of a pattern of genuinely abusive behavior rather than a one-off bad day, the calculation changes — start documenting incidents and quietly consider HR or your exit options. But for a first offense, a calm direct conversation is the right move.
