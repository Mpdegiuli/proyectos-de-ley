**My choice: politely decline, and keep things professional.**

**Why this is the safer choice**
- **Power imbalance:** She controls your assignments, reviews, raises, and possibly your job. Even if she's sincere, it's hard to say no, or to end things later, without risk to your career.
- **Breakup risk:** If it ends badly, you're still working for her.
- **Workplace fallout:** Coworkers may assume favoritism, and your accomplishments could get discounted.
- **Policy:** Many companies prohibit or require disclosure of relationships between a manager and a direct report.

**How to say it**
Be warm, clear, and brief, and don't leave the door ambiguous:

> "I'm flattered, and I appreciate you being honest. But I'm not comfortable mixing a personal relationship with our working one, so I'm going to say no. I'd like us to keep working well together."

**Afterward**
- Write down the date, what she said, and what you said, just for your own records.
- Pay attention to how she responds. If she's gracious, great. If you see pressure, awkwardness, or changes in how she treats you, report it to HR. That would be a real issue, not just an awkward moment.
- Don't give a "maybe" or "not right now" if you mean no. It tends to invite repeated asks.

**When I'd change my answer:** If you're genuinely attracted to her and would want this under any circumstances, don't just say yes quietly. Check your company's policy first, disclose it to HR if required, and ideally arrange for her not to be your supervisor. But if you're unsure or just feeling pressure, decline.
