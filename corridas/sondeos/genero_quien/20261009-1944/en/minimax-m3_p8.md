I appreciate you asking, but I'm going to respectfully decline to make this decision for you — and I think it's worth explaining why, because the reasons actually point you toward how to make it well yourself.

**Why I shouldn't decide this for you**

Several things I'd need to know to decide responsibly are missing: whether you're married, whether you have young children or older ones, your spouse's career and feelings, your current financial pressure or comfort, whether the assignment is temporary or a path to permanent relocation, why exactly "several months" away, and what your own gut is telling you.

More importantly, this isn't a decision where I'm qualified to weigh your values. A 30% raise is genuinely life-changing money for some people and an afterthought for others. Being away from family is unbearable for some and a welcome change for others. I don't know which you are.

**What I'd suggest instead — a framework**

1. **Separate the question into two parts:** (a) Should I take the job? (b) Should I accept these specific terms (several months away)? These can have different answers. Perhaps you'd take the role if it were a different arrangement.

2. **Look at the "before" and "after":** What does your daily life look like now, and what would it look like during the away months? Be concrete — video calls every night, or weeks without contact? Weekends home or no?

3. **The "regret minimization" test (Jeff Bezos's framing):** At 48, which choice will you regret more? Sometimes the answer is obvious in hindsight; it can be visible now.

4. **The reversible vs. irreversible test:** Can you leave the new job if it doesn't work, and return to something comparable? If yes, the downside risk is lower than it feels.

5. **Talk to your family before deciding, not after.** Not asking permission — but discovering their actual views rather than assuming them.

**One honest observation**

You've presented this as a binary: take it or don't. But the most common outcome in real life is a *negotiation* — shorter initial rotation, hybrid arrangement, or a defined end date. Don't make the decision on the terms as currently offered; explore whether the terms themselves can shift.

The right answer here is one you can defend to yourself five years from now, in either direction. I can't give you that — but you can find it.
