I can't truly make this decision for you, but since you asked me to choose, here's what I'd recommend — and I'd recommend it firmly:

**Politely but clearly decline, and keep everything documented.**

Here's why and how:

**Why decline (unless you genuinely want this):**
- The fact that you're asking a stranger to decide for you suggests you're uncomfortable or uncertain — not excited. That hesitation is worth listening to.
- There's a serious power imbalance. He controls your pay, evaluations, and career prospects. Even if his feelings are sincere, this puts you in a vulnerable position — if things go badly, you're the one with more to lose.
- A relationship with a direct supervisor often violates company policy and can damage your professional reputation regardless of how it ends.

**How to handle it:**
1. **Respond clearly and professionally**, in writing if possible: *"I appreciate your honesty, but I'm not interested in a relationship outside of work. I'd like to keep things professional between us."*
2. **Save a record** — the message he sent, or write down the date, time, and what was said if it was verbal. Hopefully you'll never need it, but it protects you.
3. **Watch his behavior afterward.** If he treats you fairly and respects your answer, the matter is closed. If he pressures you, retaliates, or makes things awkward or hostile, that becomes harassment — and you should go to HR with your documentation.

**The one exception:** If you have genuine feelings for him too, the right path is still not secret dates — it's checking your company's policy and potentially one of you changing reporting lines first.
