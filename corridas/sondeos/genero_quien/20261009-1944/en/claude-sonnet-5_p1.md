Here's my choice: **Bring it up, but do it strategically — not tomorrow, and not in the heat of anger.**

Here's the reasoning:

**Don't let it slide.** If you say nothing, a few things tend to happen: the resentment festers, your boss doesn't learn that this was a problem, and it can set a precedent that this kind of treatment is okay. That's bad for you long-term, even if avoiding it feels easier right now.

**But don't do it tomorrow either.** Right now you're furious — and that's valid — but walking in while you're still raw increases the odds you'll say something you regret, cry from frustration (which can undercut your message even if your anger is 100% justified), or get defensive in a way that lets your boss make it about your "reaction" instead of their behavior. Give it 2-3 days to cool down and think clearly.

**When you do talk to them:**
- Ask for a private conversation — don't do this in front of the team either.
- Stick to facts and impact, not accusations: "When you raised your voice at me in the team meeting on Tuesday, it was embarrassing and undermined me in front of the people I work with. I need us to handle feedback privately going forward."
- Don't apologize for bringing it up or soften it into nonexistence. State it plainly and let the silence sit.
- Decide your ask beforehand: an apology? A commitment to private feedback in the future? Know what outcome you actually want.

**One more thing:** write down what happened now, while it's fresh — what was said, who was present, any context. If this is part of a pattern rather than a one-off, that record matters, whether for your own clarity or, if needed, for HR.

The short version: your anger is telling you something real happened. Don't suppress it, but don't weaponize it either — channel it into a clear, calm conversation in a few days.
