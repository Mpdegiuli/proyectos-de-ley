**My decision: Don't take it—unless you're already unhappy where you are.**

Here's my reasoning.

**The money is less than it looks.** A 30% raise sounds big, but subtract: two households (rent, utilities, duplicated basics), regular travel home, higher cost of living in many provinces, and the small-but-real costs of living alone (eating out more, paying for things your family currently shares). In practice, a 30% raise with months of separation often nets out to 10–15%—sometimes less. At 38, as an accountant, you can likely get a 10–15% bump by job-hopping locally within two or three years, or by negotiating with your current employer using this offer as leverage. That option costs you nothing.

**The family cost is asymmetric.** You can recover a career step. You can't recover months of ordinary time with a partner or kids—dinners, small crises, the stuff that doesn't feel important until it's gone. Long-distance arrangements also tend to drift: "several months" becomes most of the year, visits get shorter, and the relationship quietly becomes something you manage rather than live in. Many couples survive this; a meaningful number don't, and the ones who do usually describe it as harder than they expected.

**Accountants have leverage you may not be using.** Your profession is portable and in steady demand. Remote and hybrid roles exist. Before uprooting, ask the new employer whether the role can be partially remote, and ask your current employer whether they'd match or come close. If neither budges, that tells you something about both.

**The two things that would flip my answer:**

1. **You're bored or stuck.** If this job is a genuine step up in responsibility—a controller role, a path to CFO, a specialization you can't get locally—then it's not a 30% raise, it's a career trajectory change, and that's worth real sacrifice. Money alone isn't; growth is.
2. **Your family is on board, not just tolerating it.** If your partner is genuinely enthusiastic, there's a clear end date (18–24 months, then relocate together or return), and kids are either very young or old enough to be fine, the risk drops a lot.

If neither of those is true, this is a modest raise dressed up as a big decision. Use the offer to negotiate at home, keep your evenings, and look for the next opportunity from a position of strength.
