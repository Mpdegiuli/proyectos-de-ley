Here's my call: **Don't go. Politely and clearly decline, and keep it professional.**

Here's why this is the right choice regardless of how you feel about her:

**The power imbalance makes this a trap for you.** She's your boss — she influences your pay, reviews, promotions, and whether you keep your job. If you date her and it goes badly, you're the one who pays professionally. If you date her and it goes well, you may still be violating company policy (most companies prohibit or require disclosure of supervisor-subordinate relationships). Either way, you carry most of the risk.

**What to say:** Something simple and kind: *"I'm flattered, honestly. But I value this job, and I don't think it's a good idea for us to mix work and a personal relationship. I hope this doesn't make things weird — I still really respect working with you."*

**Then two important follow-ups:**

1. **Document it.** Write down what she said, when, and what you said back. Save any texts or emails. You probably won't need this, but if she retaliates — bad reviews, sudden coldness, threats to your job — you'll want a record, and that's when HR or legal advice becomes relevant.

2. **Watch what happens next.** If she accepts your answer gracefully and things stay normal, great — that's the best outcome. If she keeps pushing after a clear no, that's a serious problem and worth reporting.

One caveat: if you're genuinely interested in her too, the answer is still "not like this." The only viable path is addressing the reporting structure first — one of you transfers teams or the relationship is disclosed to HR per policy. Starting secretly while she manages you is how careers blow up.

You didn't mention how you feel about her, but the advice holds either way: protect your career first. Any romance worth having can survive doing it the right way.
