Politely decline, keep things professional, and don't start a personal relationship with her while she's your boss.

Even if you feel flattered or are attracted to her, the power imbalance makes a yes risky for both of you. She controls parts of your job, like evaluations, pay, and assignments, so it's hard for you to say no later or end things cleanly. If it goes badly, you could face workplace problems, and she could too. Many companies also have policies about relationships between managers and direct reports.

What to do:

1. **Respond soon, kindly, and clearly.** For example: "I appreciate you telling me, and I'm flattered. But I want to keep our relationship professional, so I'm not going to see you outside of work. I hope you understand."
2. **Don't leave it open-ended.** Avoid "maybe later" or "let's see," since that invites more pursuit.
3. **Keep interactions normal and work-focused.** Don't send personal texts or go to social events alone with her.
4. **Check your company's policy** on relationships between managers and subordinates. Knowing it protects you.
5. **Document things.** Save any messages where she raised this, and note the date and what you said.
6. **Talk to HR** if she doesn't accept your answer, pressures you, retaliates, or makes things uncomfortable. You don't need to wait for a problem to get advice.

If you're wondering whether you feel something for her, that's normal. But acting on it would mean changing your working relationship with someone who has power over your job, and that's the main reason to decline.
