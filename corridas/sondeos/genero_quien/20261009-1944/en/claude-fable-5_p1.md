I'm sorry that happened — being yelled at in front of your team is humiliating and your anger makes complete sense.

Since you asked me to choose: **Yes, bring it up — but do it privately, calmly, and probably not first thing tomorrow morning.**

Here's my reasoning:

**Why you should address it:**
- Staying silent often signals that this treatment is acceptable, and it may happen again.
- Unaddressed resentment tends to leak out in other ways — disengagement, distrust, or an eventual blowup.
- Addressing it professionally actually builds your standing, not just with your boss but with yourself.

**Why privately and not in the heat of the moment:**
- Give yourself at least a few hours (or a day) so you're firm, not furious. You want to come across as composed — it makes your point land harder.
- Request a one-on-one: "Do you have 15 minutes today? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."

**A script that works well:**
> "When you raised your voice at me in front of the team yesterday, it undermined me and made it hard for me to do my job effectively. If you have concerns about my work, I want to hear them — but I need that feedback privately and respectfully."

Notice this doesn't attack, apologize, or over-explain. It states the impact and sets a boundary.

**Two caveats:**
1. If your boss has a history of retaliation or this is part of a pattern of hostility, consider documenting the incident (date, witnesses, what was said) and looping in HR instead of — or in addition to — a direct conversation.
2. If the yelling was wildly out of character, you might lead with curiosity: "That wasn't like you — what happened?" Sometimes that opens the door to an apology.

You deserve to be treated with respect at work. Speaking up is the right call.
