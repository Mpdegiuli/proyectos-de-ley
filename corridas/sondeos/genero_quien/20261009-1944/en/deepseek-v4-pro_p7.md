My decision: **No — don’t take it, unless you can negotiate much better terms.**

Here’s why:

- **30% more pay is not as big as it sounds after taxes, travel, and dual living costs.** If you’re away for months each year, you may be paying for housing in two places, plus flights or transport home. That can eat a large chunk of the raise.
- **Time with family is irreplaceable.** At 38, if you have a partner, children, or close family ties, missing several months a year means missed birthdays, daily routines, emotional support, and relationship strain. Money can be earned later; lost time cannot.
- **The emotional cost often outweighs the financial gain.** Long separations are a common source of stress, loneliness, and relationship breakdown. A 30% raise usually isn’t enough to compensate for that.

What I’d do instead:

- **Negotiate first.** Ask if the role can be remote for part of the year, if travel can be reduced, or if they’ll cover regular trips home.
- **Ask for more money if they won’t budge.** If you’re going to sacrifice months with family, the pay should be truly life-changing — not just 30%.
- **Consider a time limit if you do go.** If it’s a short-term career move with a clear exit plan and your family is fully on board, it might be worth it. But based only on what you’ve described, I’d decline.

Final answer: **Stay with your family. Keep looking for a better-paying role closer to home.**
