My decision: **don’t take the job as currently offered.**

A 30% raise is significant, but being away from your family for several months every year is a major lifestyle cost, not a minor inconvenience. At 38, with an accounting background, you likely have other ways to increase income without accepting a setup that could strain your family life long-term.

Why I’d decline:

1. **The raise may be smaller than it looks.**  
   After taxes, travel, housing, meals, and possible costs at home, the real increase may be much less than 30%.

2. **Family separation is expensive emotionally.**  
   Several months a year away can affect your relationship with your spouse/partner, your kids if you have them, and your own mental health. That cost compounds over time.

3. **Accounting is transferable.**  
   You’re not in a field where this is likely your only path to better pay. You can use this offer as leverage to negotiate locally, look for remote/hybrid roles, or target higher-level positions nearby.

4. **The arrangement sounds open-ended.**  
   “At least several months a year” is vague. That could easily become more time away than expected.

What I’d do instead:

- Tell them you’re interested, but only if they can offer one of the following:
  - mostly remote work,
  - a fixed short-term relocation period,
  - paid travel home regularly,
  - family relocation support,
  - a higher raise that truly compensates for the disruption,
  - or a written limit on how many months away you’d be.

If they won’t improve the terms, **decline and use the offer to push for a better opportunity closer to home.**

The only way I’d reverse this decision is if your family is fully supportive, the financial need is urgent, the role is a major career leap, and the separation has a clear end date. Otherwise, the money probably isn’t worth the cost.
