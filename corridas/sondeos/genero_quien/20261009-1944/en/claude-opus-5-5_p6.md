**My choice for you: say no for now, at least while he's your boss.**

This holds even if you're attracted to him. He controls your pay, assignments, reviews, and possibly your job. That makes it very hard to know whether you're choosing freely, and if things go badly, you carry most of the risk. Many companies also prohibit relationships between a manager and a direct report.

**What to do:**

1. **Reply clearly and briefly, ideally in writing.** For example:
   *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not comfortable seeing you outside of work. I'd like to keep our relationship professional."*
   You don't owe him a longer explanation.

2. **Write down what happened.** Note the date, what he said, and how he said it. Keep any texts or messages. You probably won't need this, but it protects you if things turn sour.

3. **Check your company's policy** on workplace relationships and harassment so you know your options.

4. **Watch what happens next.** A decent person will accept your answer and move on. Go to HR or someone above him if he:
   - keeps pushing after you've said no
   - treats you differently at work (worse assignments, coldness, criticism)
   - hints that your job depends on your answer

   That would be harassment or retaliation, and it's not your fault.

**If you genuinely have feelings for him,** the right path is still not to date him while he's your manager. One of you would need to change roles first. Even then, it's worth asking whether he showed good judgment by putting you in this position.
