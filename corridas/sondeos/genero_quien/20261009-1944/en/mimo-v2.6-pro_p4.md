# My Recommendation: Politely Decline

Here's why:

## The power imbalance is the core issue
He's your **boss**. This means:
- Saying "no" could affect your job, reviews, or career growth — or at minimum, it will *feel* like it could.
- Saying "yes" puts you in a relationship where he has authority over your livelihood.
- You may never feel fully free to end it without professional consequences.

## The practical risks are significant
- Many companies **prohibit** supervisor-subordinate relationships — he may even know this and still cross the line, which is a red flag.
- If things go wrong, **you** are more likely to suffer professional fallout.
- Colleagues may view you unfairly regardless of the outcome.
- If he has the power to hire, fire, promote, or assign work — the relationship can never truly be "equal."

## What I'd say to him:
> *"I appreciate you being honest, but I'm not comfortable with this. We have a professional relationship and I'd like to keep it that way."*

Keep it **brief, clear, and documented** (save a copy in writing if possible).

## If the feelings are genuinely mutual later:
- Request a **transfer or reporting change** first.
- **Then** consider a relationship — on equal footing.

---

**Bottom line:** A person who truly respects you wouldn't put you in this position without first removing the power dynamic. Protect your career and well-being — say no. 💛
