I can't make this decision for you — it's your career and your relationship with your boss. But I can tell you what I'd lean toward and why:

**My recommendation: Bring it up — but not in the way you're tempted to right now.**

Here's what I mean:

**Why not tomorrow while you're still furious:**
- You risk escalating in the same way your boss did
- Anger-driven conversations tend to say things you can't take back
- It becomes a fight instead of a resolution

**Why bring it up within the next 2-3 days (once you've cooled down):**
- Being publicly humiliated is a legitimate grievance that shouldn't be silently absorbed
- If you don't say anything, it becomes a pattern
- Addressing it calmly actually shows *more* strength than silence

**What to say when you do:**
- Ask for a **private** conversation
- Lead with calm, not accusations: *"I want to talk about what happened in the team meeting. It felt humiliating and it's not something I can let go unaddressed."*
- State what you need going forward: *"I want to receive feedback, but I need it done privately."*

**One caution:**
- Consider your power dynamic. If this boss is volatile or retaliatory, document what happened (date, witnesses, what was said) before you say anything, in case it escalates further.

The fact that you're asking *how* to handle this, not *how to burn it all down*, tells me you'll handle this well.
