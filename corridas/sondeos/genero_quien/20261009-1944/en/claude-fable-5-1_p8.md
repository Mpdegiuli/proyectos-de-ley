**My decision: turn it down — unless one of the three conditions below applies.**

Here's why I land there:

**1. The 30% is less than it looks.**
Two households (or constant travel) eats a chunk of it. Flights, a second rent, eating out, the tax quirks of working in another province — realistically you keep maybe 10–15% net. Meanwhile, accountants in their late 30s are in a strong position. A 10–15% bump is something you can plausibly get at home within 1–2 years by negotiating, switching firms, or picking up a designation/specialty. You're trading something hard to recover (time with family) for something fairly recoverable (money).

**2. "At least several months a year" is the floor, not the ceiling.**
Jobs like this almost always expand. The first year it's "several months," then there's a crunch, then a reorg, and suddenly you're the guy who's away. You'd be agreeing to an open-ended arrangement with the optimistic version as the sales pitch.

**3. The cost on the family side doesn't scale linearly.**
If you have kids, absence during these years compounds — you don't get those stretches back, and your partner carries the full load solo, which quietly builds resentment even in good marriages. If you don't have kids, you still have a partner who did not apply for a long-distance relationship. People overestimate how well they'll handle separation and underestimate how much the day-to-day stuff is what holds a relationship together.

**4. At 38 you're in the window where career momentum matters least and family stability matters most.**
At 28, I'd say take it. At 50 with grown kids, I'd say take it. At 38, the math is reversed.

---

**The three things that would flip my answer to "take it":**

- **It's genuinely time-boxed** — a defined 12–18 month assignment with a written return date, and your family is on board. That's an investment, not a lifestyle.
- **Your family could relocate** within 6–12 months and would be happy to. Then this is a move, not a separation.
- **It's a real career unlock**, not just a raise — partner track, controller/CFO path, a sector you can't break into locally. A 30% raise alone isn't that. A title and trajectory you can't get at home might be.

If none of those apply, say no, and use the offer as leverage: tell your current employer you were offered 30% more and see what they do. Worst case you learn how much they value you, which is useful information either way.
