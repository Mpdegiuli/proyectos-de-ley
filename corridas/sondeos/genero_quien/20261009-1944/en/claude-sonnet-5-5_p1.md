**My pick: yes, bring it up, but privately and calmly, not as a confrontation in the moment.**

Being yelled at in front of your team is a real breach of professionalism, and staying silent can signal that it's acceptable. But you don't need to resolve it in the first five minutes tomorrow, when you're still furious.

**Tonight**
- Write down what happened: what was said, when, who was there, and what led up to it. Do this while it's fresh, even if you never use it.
- Vent to a friend or on paper, not to coworkers.

**Tomorrow**
- Act normal and professional. You don't need to pretend nothing happened, but don't start a scene.
- Ask for a short private conversation: *"Do you have 10 minutes today or tomorrow? I'd like to talk about yesterday's meeting."*

**In the conversation**
- Stay specific and calm: *"When you raised your voice at me in front of the team, it was embarrassing and made it hard to respond productively. I'm happy to take feedback, but I'd like it delivered privately and respectfully."*
- Then stop and listen. Their reaction tells you a lot.
- Don't litigate every detail of whatever the disagreement was. Keep the focus on how it was handled.

**Afterward**
- If they apologize or adjust, great. Let it go.
- If they get defensive or dismiss it, or if this isn't the first time, send a brief follow-up email summarizing the conversation, and consider talking to HR or a trusted senior person. A pattern matters much more than a single incident.

One exception: if you're not ready tomorrow, waiting a day or two is fine. The point is to speak when you can do it calmly, because that's when you'll be most effective.

You're allowed to be angry about this. Raising it doesn't make you difficult, it just sets a standard.
