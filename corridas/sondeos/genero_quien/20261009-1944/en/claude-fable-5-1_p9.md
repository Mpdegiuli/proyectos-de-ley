**My call: Don't take it as offered.**

Here's why:

**The money isn't as big as it looks.** A 30% raise is real, but after taxes, the costs of maintaining two households (or constant travel), and the things you'll spend money on to compensate for being gone (flights home, childcare your partner now has to cover alone, eating out), the net gain is often closer to 10–15%. That's a good raise, not a life-changing one.

**The cost is the kind you can't buy back.** "Several months a year" away is not a sabbatical, it's a structural change to your family life. At 38, if you have kids, you're in the years they'll actually remember. If you have a partner, you're asking them to effectively solo-parent or solo-run the household for a third of the year. People who take these arrangements tend to underestimate how much the distance erodes things, because the erosion is slow and the paycheck is immediate.

**You're in accounting, not a niche field.** You don't need to move provinces to get a 30% bump. Accountants with your experience can usually find that through a job change locally, a promotion, a designation upgrade, or a shift into industry/advisory. The scarcity you're feeling is probably about *this* offer, not about your options.

**The tell is in how you asked.** You didn't say "I'm excited about this role." You said "I'd have to be away from my family." You already know which side you're on; you're looking for permission to say no to money. Here it is.

**What to do instead:**
1. Go back to them and ask if the role can be done remotely with periodic travel (e.g., one week a month). Many "must be on site" requirements are softer than they first appear.
2. If they won't flex, take this offer to your current employer or start a local search. A competing offer is the strongest leverage you'll ever have.
3. If neither works, let it go. Another 30% will come along. The years won't.

The one thing that would flip my answer: if your family situation is such that the absence is genuinely low-cost (e.g., no kids, partner travels too, or you're already apart a lot), or if the money solves a specific urgent problem like debt that's crushing you. If that's your situation, take it and set a hard end date — two years, then home.
