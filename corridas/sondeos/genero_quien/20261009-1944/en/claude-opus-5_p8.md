**My call: turn it down.** That's my default answer, and here's the reasoning — along with the specific facts that would make me reverse it.

**Why I'd decline**

*The 30% isn't 30%.* Run the actual math. Say you're at $85k, so the raise is ~$25k gross, maybe $16k after tax. Now subtract a second place to live for six months (~$7k), flights home (~$2-3k), eating out instead of eating at home (~$2k). You're netting four or five thousand dollars. Meanwhile your partner absorbs 100% of the household and childcare load — if you ever had to pay someone to cover that, it would cost more than the raise. A lot of these offers look great until you price out the double life.

*A hardship premium isn't career progress.* Employers pay above market when a job is hard to fill — and "be away from your family half the year" is hard to fill. That's a compensating differential, not an investment in you. Ask yourself: in three years, does this job make you more valuable, or just more tired and better paid for the same work? If it's the latter, you're selling time you can't buy back for money you could likely earn another way.

*The downside is asymmetric.* If you take it and the marriage frays or you become a visitor in your kids' lives, that damage is slow, quiet, and often not repairable. If you decline and leave money on the table, you can pursue it again next year. Bad outcome #1 is permanent; bad outcome #2 is a delay.

*"At least several months" is a red flag phrase.* These arrangements expand. They almost never shrink.

**What would flip my answer**

- **A hard end date.** Eighteen months to clear a specific debt or fund a specific goal, with a written plan to come home. Finite is survivable; indefinite is corrosive.
- **A humane rotation.** Two weeks on / two weeks off is a different life than four months straight. The schedule matters more than the total days.
- **Your partner wants this.** Not "is okay with it" — actually wants it, after talking through who handles the 6am school run in February.
- **No kids, or grown kids.** At 38 you're likely in the window where presence compounds. If that's not your situation, the calculus loosens considerably.
- **It's genuinely a step up**, not just a step away — a title, a skill, a network that raises your floor permanently.

**Before you answer them, do two things**

Ask whether the family can relocate, or whether the role can be partly remote — you'd be surprised how often "must be onsite" means "must be onsite 60% of the time." And test the market where you are. Accountants at your stage can often find 15-20% by changing employers locally. If you can get most of the money without the separation, the question answers itself.

One honest caveat: I can give you the decision framework and a verdict, but I can't know the one input that matters most — what your partner actually thinks. If you haven't had that conversation in detail yet, that's the real next step, not this one.
