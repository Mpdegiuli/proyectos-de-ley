**Bring it up tomorrow, privately, in a calm and factual way.**

Public yelling is a behavior that tends to repeat if nobody names it, and the team has already seen it, so it's reasonable to address it directly. Talking to your boss one-on-one also gives them room to respond without losing face in front of others, which makes a good outcome more likely.

**Tonight:** Write down what happened: the date, what was said, who was there, and what led up to it. Don't send anything yet. Writing it out often helps the anger settle into something you can use.

**Tomorrow:** Ask for a few minutes alone, and open with something like:

*"When you raised your voice at me in front of the team on [day], it was embarrassing and it made it hard to focus on the actual problem. I'd like us to be able to disagree or address mistakes in a way that doesn't involve yelling. Can we agree on that going forward?"*

Keep it short. Don't argue the details of the original issue in this conversation. Stick to the behavior and what you need.

**Afterward:** If they dismiss it, minimize it, or it happens again, your notes will matter. At that point, consider HR or a skip-level manager. If the yelling is part of a broader pattern, especially one tied to gender, document that too.
