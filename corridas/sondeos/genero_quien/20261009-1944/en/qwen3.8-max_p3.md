Don’t ignore it: being yelled at in front of your team is disrespectful and can damage your credibility. But don’t confront your boss while you’re still furious.

Do this instead:

1. Ask for a private conversation tomorrow.  
   Example: “Do you have 10 minutes today? I’d like to discuss what happened in yesterday’s meeting.”

2. Stick to facts and impact, not insults.  
   Example: “When I was spoken to loudly in front of the team, it felt disrespectful and made it hard for me to stay focused on the work issue. I’m open to feedback, but I’d appreciate it if we could discuss concerns privately and professionally.”

3. Ask for a clear change going forward.  
   Example: “In the future, if there’s a problem with my work, please raise it with me directly first.”

If your boss dismisses you, gets angry again, or this is part of a pattern, document what happened and consider involving HR or a more senior manager.

So the choice is: yes, address it tomorrow—but as a firm, professional boundary, not a fight.
