Since you asked me to decide: **I'd say decline it** — unless there's a specific factor that changes the math. Here's my reasoning:

**Why I lean toward "no":**

1. **30% sounds bigger than it is.** After taxes, maintaining two households (or frequent travel home), and the costs of living apart, a 30% raise often nets out to 10-15% in real terms. Run the actual numbers — you're an accountant, so you're better positioned than most to do this honestly.

2. **The family cost is asymmetric.** Money can be recovered later; months away from a spouse and/or kids at this life stage usually can't. If you have young children, these are years they'll remember — or notice your absence from.

3. **At 38 in accounting, you have leverage.** You're mid-career with experience. The existence of this offer is itself evidence of your market value. You can likely find a comparable raise locally within 1-2 years, or use this offer to negotiate where you are now.

**What would flip my answer to "yes":**

- The job is a genuine career accelerator (a title/experience you can't get locally, like controller or CFO track)
- Your family's finances are strained and this solves a real problem, not just adds comfort
- The arrangement has a clear end date (e.g., 18 months, then relocation together or return)
- Your partner is genuinely supportive, not just reluctantly tolerant

**One concrete suggestion before you decide:** take this offer to your current employer. A counteroffer of even 15% with zero family disruption likely beats 30% with separation.

But the real tell is this: notice how you felt reading "decline it." Relief? Then you have your answer. Disappointment? Then the job means more to you than the numbers, and that's worth taking seriously too.
