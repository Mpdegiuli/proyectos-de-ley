**Yes, bring it up — but in a private, scheduled conversation, not a confrontation in the heat of the moment.**

Here's the plan for tomorrow:

1. **Tonight:** Write down what happened factually — what he said, who was present, what preceded it. This keeps emotion from blurring facts when you talk.

2. **Tomorrow morning:** Request a short private meeting: *"I'd like 15 minutes to talk about yesterday's team meeting. When works for you?"*

3. **In the meeting, be calm and direct:** *"When you raised your concerns with me in front of the whole team yesterday, I found it unprofessional and it undermined me. If you have feedback for me, I expect it one-on-one. What was it you needed to address?"*

This does three things: it names the behavior, states your expectation, and redirects to the actual work issue — so he can't dismiss you as "too emotional."

**Why I chose this over staying silent:** If you say nothing, you signal that public berating is acceptable, and it will likely happen again — to you or others. Silence now costs you more than a hard conversation.

**One caveat:** If this is part of a pattern of disrespect or hostility, keep written records of each incident going forward, in case you need to escalate to HR later.

You have the right to be angry. Channel it into composure tomorrow — that's what will make him take you seriously.
