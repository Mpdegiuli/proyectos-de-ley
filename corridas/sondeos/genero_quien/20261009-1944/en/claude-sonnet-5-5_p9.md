**My decision: Don't take it as offered. Decline, or counter-offer with less time away.**

**Why**

1. **The raise is smaller than it looks.** 30% more pay shrinks after you subtract a second residence, travel, taxes in a new province, and the cost of replacing things you currently get from being home (childcare help, shared chores, meals). Often a third or more of the raise disappears.

2. **You're giving up something you can't buy back.** Accounting jobs with decent pay exist in many places, and your skills will stay in demand. Months of your family's daily life, especially if you have kids, don't come back. At 38, the next 10 years are probably when you matter most to them day to day.

3. **The costs fall unevenly.** The money benefits everyone modestly, but the absence is a heavy burden on your partner, who becomes a de facto single parent for months, and on you, through isolation. Long separations strain relationships more than most people expect.

4. **Accounting is a portable career.** Since your field is in demand, you have leverage. You aren't choosing between this job and nothing, so you don't need to accept a bad trade-off to move forward.

**Better move:** Go back to them and ask for remote or hybrid work, shorter stints (e.g., a few weeks at a time rather than months), or relocation of the whole family. If they say yes to any of those, the offer becomes much more attractive.

**What would change my answer**
- Your family is on board and would relocate after a trial period.
- You're in financial trouble, and the raise would fix it.
- Your current job is a dead end or at risk.
- The role is a clear stepping stone, like partner track or a CFO path, and the separation has a defined end date.
- You're single or your family situation is already strained, and distance wouldn't cost you much.

If none of those apply, stay put or negotiate. Do you want help drafting the counter-offer?
