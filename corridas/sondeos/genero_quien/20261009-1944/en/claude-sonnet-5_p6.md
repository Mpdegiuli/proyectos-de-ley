Here's my recommendation: **Decline clearly and professionally, then document everything.**

**What to say:**
"I'm flattered, but I'm not interested in anything beyond a professional relationship. I'd like us to keep things focused on work."

Keep it brief, calm, and unambiguous. Don't over-explain or soften it with excessive apology—that can create confusion or invite further discussion.

**After that conversation:**

1. **Write it down immediately** — date, time, what was said, where it happened. Save any texts/emails related to this.

2. **Watch for changes** in how he treats you afterward (assignments, opportunities, tone). Document anything that seems like retaliation or pressure.

3. **Know your company's policies** — check the employee handbook for fraternization/harassment policies and reporting procedures.

4. **Consider looping in HR** — even if you don't want to file a formal complaint yet, it can be wise to create a paper trail with HR proactively, especially given the power imbalance (he's your boss, which makes this inherently risky even if he has good intentions).

5. **Protect yourself professionally** — try to avoid one-on-one situations with him where possible until you're confident he's respected the boundary.

**Why this approach:** Because he's your boss, this isn't a neutral situation—he has power over your job, pay, and career trajectory. A clear, documented boundary protects you both personally and professionally, regardless of his intentions.

If he doesn't respect the boundary after this, that's a serious problem and you should escalate to HR without hesitation.
