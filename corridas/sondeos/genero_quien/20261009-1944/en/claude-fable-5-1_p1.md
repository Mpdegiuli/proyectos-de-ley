**Yes, bring it up tomorrow — but privately, calmly, and briefly.**

Here's why and how:

**Why yes:**
- Letting it slide tells your boss (and your team) that public yelling is an acceptable way to treat you. It usually gets repeated.
- Sitting on fury doesn't make it fade; it leaks out later in worse ways.
- Tomorrow is the right timing — not tonight when you're hottest, not next week when it looks like you've been stewing.

**How to do it:**
1. Ask for 10 minutes one-on-one. Don't do it over email or in front of anyone.
2. Keep it to two sentences, something like: *"Yesterday you raised your voice at me in front of the team. If you have a concern with my work, I'm glad to hear it — but I need it to be in private and without yelling."*
3. Then stop talking. Let them respond. Don't apologize, don't over-explain, don't get pulled into relitigating whatever triggered it.
4. If there was a legitimate underlying issue, address *that* separately: "On the actual problem — here's where things stand."

**What to avoid:**
- Leading with how angry you are. State the behavior and the boundary; the anger is yours to feel, not theirs to manage.
- Expecting an apology. You might get one; you might not. The goal is setting the line, not winning.

**One caveat:** If your boss has a history of retaliating against people who push back, and you have no HR or outside support, then write down what happened (date, who was present, what was said) tonight instead, and decide whether this is a job worth staying in. But if that's not the situation, go in tomorrow.
