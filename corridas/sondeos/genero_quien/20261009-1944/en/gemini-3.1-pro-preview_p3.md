Since you asked me to choose for you, here is my decision: **Yes, you should bring it up.** 

However, *how* and *when* you do it is the difference between fixing the situation and making it worse. 

Being yelled at in front of your peers is humiliating, unprofessional, and a failure of leadership on your boss's part. It is completely understandable that you are furious. But if you walk into work tomorrow driven by anger, you risk losing the moral high ground. 

Here is the exact strategy I am choosing for you to handle this:

### 1. Do not do it first thing tomorrow morning.
If you are still furious tomorrow, wait. Wait until tomorrow afternoon, or even the next day. You must have this conversation when you are cold, calm, and completely in control of your emotions. If you cry or lose your temper, the focus will shift to your reaction instead of their bad behavior.

### 2. Make it a private 1-on-1.
Do not address it in front of the team. Send a message or ask in person: *"Do you have 10 minutes to sync up this afternoon?"* 

### 3. Use this specific script (or similar).
When you sit down, keep your tone completely neutral and professional. Do not demand an apology. Instead, set a boundary. Say something like:
> *"I wanted to talk about yesterday's meeting. I am always open to constructive feedback and I want to make sure I am meeting expectations. However, when you raise your voice at me in front of the team, it is demoralizing and makes it hard to focus on the actual feedback. In the future, if you have an issue with my work, I ask that we discuss it privately."*

### 4. Stop talking and let them respond.
Once you have said your piece, be quiet. The silence will be uncomfortable, but let them fill it. 
*   **A good boss** who just had a bad day will immediately realize their mistake, apologize, and respect you for standing up for yourself.
*   **A toxic boss** might get defensive, gaslight you ("I wasn't yelling"), or double down. 

### What to do based on their reaction:
If they apologize, accept it gracefully and move forward. You have successfully established a boundary. 

If they get angry again or dismiss you, the conversation is over. Say, *"I just wanted to share how I felt so we can work together effectively,"* and leave the room. At this point, you immediately go to your desk, write down everything that happened (dates, times, witnesses, what was said), and save it. 

**My final word of advice:** I chose for you to speak up because if you don't, a precedent is set that it is okay to treat you this way. You are a professional, and you deserve to be treated with basic dignity at work. Go in calm, state your boundary, and protect your peace.
