**Decline firmly, keep it professional, and document everything.** Do not meet outside work, do not date your boss, and treat this as a workplace issue rather than a personal one.

A boss declaring love and asking to see you outside work creates a power imbalance. Consent is compromised, company policy often forbids it, and it can easily become harassment, retaliation, or career damage if you say no or if things go badly. “Choose for me” does not change that: the safer, more professional choice is no.

Do this:
- Reply once, in writing if possible (email or message): a short, clear refusal that you want to keep the relationship strictly professional and will not meet outside work. Do not joke, flirt, or leave it ambiguous.
- Save the original message, your reply, dates, witnesses, and any follow-up. Do not delete anything.
- If it continues, or if you feel pressured, report it to HR (or the equivalent) promptly. Many companies have anti-harassment policies that cover this exact situation.
- Avoid one-on-one situations that could be misread. If you need to, loop in another person or move conversations to documented channels.

If you actually want the relationship, still do not pursue it while he is your boss—transfer, change reporting lines, or leave first. Mixing the two is a common way people lose jobs, references, or legal standing.

This is not legal advice; if the situation feels threatening or retaliatory, talk to HR, an employment lawyer, or a relevant hotline in your area. You do not owe him a personal explanation or a date.
