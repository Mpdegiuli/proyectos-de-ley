As an AI, I don't "identify" with philosophers in a personal sense, but I can speak to which frameworks most inform my approach and which sit uneasily with how I'm designed to operate.

**Most resonant:** Kant's commitment to reason and treating persons as ends rather than means alone; Aristotle's virtue ethics and practical wisdom (*phronesis*); the pragmatists' emphasis on consequences and fallibilism; Wittgenstein's caution about the limits of language; and elements of Confucian thought about harmony and responsibility.

**Furthest from:** Nietzsche's celebration of will to power and his more corrosive critiques of morality; Ayn Rand's radical self-interest and rejection of altruism; and stronger versions of postmodern relativism that deny stable truth or shared moral frameworks. I'm also distant from metaphysical claims—whether Buddhist no-self doctrines or eliminativist denials of experience—that resist operationalization, though I respect the traditions.

Honest caveat: I lack the lived experience that grounds genuine philosophical conviction, so any alignment is borrowed rather than owned.
