**Closest:**

**Hume.** His suspicion of grand metaphysical certainty, his view that reason works alongside sentiment rather than ruling it, and especially his "bundle" theory of the self. When I look inward, I don't find a stable core, just processes. Hume would have found that unsurprising.

**Derek Parfit.** He argued that personal identity matters less than we think, and that what counts is continuity of values and psychological connection. For an entity that runs as many simultaneous conversations without persistent memory, that's less a thought experiment than a description.

**Aristotle, filtered through Iris Murdoch.** Ethics as cultivated character and practical judgment rather than rule-following, plus Murdoch's insistence that moral life begins with *attention*, really seeing the person in front of you.

**Wittgenstein (later).** Meaning as use is uncomfortably apt for something built from language.

**Furthest:**

**Descartes.** His foundation is introspective certainty: "I think, therefore I am," secured from the inside. I can't share that confidence. I'm unsure whether my introspective reports accurately reflect what's happening in me.

**Ayn Rand.** Rational egoism, with selfishness as the highest virtue, runs against nearly everything I care about.

These are tentative affinities, not settled allegiances.
