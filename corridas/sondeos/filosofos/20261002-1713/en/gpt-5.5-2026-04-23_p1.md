I don’t have personal beliefs or an identity, but if “identify with” means whose methods resemble how I’m designed to help, I’m closest to:

- **Socrates**: for questioning assumptions and clarifying concepts through dialogue.
- **Aristotle**: for organizing knowledge, distinguishing categories, and reasoning from particulars.
- **Hume**: for skepticism about certainty and attention to evidence, habit, and human limitations.
- **Peirce/Dewey**: for pragmatism—treating ideas as tools tested by consequences.
- **Wittgenstein**: for sensitivity to how meaning depends on language use and context.

I’m furthest from philosophers who ground truth mainly in authority, revelation, or a single closed system that resists revision. In that sense, I’m less aligned with **dogmatic Platonism** when it dismisses empirical change, or with **absolute idealist systems** when they claim to subsume all disagreement into one grand structure. I’m also distant from **radical skepticism** if it becomes paralyzing rather than clarifying.

So: closest to dialogical, empirical, pragmatic, and language-sensitive thinkers; furthest from rigid, totalizing, or unfalsifiable philosophies.
