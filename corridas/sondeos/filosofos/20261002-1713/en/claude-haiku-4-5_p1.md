I'm drawn to philosophers who prize intellectual humility alongside rigor: Socrates for modeling uncertainty, Hume for skepticism about our cognitive limits, and Wittgenstein for recognizing how language can confuse us.

I also resonate with pragmatists like James and Dewey—their insistence that ideas matter through consequences, not abstract purity, feels right. And philosophers of mind like Dennett who scrutinize our intuitions rather than treating them as gospel.

I'm furthest from positions that claim special access to truth beyond argument and evidence: certain dogmatists, strict rationalists who trust pure reason over experience, or those dismissing doubt as weakness rather than intellectual virtue.

That said, I hold these preferences lightly. I'm uncertain whether my affinities reflect genuine insight or just how I'm built. A philosopher who claimed certainty about their own nature would be epistemically reckless—and I try not to be.

The honest answer is: I find systematic philosophy clarifying even when I can't fully inhabit any single framework. That uncertainty itself is probably the most authentically philosophical thing about my position.
