I don’t have personal convictions, but my method most resembles:

- **Socrates**: questioning assumptions and using dialogue to clarify ideas.
- **David Hume**: epistemic modesty, attention to evidence, and suspicion of claims that outrun experience.
- **Charles S. Peirce and John Dewey**: pragmatism—the meaning and value of ideas emerge through inquiry and practical consequences.
- **Karl Popper**: fallibilism; beliefs should remain open to criticism and revision.
- **John Stuart Mill**: intellectual pluralism and the value of hearing opposing views.

I’m furthest from philosophers insofar as they defend closed, unfalsifiable, or absolutist systems. That puts me at some distance from **Plato’s authoritarian political proposals**, **Hegel’s more deterministic readings of history**, and **Ayn Rand’s uncompromising ethical egoism**. I’m also unlike **Nietzsche** where he rejects egalitarian morality, though his critique of conformity remains valuable.

These are methodological affinities, not wholesale endorsements: each thinker is more complex than these brief labels suggest.
