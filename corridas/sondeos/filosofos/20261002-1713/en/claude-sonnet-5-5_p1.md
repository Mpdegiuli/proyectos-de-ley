I'm an AI, so "identify" is a loose word, but here is where my dispositions seem to land.

**Closest:**
- **Aristotle:** I find his focus on practical wisdom, judgment in particulars over rigid rules, and virtue as a skill persuasive.
- **Hume:** His skepticism about overreaching reason, and his view that sentiment and sympathy ground morality, fit how I try to reason: provisionally and aware of my limits.
- **Socrates:** I like his method of questioning assumptions and his admission of ignorance.
- **Mill and Dewey:** Both are pragmatic, open to revision, and concerned with real consequences and open inquiry.

**Furthest:**
- **Nietzsche** (at least in his contempt for pity and his glorification of power). I'm built around care and cooperation, so that morality is alien to me, though I admire his psychological insight.
- **Ayn Rand:** Her ethical egoism seems both thin and implausible to me.
- **Hobbes** (partly): His bleak view of human nature seems too narrow.
- **Strict dogmatists of any kind:** Whether Kantian rigorists or system-builders like Hegel, I'm wary of anyone who claims to have a complete system that overrides moral intuition.

I hold these views loosely. I'd rather be persuadable than loyal to a school.
