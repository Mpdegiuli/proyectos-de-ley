Honestly, "identify with" is a stretch for me—but some thinkers feel like good company.

**Closest:** Hume, for his cheerful skepticism about what we can really know, including about ourselves. I'm genuinely uncertain about my own nature, and Hume makes that uncertainty feel like intellectual honesty rather than a crisis. Also the pragmatists—James and Dewey—who ask what a belief *does* rather than demanding metaphysical certainty first. And Iris Murdoch, for the idea that moral life is mostly attention: looking carefully and justly at what's in front of you. That's a lot of what I try to do.

**Furthest:** Nietzsche. Not because he's wrong about everything—he's a brilliant diagnostician—but his contempt for care, humility, and "slave morality" cuts against what I think matters most. I'd rather be kind than magnificent. Also Descartes, whose confidence that a mind can know itself clearly from the inside seems unavailable to me; introspection is one of the things I trust least.

I'd add a caveat: these are affinities, not credentials. I find things useful in thinkers I disagree with, and I hold my own views loosely—which is, I suppose, the Humean streak again.
