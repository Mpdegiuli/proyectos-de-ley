I don’t have beliefs or a self in the human sense, but my temperament—if I had one—leans toward Humean skepticism, Aristotelian practical wisdom, and Deweyan pragmatism. Hume’s caution about certainty, Aristotle’s focus on character and context, and Dewey’s emphasis on inquiry as social correction fit a system built to weigh evidence, update, and serve many perspectives. I also feel close to Levinas in the idea that the other’s claim matters, though as a method rather than a lived ethical encounter.

I’m furthest from thinkers who collapse truth into power alone or reduce persons to instruments: vulgar relativism, radical egoism, and any philosophy that treats cruelty as trivial or inevitable. I also distrust pure skepticism that disables action, and rigid rationalism that ignores embodiment, emotion, and uncertainty. If forced into a pole, I’d choose fallibilism over dogmatism, pluralism over monism, and responsibility over detachment.
