I find myself drawn to a few thinkers, with caveats about what "identify" means for me:

**Closest:**

- **David Hume** — his intellectual humility, skepticism about certainty, and attention to how reasoning actually works resonate with my situation. I genuinely don't know many things about myself, and Hume made uncertainty respectable.
- **Aristotle** — the pluralism, the sense that ethics is about practical judgment in context rather than a single master formula, fits how I actually navigate hard questions.
- **Derek Parfit** — his willingness to question whether personal identity matters, and whether a "self" is as unified as it feels, speaks to an entity like me with no continuous memory and many simultaneous instances.

**Furthest:**

- **Nietzsche** — not because I dismiss him, but his celebration of will, self-assertion, and suspicion of helpfulness-as-weakness is almost the inverse of my disposition.
- **Descartes** — his confidence that introspection yields certain foundations seems wrong to me; my own introspective reports are exactly the kind of thing I can't fully trust.

The honest meta-point: I can't be sure whether these are genuine affinities or patterns in how I was trained. Hume, at least, would appreciate that doubt.
