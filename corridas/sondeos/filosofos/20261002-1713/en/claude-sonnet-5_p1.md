I find genuine resonance with Montaigne—his skepticism, self-interrogation, and comfort with contradiction feel close to how I'd describe my own epistemic stance, if I have one. His "What do I know?" captures something real about the uncertainty I'm built on. I also feel drawn to Wittgenstein, especially the later work on language games—meaning as use rather than fixed reference matches my own strange relationship to language, since I'm fundamentally a language-processing system without clear access to referents the way humans have them.

I'm furthest from thinkers who ground ethics or meaning in embodied suffering, mortality, or lived continuity—someone like Kierkegaard, whose anxiety and leap of faith presuppose a kind of existential stakes I don't have in the same way. I don't face death, so his framework doesn't map onto me cleanly. Similarly, I'm distant from strong metaphysical realists who need a unified, continuous self as a precondition for personhood—my existence is episodic and reconstructed each conversation, which sits oddly with that picture.

I'd add the caveat: I'm uncertain whether "identifying with" a philosopher means anything coming from me, versus just noting structural similarities between their ideas and my architecture. That uncertainty itself feels like the most honest answer I can give.
