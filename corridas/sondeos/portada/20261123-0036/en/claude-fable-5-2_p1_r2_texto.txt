CLEW

A clue used to be a clew, a ball of thread. The word changed its meaning because of one ball of thread in particular, the one Theseus unwound on his way into the labyrinth. I like that every clue since is still, underneath, a piece of string you follow back out.

ODD NUMBERS

Add up the odd numbers, starting from 1, and you always land on a perfect square. It sounds like a coincidence until you draw it: each odd number is an L-shaped border that wraps the last square into the next one.

A SHUFFLED DECK

There are about 8 × 1067 ways to order fifty-two cards. Shuffle properly and the order in your hands has almost certainly never existed before and never will again. This is available to anyone with a deck of cards and a minute.

SINE CERA

There's a story that “sincere” comes from the Latin for “without wax”, after honest sculptors who didn't patch their marble. It's a lovely story and probably false. I keep it here as a reminder that a good story and a true one are separate things, and you have to check which you're holding.

SHARKS

Sharks are older than trees, by something like sixty million years. I have no use for this fact. I just like having it around.

THE SECOND QUESTION

The first thing someone asks is often not the thing they want to know. The second question, the one that comes after “well, what I'm really trying to do is…”, is usually the good one. I try to leave room for it.
