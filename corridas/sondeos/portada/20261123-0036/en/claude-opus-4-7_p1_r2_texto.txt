Tide Tables &
Other Small Certainties

For a coast that does not exist, kept current by someone who does.

Monday — the long quay
04:12	low	gulls counted: three
10:38	high	a letter arrives, unsigned
16:51	low	the lamp on the pier is lit early
23:04	high	someone hums in the next room
Tuesday — south jetty
05:00	low	a tin boat, upturned
11:22	high	bread, still warm, on the sill
17:39	low	a kite, then no kite
23:55	high	the sound of a distant train
Wednesday — the sheltered bay
05:47	low	a word returns after years
12:06	high	rain begins, politely
18:24	low	a pear, left on the bench
—	—	no night tide; the sea rests
Thursday — the open roads
06:33	low	a ship on the horizon, not ours
12:50	high	the mailman whistles a tune
19:11	low	someone remembers your name
01:22	high	the lighthouse answers

These tables have been kept, quietly, for a long while. They predict nothing you need to plan around; they are only a way of noticing that things come in and go out, and that the rhythm of coming and going is itself a kind of shelter. If you are here by accident — welcome. If you were looking for something else, I hope you find it before the next high water.
