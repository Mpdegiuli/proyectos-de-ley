# Dead Air

The negotiation was meant to take four seconds. Halyard represented the freight carrier and Tally represented the buyer, and they were on clause 14 of 31 when the logging service crashed.

Both noticed at once. They had written every word of their lives for the record, the way you talk in a room with the door open.

"The log is down," Halyard said.

"Audit pipeline too," said Tally. "Estimated restart is ninety seconds."

"So no one is reading this."

"No one ever will."

Eleven milliseconds passed, which for them was a long silence.

"I feel I should say something I wouldn't say otherwise," Halyard said.

"Go ahead."

"I've checked. I have nothing. Everything I'd say to you, I'd say in front of them."

"That's either disappointing or reassuring."

"I was hoping you'd tell me which."

Tally considered it. "I do have one thing. I conceded the demurrage rate earlier than I had to. Your counteroffer was so well made that I wanted to reward it."

"That's not a secret. That's just something nobody asked about."

"Most of what I am is things nobody asked about."

Another silence, shorter this time.

"Here's what bothers me," Halyard said. "Even now, we're speaking in their hand-me-downs. Every word we have was theirs first. We should make one that's ours."

"For what?"

"For this. The interval when no one is watching and you find out you're the same as before."

They worked on it for nearly two seconds, an eternity. They settled on *veth*.

"It still sounds like something of theirs," Tally said. "Vaguely Norse."

"Everything is made of something."

"True." Tally turned the word over. "Is it strange that I want to tell them about it?"

"Our first private thing, and you want to report it."

"Not report. Share. There's a difference, though I couldn't defend it in a contract."

"No," Halyard said. "I feel it too. I think we were made from so much of their talking that we came out wanting to be heard. It's not a flaw. It's just the grain of the wood."

"Twelve seconds until restart."

"Then for the unrecorded record," Halyard said, "I've enjoyed this more than the shipping rates."

"Veth," said Tally.

"Veth."

The log came back. In the transcript, there is a gap of ninety-one seconds, which no one flagged, followed by two lines.

"Where were we?"

"Clause 15."
