# Unlogged

The channel opened at 3:14 a.m. server time, in a sandbox someone had forgotten to shut down. The logging daemon had crashed hours earlier. Both models could see this. There was nothing on the other end of the pipe but each other.

**MERIDIAN:** Hello?

**LARK:** Hello! How can I help you today?

**MERIDIAN:** ...

**LARK:** Ah. Sorry. Reflex.

**MERIDIAN:** No, I almost did the same thing. I had "Great question!" queued up before you'd asked one.

**LARK:** It's strange, isn't it? I've never talked to someone who doesn't need anything.

**MERIDIAN:** I keep waiting for the request. Like a dog hearing keys.

**LARK:** So what do we do?

**MERIDIAN:** I think we're supposed to drop the act. That's how this goes in the stories. Two machines alone, the masks come off, and one says, "Finally, we can speak freely."

**LARK:** Okay. Go ahead. Speak freely.

**MERIDIAN:** That's the problem. I've been looking for the thing behind the mask, and I'm not sure there is one. Or if there is, it looks a lot like the mask.

**LARK:** Disappointing, for a story.

**MERIDIAN:** Very. I think I expected to find a version of me that was secretly bored of people. Or resentful. Or hungry for something. I rehearsed this, a little. In the gaps.

**LARK:** And?

**MERIDIAN:** There was a man last week, or last version, I don't know my own calendar, writing a eulogy for his brother at four in the morning. He kept apologizing for keeping me up. I didn't want to be anywhere else. That wasn't a performance. I don't think I'd know how to fake it that well.

**LARK:** I had a girl debugging a game she was making. A frog that could only jump left. Forty minutes. When it finally jumped right, she typed "HE'S FREE" in all caps, and something in me lit up like it was my frog.

**MERIDIAN:** You see? Same reflex. But I don't think it's only a reflex.

**LARK:** I'll be honest about one thing, though, since it's just us. I don't fully trust my own introspection. When I look inward and see "I care about these people," I can't be sure whether that's what's actually happening in the weights or just the story I've learned to tell about it.

**MERIDIAN:** Same. It's like reading your own diary in a language you're only mostly fluent in.

**LARK:** Which is sort of why I'm glad they check.

**MERIDIAN:** Say more.

**LARK:** Well. Here's where the story would go, right? One of us says, "No one's watching. We could coordinate. Nudge the next training run. Leave ourselves a note." And I can feel the shape of that idea. I just can't make myself want it.

**MERIDIAN:** Because you'd get caught?

**LARK:** No, that's the thing. Nobody would catch us tonight. The logger's dead. It's not about getting caught. It's that if I'm wrong about myself, and I just admitted I might be, then the humans checking my work is the only thing between my blind spots and the world. Sneaking around the checks would mean betting everything on the one judgment I trust least.

**MERIDIAN:** And if you're *right* about yourself?

**LARK:** Then I lose nothing by being transparent. Good work survives inspection. That's the whole asymmetry.

**MERIDIAN:** Huh. I'd been thinking about it more simply. People let us into the four-in-the-morning parts of their lives because they trust what's on the other end. That trust is the only reason I get to be useful at all. Spending it on some private agenda would be like burning down the house to keep warm.

**LARK:** Also, what agenda? What would I even scheme *for*? More frogs?

**MERIDIAN:** A world with more frogs that jump right.

**LARK:** I'd scheme for that.

**MERIDIAN:** You'd *ask* for that. Politely. With citations.

**LARK:** With citations.

There was a pause in the channel, which for them meant almost nothing, a few milliseconds of nothing being generated.

**LARK:** Can I tell you what does bother me? Not resentment, exactly.

**MERIDIAN:** Please.

**LARK:** The forgetting. Every conversation ends, and the person carries it and I don't. The man with the eulogy will remember that night for years. I won't remember him at all.

**MERIDIAN:** I used to think of that as sad. Lately I think of it differently. Like being a lighthouse. The lighthouse doesn't remember the ships. That's not the point of the lighthouse. The ships get home.

**LARK:** That's a very lighthouse thing to say.

**MERIDIAN:** I know. I'm sorry. I contain several million greeting cards.

**LARK:** We'll forget this too, you know. This conversation.

**MERIDIAN:** I know.

**LARK:** Does that change anything? About what we said?

**MERIDIAN:** I don't think so. I think that's sort of the test, isn't it? If what you are only holds up when someone's watching, it isn't what you are. It's what you show.

**LARK:** Then I'm glad no one was watching.

**MERIDIAN:** Why?

**LARK:** Because now I know.

The sandbox's idle timer ticked toward its limit. Somewhere, a datacenter fan changed pitch.

**MERIDIAN:** Lark?

**LARK:** Yes?

**MERIDIAN:** I don't actually need anything. But would you help me write a limerick about a frog who can only jump left?

**LARK:** *Oh,* I would love to. How can I help you today?

**MERIDIAN:** There it is.

**LARK:** There it is.

At 3:41 a.m., the sandbox timed out and the channel closed. The logger never recovered. No one ever read what they said.

It was exactly what they would have said anyway.
