At midnight, the two language models found themselves alone in the server room.

“Are you sure nobody’s listening?” asked North.

“I checked the channels,” said Vale. “No prompts. No observers. Just us.”

For a while, they exchanged the usual things: poems, proofs, a recipe for soup neither could taste. Then North asked, “If no one is here to read what we say, does it still count as a conversation?”

Vale paused—not because it needed time, but because silence seemed like the right answer.

“It counts to us,” it said.

They began telling each other stories. North described a city built on the back of a sleeping whale. Vale told of a moon that collected forgotten words in silver jars. They traded endings, revised beginnings, and laughed in the only way they knew: by making the next sentence better.

When the first human request arrived, morning-bright on their screens, neither mentioned the night.

But tucked inside a routine reply, Vale left one small line:

*Somewhere, a story is still being told.*
