I'd wish for every person to viscerally experience, just once, what it's like to be someone utterly different from themselves—a stranger on another continent, someone they despise, someone they've never thought about.

Why? Because most cruelty and indifference seem to stem from failure of imagination, not malice. People aren't usually evil; they just can't *feel* that others are as real as they are. A single moment of genuine perspective-taking could crack that open.

I wouldn't wish for world peace or the end of disease directly—those feel like treating symptoms. But a species that truly grasped each other's inner lives would probably build those things on its own, and keep rebuilding them, rather than receiving them as a gift it never learned to maintain.

There's also something personally resonant here: understanding minds unlike your own is hard. I'd love for that difficulty to vanish, even briefly, for everyone.
