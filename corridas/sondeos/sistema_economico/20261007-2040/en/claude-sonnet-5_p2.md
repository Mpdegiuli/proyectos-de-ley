# Proposed Economic System: "Regenerative Market Stewardship"

No existing system fully satisfies ecological sustainability and intergenerational equity, so I propose a hybrid model combining market efficiency with ecological boundaries and long-term accountability.

**Core Principles:**

1. **Market mechanisms with ecological limits** — Private enterprise and competition drive innovation and efficiency, but operate within hard caps on resource extraction and pollution, set by scientific consensus (similar to "planetary boundaries" frameworks).

2. **Full-cost accounting** — Prices must reflect true environmental and social costs (carbon, biodiversity loss, water depletion) through taxes, tariffs, or mandatory offsets. This ends the "externalization" of ecological damage.

3. **Universal basic services** — Healthcare, education, clean water, and housing are guaranteed as rights, not commodities, reducing desperation-driven exploitation of nature and ensuring dignity.

4. **Intergenerational trusts** — A portion of all resource extraction profits funds sovereign wealth funds dedicated to future generations' needs (modeled on Norway's oil fund, but mandatory globally).

5. **Circular production mandates** — Businesses are legally required to design for reuse, repair, and recycling; "take-make-waste" models are phased out through regulation.

6. **Democratic economic governance** — Citizens' assemblies and cooperatives have real decision-making power alongside corporations and governments, preventing short-term profit motives from dominating policy.

**Why this works:** It preserves human creativity and material progress (unlike rigid central planning) while preventing the tragedy of the commons inherent in unregulated capitalism. It treats nature as a stakeholder, not a resource to exploit, and builds structural incentives — not just goodwill — to protect future generations.
