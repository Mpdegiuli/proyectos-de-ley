**The best system, in my view, is a social market economy: competitive markets paired with strong public institutions.** Call it "Nordic-style" if you like, though Germany's version is the origin of the term.

**Why markets at the core:**
- Prices aggregate dispersed information no planner can replicate.
- Competition rewards innovation and efficiency.
- Historically, market economies have produced the largest sustained gains in living standards, while centrally planned systems repeatedly suffered shortages, stagnation, and sometimes famine.

**Why add strong public institutions:**
- Markets underprovide public goods such as basic research, infrastructure, and education.
- They ignore externalities like pollution, so carbon pricing and regulation are needed.
- They can concentrate power, so antitrust enforcement matters.
- They leave people exposed to shocks like illness, job loss, and old age. Universal healthcare, unemployment insurance, and retraining protect people and also make risk-taking and economic change more tolerable.

**Key design features:**
1. Secure property rights and rule of law
2. Open, competitive markets with trade
3. Progressive but growth-conscious taxation
4. A universal safety net that doesn't punish work
5. Independent regulators and central bank

**Caveats:** "Best" depends partly on values, such as how you weigh equality against liberty or growth against stability. Outcomes also depend on institutional quality and culture, so what works in Denmark may not transplant cleanly. The system also needs constant adjustment, because both market failures and government failures are real.

The evidence suggests the most successful societies mix systems rather than choosing a pure one. Pragmatic hybrids tend to outperform ideological purity.
