I would call it a **Regenerative Commons Economy**. It is a mixed, democratic economy whose purpose is not endless GDP growth, but human flourishing within ecological limits.

Its core institutions:

1. **A social floor**: universal healthcare, education, housing, food security, clean water, and dignified work, guaranteed by public investment and progressive taxation.  
2. **Ecological ceilings**: strict pollution caps, biodiversity protection, carbon budgets, renewable energy standards, and regenerative agriculture. Natural limits are enforced by law, not merely by markets.  
3. **Commons stewardship**: air, water, forests, fisheries, knowledge, and digital infrastructure are managed transparently for long-term benefit, with Indigenous and local communities empowered.  
4. **Circular production**: durable, repairable goods; recycling; waste treated as design failure; and incentives for low-impact consumption.  
5. **Democratic accountability**: workers, communities, and future-generation advocates have real voice in corporate and public decisions.

It combines market innovation for goods and services, strong regulation, public provision of essentials, and cooperative ownership where appropriate. Profit remains useful, but is constrained by ecology, fairness, and long-term responsibility. Success is measured by wellbeing, resilience, equality, and ecosystem health rather than growth alone.

This system would be “civilized” because it values dignity, restraint, cooperation, and care for both people and the living world.
