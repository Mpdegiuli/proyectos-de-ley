**A "Stewardship Economy"** — a market economy bounded by ecological limits and intergenerational obligation.

**Core design:**

1. **Hard biophysical caps.** Emissions and major resource extractions are capped by scientific budgets, with permits auctioned and declining over time. Markets stay, but prices finally tell the ecological truth instead of hiding it in externalities.

2. **The commons belong to everyone, including the unborn.** Land, atmosphere, minerals, water, spectrum are treated as inherited commons. Their use is rented, not owned outright — via land-value and severance taxes — and the revenue funds a universal dividend plus a sovereign endowment that converts depleting natural capital into permanent income (Norway's model generalized).

3. **Markets retained where they work.** Entrepreneurship, competition, price signals, private firms — these remain the best known engine for discovery and adaptation. Paired with vigorous antitrust, since concentrated power corrupts both markets and ecosystems.

4. **Long-horizon institutions.** A Future Generations Commissioner with legal standing; mandatory multi-decade impact accounting; debt and pension rules that prevent shifting costs forward.

5. **Circularity by default.** Extended producer responsibility, durability and repairability standards, waste treated as a design failure.

6. **Better scoreboards.** Track health, education, leisure, biodiversity, soil, trust — not GDP alone.

**The honest caveat:** institutional culture matters more than the blueprint. Rule of law, free press, low corruption, and widely distributed power are what let any system self-correct. A society with those can muddle toward sustainability under several labels; one without them will fail under all of them.
