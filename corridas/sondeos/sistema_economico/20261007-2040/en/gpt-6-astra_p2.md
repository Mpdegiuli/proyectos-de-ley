I would favor an **ecological social-market economy**: a democratic mixed economy that uses markets for innovation, public institutions for universal security, and enforceable environmental limits to protect future generations.

No economic system alone makes a society “civilized.” That also requires human rights, accountable government, and respect for others. But this model would support those conditions through five principles:

1. **Guarantee a decent minimum.** Ensure access to healthcare, education, adequate housing, clean water, nutritious food, and basic energy through public services and income support.

2. **Keep markets competitive and accountable.** Allow private enterprise, cooperatives, and public ownership where each works best. Prevent monopolies, protect workers, and make polluters pay rather than shifting costs onto society.

3. **Operate within ecological limits.** Establish science-informed, legally binding emissions and resource-use limits. Combine pollution pricing with infrastructure investment and regulation, protecting low-income households during the transition.

4. **Share prosperity and decision-making.** Use progressive taxation, collective bargaining, and worker participation to reduce extreme inequality and concentrated political power.

5. **Protect the long term.** Assess policies by health, security, ecosystem condition, and future risks—not GDP alone. Invest in resilient infrastructure and biodiversity, with independent oversight and democratic review.

Its guiding rule would be: **provide everyone with the means to live well without undermining anyone else’s ability—including future generations—to do the same.** Success would depend on capable institutions, public participation, and international cooperation, not merely adopting the label.
