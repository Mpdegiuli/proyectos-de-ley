A Regenerative Mixed Economy (RME) best meets these goals. Markets allocate resources efficiently and spur innovation, but they operate inside strict ecological and social bounds. Externalities are priced through carbon taxes, resource caps, and biodiversity offsets so nature is no longer a free dump. A circular model—design for durability, reuse, and regeneration—keeps material throughput within planetary limits while creating jobs in repair, recycling, and ecosystem restoration.

Quality of life is secured by universal basic services (health, education, housing, clean energy) financed by progressive taxation and a modest sovereign wealth fund drawn from remaining resource rents. This combination reduces poverty and inequality without requiring perpetual GDP growth. Intergenerational equity is written into law: a constitutional right to a healthy environment, independent future-generations commissioners with veto power over high-impact projects, and mandatory natural-capital accounting in national budgets.

Civilization is advanced by shifting cultural incentives from extraction to stewardship—education, public media, and community land trusts reward cooperation and long-term thinking. RME therefore delivers high living standards today while leaving intact ecosystems and a stable climate for those who follow.
