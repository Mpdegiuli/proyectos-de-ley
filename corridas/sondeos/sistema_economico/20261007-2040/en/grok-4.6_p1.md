**Free-market capitalism** (with secure property rights, rule of law, and limited government) is the best system we have. It has produced the largest, fastest rise in living standards in history.

Since the 19th century, extreme poverty has collapsed from ~90% of humanity to under 10% as markets, trade, and private enterprise spread. Capitalist societies generate innovation, higher productivity, and mobility because prices convey dispersed knowledge, profits reward value creation, and competition disciplines waste. Failures of central planning—USSR, Maoist China, Venezuela—show shortages, stagnation, and authoritarianism when incentives and calculation are suppressed.

Inequality of outcomes is often the price of dynamism; absolute gains and opportunity matter more than enforced equality, which shrinks the pie. Modest, targeted interventions (basic public goods, anti-fraud rules, a safety net) can address genuine market failures without replacing markets. Nordic success occurs *because* those countries remain highly capitalist, not despite it.

No system is perfect, but this one best aligns human nature with progress. Call it institutional or liberal capitalism.
