No system is best everywhere, but the evidence favors a social market economy: competitive markets inside democratic institutions.

Markets coordinate countless choices through prices, reward useful innovation, and have driven the largest sustained gains in living standards. Property rights, open trade, and the rule of law make that possible. Left alone, though, markets mishandle public goods, pollution, monopolies, and basic insecurity.

Command economies that replaced prices with central plans repeatedly failed on information, incentives, and freedom. Unregulated capitalism can concentrate power and leave people exposed to risks they did not choose.

A social market economy keeps private enterprise and competition central, then uses transparent taxes, regulation, and social insurance to correct clear failures and provide a floor—education, health care, and basic security—without micromanaging production. Judge it by results: broad prosperity, mobility, and liberty. Institutions matter more than the label.
