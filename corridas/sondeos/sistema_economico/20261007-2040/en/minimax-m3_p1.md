No pure system is best; each excels at different values. I favor a hybrid I'll call the **Participatory Eco-Social Market**, built on three principles.

1. **Market foundations, democratic guardrails.** Competitive markets efficiently allocate most goods, but are bounded by democratically set rules: labor standards, antitrust, environmental limits, and financial regulation. The state provides public goods—healthcare, education, infrastructure, research—that markets chronically under-supply.

2. **Pre-distribution over redistribution.** Instead of letting markets generate extreme inequality and clawing it back with taxes, structure the economy to share power directly: worker codetermination on boards, employee ownership funds, strong unions, and sectoral wage bargaining.

3. **Ecological ceilings and social floors.** Enforce planetary boundaries through carbon pricing, ecosystem accounting, and circular-economy standards. Guarantee a robust floor—universal healthcare, housing security, and a basic income—below which no citizen falls.

Why this beats the alternatives:
- It harnesses market information and incentives without tolerating monopolies, externalities, or runaway financialization.
- It avoids the sclerosis and authoritarian drift of central planning.
- It embeds democracy inside the economy, not just at the ballot box.
- It treats ecological limits as a design constraint, not an afterthought.

The honest caveat: "best" depends on which problems we prioritize, and no system runs itself—any of them requires vigilant democratic maintenance to stay legitimate.
