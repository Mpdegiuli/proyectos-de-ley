No existing system fully satisfies me, so I'd choose a hybrid: the **Pluralist Market Commonwealth**. Most firms compete freely, but the system adds three features.

1. **Democratic ownership in key sectors.** Utilities, natural monopolies, and core infrastructure are run as public or cooperative enterprises, with workers and users represented on their boards. Competition cannot discipline monopolies, so public or cooperative ownership is a better fit there.

2. **Universal baseline security.** Healthcare, education, a basic income floor, and unemployment insurance are guaranteed. People can then take risks, change jobs, and refuse exploitative work without facing ruin.

3. **Strong rules against concentration and externalities.** Antitrust enforcement, carbon pricing, and environmental standards correct failures markets cannot fix alone. Limits on campaign finance keep wealth from buying political power.

Why this design? Markets are unmatched at discovering prices, allocating resources, and rewarding innovation. Central planning has repeatedly failed on information and incentives. But unregulated markets produce monopolies, pollution, insecurity, and inequality that can erode the democracy sustaining them. The commonwealth elements address these weaknesses while preserving the decentralized dynamism that makes markets productive.

The model is not utopian. Public enterprises can become inefficient or captured by politicians, and generous social spending requires taxes that may discourage work and investment. Its success therefore depends on institutions: independent regulators, transparent budgeting, and regional experimentation. I would judge it by outcomes, such as prosperity, freedom, and stability, rather than by ideology.
