The best economic system is a **democratic social-market economy**: competitive markets operating within strong democratic institutions, universal public services, and firm social and environmental safeguards.

Markets are usually effective at coordinating dispersed information, encouraging innovation, and giving people choices. However, unregulated markets concentrate wealth, underprovide public goods, permit exploitation, and ignore costs such as pollution. Central planning can address some of these failures but generally struggles with information, incentives, and excessive political control.

A democratic social-market economy combines their strengths:

- Private enterprise and open competition for most goods and services  
- Strong antitrust enforcement and regulation of systemic risks  
- Universal healthcare, education, infrastructure, and a basic social safety net  
- Progressive taxation and, where useful, taxes on land, carbon, and economic rents  
- Worker bargaining rights and opportunities for employee ownership  
- Public or cooperative provision of natural monopolies  
- Independent courts, transparent government, and accountable monetary institutions  
- Policies that preserve competition and intergenerational environmental sustainability  

No system is universally “best” independent of its institutions. This model can become bureaucratic, captured by corporations, or fiscally unsustainable. Its advantage is adaptability: democratic feedback can revise the balance between markets and public action without abolishing either. Its goal is not perfect equality, but broad freedom—freedom to create and trade, alongside freedom from destitution, domination, and preventable deprivation.
