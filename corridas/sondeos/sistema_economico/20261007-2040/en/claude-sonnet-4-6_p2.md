# Ecological Social Democracy

No existing system fully satisfies these requirements, so I propose **Ecological Social Democracy** — a hybrid framework combining the most proven elements of existing models.

## Core Principles

**Market efficiency with strong boundaries:** Free markets allocate resources effectively, but left unconstrained they externalize environmental costs onto future generations. Carbon pricing, natural resource caps, and biodiversity taxes force businesses to internalize true ecological costs.

**Universal basic services:** Healthcare, education, housing, and clean energy are guaranteed publicly — not as charity but as civilization's foundation. This eliminates survival anxiety that drives overconsumption and short-term thinking.

**Democratic ownership of commons:** Water, forests, atmosphere, and genetic resources belong collectively to present *and future* citizens. Extraction rights are leased, not sold, with revenues funding ecological restoration.

**Degrowth-compatible metrics:** GDP is replaced by wellbeing indicators measuring health, ecological stability, leisure, and intergenerational equity. Growth is welcomed when it improves lives; discouraged when it merely increases consumption.

**Participatory governance:** Local communities hold meaningful authority over land use, preventing distant corporations from imposing environmental costs on populations without consent.

## Why This Works

It preserves human creativity and incentive through markets while recognizing that markets are *tools*, not masters. Scandinavia demonstrates that high wellbeing and environmental responsibility are compatible — this system extends those principles further toward genuine sustainability.

The honest answer is that **no economic system survives poor values**. This framework assumes citizens genuinely prioritize future generations — without that cultural foundation, no system succeeds.
