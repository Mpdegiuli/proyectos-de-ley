# A Regenerative Market Economy

No existing system fully satisfies these criteria, so I propose a hybrid: the **Regenerative Market Economy (RME)**.

## Core Structure

RME combines three elements:

1. **Markets for innovation** — Competitive markets remain the engine for generating wealth, goods, and technological progress. History shows they outperform central planning at meeting diverse needs.

2. **Hard ecological boundaries** — Markets operate within non-negotiable physical limits: carbon caps, biodiversity protections, and resource extraction ceilings enforced by law, not left to pricing alone. Nature is not a commodity to bid on but a precondition for all economic activity.

3. **Universal foundational services** — Healthcare, education, housing security, and basic income are guaranteed outside the market, ensuring dignity regardless of one's economic value to employers.

## Why It Works

The fatal flaw of pure capitalism is treating nature and future generations as externalities—people who cannot bid in today's markets. The fatal flaw of central planning is informational: no committee can process the dispersed knowledge markets aggregate.

RME fixes both. It lets markets answer *how* to produce efficiently, while democratic institutions answer *whether* and *how much* to produce, constrained by ecological science.

## Civilization Component

"Civilized" implies more than wealth: it means long-horizon thinking. RME constitutionally represents future generations—perhaps through citizen assemblies with veto power over decisions with century-scale consequences.

## Honest Caveats

No system is self-executing. Corruption, short-termism, and human tribalism threaten any design. Institutions matter more than blueprints—but a blueprint that internalizes ecological costs at least makes sustainability possible rather than structurally impossible.

**In short:** markets as servant, not master; ecology as constitution, not externality.
