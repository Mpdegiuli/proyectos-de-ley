A strong candidate would be a **Regenerative Social Market Economy**.

It would combine the innovation and efficiency of markets with firm democratic rules that protect people, communities, and nature. Private enterprise could exist, but it would operate inside ecological limits: carbon budgets, biodiversity protection, circular production, strict pollution rules, and pricing that reflects real environmental costs.

Its main goals would not be endless GDP growth, but **human well-being within planetary boundaries**. Success would be measured by health, education, housing, ecosystem quality, equality, leisure time, and long-term resilience.

Key features:

- **Universal basic services**: healthcare, education, clean water, public transport, housing support, and digital access.
- **Ecological taxation**: tax pollution, resource extraction, and waste more; tax labor and basic needs less.
- **Circular economy**: products designed to be repaired, reused, recycled, or safely returned to nature.
- **Intergenerational funds**: part of national wealth invested for future generations, especially from natural resource use.
- **Worker and community participation**: employees and local communities share in decision-making.
- **Limits on extreme inequality**: through progressive taxation, inheritance rules, and strong public institutions.
- **Public investment in green infrastructure**: renewable energy, ecosystem restoration, resilient agriculture, and climate adaptation.

This system would not abolish markets, but it would make them serve civilization rather than dominate it. Its ethical principle would be simple: **no generation has the right to live well by making the future unlivable.**
