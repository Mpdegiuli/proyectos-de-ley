**Eco-Social Stewardship Economics ("EcoStewardship")**

No existing system fully balances civilization, ecological harmony, and intergenerational equity. I propose *EcoStewardship*: a hybrid model combining regulated market efficiency with democratic planning, ecological accounting, and a universal social floor.

**Core pillars:**

1. **Steady-state market economy.** Markets drive innovation and allocate most goods, but GDP growth is replaced by *wellbeing indicators* (health, biodiversity, education) as official success metrics. Degrowth is pursued where resource throughput exceeds planetary boundaries.

2. **Ecological pricing.** Carbon taxes, resource-extraction levies, and ecosystem-service payments make every product reflect its true environmental cost. Polluters pay; regenerators are rewarded.

3. **Universal social floor.** Healthcare, education, housing, and nutrition are guaranteed rights—funded by progressive taxation and ecological revenues—eliminating desperate overconsumption driven by insecurity.

4. **Democratic long-term governance.** An independent *Future Generations Council* holds veto power over policies that would degrade natural capital. Citizens' assemblies shape environmental priorities.

5. **Community and cooperative ownership.** Essential utilities, land, and commons are collectively owned; worker cooperatives are favored over extractive monopolies, distributing wealth broadly.

6. **Circular production.** Extended producer responsibility mandates repairability, reuse, and recycling. Planned obsolescence is illegal.

**Why it works:** It preserves the adaptive power of markets while binding them to ecological limits and social justice. Wealth concentration is curbed, communities gain autonomy, and nature's value is non-negotiable—ensuring future generations inherit a thriving planet rather than exhausted resources.

EcoStewardship treats civilization not as dominance over nature, but as its most responsible expression.
