# The Stewardship Economy

A hybrid drawing on social democracy, ecological economics, and Kate Raworth's "Doughnut" model. Its core principles:

**1. Markets as tools, not masters.** Decentralized markets handle most allocation efficiently, but operate within rules set democratically.

**2. A hard ecological ceiling.** Legally binding, science-based caps on emissions, resource extraction, and habitat destruction. Nature is treated as capital that cannot be liquidated; every externality is priced, and the polluter pays.

**3. A guaranteed social floor.** Universal healthcare, education, housing, and clean energy. Civilization means no one falls below dignity — something markets alone never reliably deliver.

**4. Intergenerational trusteeship.** A constitutional council empowered to block policies that shift costs onto future generations; resource revenues flow into a sovereign wealth fund owned equally by all citizens, present and future.

**5. Success measured by wellbeing.** Health, leisure, equality, and ecological vitality replace GDP growth as the primary scoreboard; growth is welcome only where it doesn't breach the ceiling.

**6. Dispersed power.** Progressive taxation and antitrust enforcement prevent wealth from converting into political capture — the Achilles' heel of both capitalism and state socialism.

Why not a pure alternative? Unregulated capitalism externalizes nature and the future; central planning misallocates resources and has historically polluted heavily too. The Stewardship Economy keeps market dynamism and innovation while making the biosphere and posterity explicit stakeholders with enforceable rights.

Civilization, nature, and the unborn share one trait: none can lobby. This system's job is to give them a voice anyway.
