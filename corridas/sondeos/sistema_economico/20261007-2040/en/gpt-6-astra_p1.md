My choice is a **democratic mixed economy**: competitive markets supported by capable public institutions, universal basic services, and a strong social safety net.

“Best” depends on what we value. I would prioritize broad prosperity, individual freedom, economic security, and environmental sustainability—not GDP alone.

This system combines several strengths:

- **Markets coordinate production and encourage innovation.** Prices convey information, competition rewards useful ideas, and entrepreneurship allows experimentation without requiring central planners to anticipate everything.
- **Public institutions address market failures.** Governments can provide infrastructure and basic research, regulate pollution, prevent monopolies, and protect consumers and workers.
- **Universal services and social insurance expand practical freedom.** Healthcare, education, and protection against unemployment or disability make people less dependent on family wealth—and better able to take productive risks.
- **Progressive taxation spreads opportunity.** Redistribution can reduce deprivation while preserving incentives to work, invest, and innovate.

Its central weakness is that **government can fail too**: bureaucracy, corruption, regulatory capture, and poorly designed benefits can undermine results. So the system needs transparent institutions, independent oversight, fiscal discipline, and policies that can be evaluated and revised.

I would favor neither unrestricted capitalism nor comprehensive state planning. The strongest approach is pragmatic: **use markets where competition works, public provision where it works better, and democratic accountability throughout.** The precise balance should vary with a country’s needs and institutional capacity.
