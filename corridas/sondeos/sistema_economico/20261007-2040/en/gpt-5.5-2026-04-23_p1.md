The best economic system is a **democratic social market economy**: markets for most goods and services, combined with strong public institutions, universal basic services, and democratic accountability.

Markets are powerful because they coordinate information, reward innovation, and allow consumer choice. But left alone, they also produce monopolies, pollution, financial crises, extreme inequality, and underinvestment in public goods. A social market economy keeps the useful parts of capitalism while limiting its harms.

Its core features would be:

- **Private enterprise and competition**, with strong antitrust laws.
- **Universal healthcare, education, and basic social insurance**, so opportunity is not determined by birth.
- **Progressive taxation** to fund public goods and reduce excessive inequality.
- **Worker protections and bargaining rights**, so growth benefits labor as well as capital.
- **Environmental pricing and regulation**, so markets reflect ecological costs.
- **Independent courts, free press, and democratic oversight**, to prevent both corporate capture and state abuse.
- **Open trade**, but with support for workers and regions harmed by disruption.

This system is best because it balances three goals that pure systems often sacrifice: **efficiency, fairness, and freedom**. Pure capitalism maximizes dynamism but tolerates insecurity and concentration of power. Central planning promises equality but often suppresses freedom and misallocates resources. A democratic social market economy accepts that no single mechanism—market or state—solves everything. It uses each where it works best.

In short: **competitive markets to create wealth, democratic government to set the rules, and social guarantees to ensure human dignity.**
