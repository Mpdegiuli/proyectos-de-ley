I would choose a **Regenerative Social Market Economy**: a mixed system combining competitive markets, democratic government, universal public services, and firm ecological limits.

Markets are useful for innovation and coordinating everyday production, but they should not determine access to necessities or permit unlimited environmental damage. Every person would therefore be guaranteed adequate housing, healthcare, education, nutrition, clean water, public transport, and a minimum income. Workers would have strong bargaining rights and opportunities to share ownership and participate in workplace decisions.

The economy would operate within scientifically defined limits for carbon emissions, biodiversity loss, pollution, land use, and resource extraction. Environmental costs would be included in prices through carbon and resource taxes, while destructive subsidies would end. Revenue would fund renewable energy, ecosystem restoration, resilient infrastructure, and support for communities affected by the transition.

Prosperity would be measured not primarily by GDP, but by health, security, equality, leisure, ecological stability, and democratic participation. Essential natural resources and monopolies would be publicly or cooperatively governed, while private enterprise would remain welcome where genuine competition serves society.

To protect future generations, an independent **Future Generations Council** could review major laws and budgets, with constitutional authority to challenge policies causing irreversible harm.

Such a system would not eliminate conflict or scarcity, but it would align economic freedom with social dignity and planetary boundaries—the foundations of a genuinely civilized society.
