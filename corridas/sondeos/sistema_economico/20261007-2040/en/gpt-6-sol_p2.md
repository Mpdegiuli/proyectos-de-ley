I would choose a **regenerative social market economy**. Markets would let people create, trade, and innovate, but democratic institutions would set firm limits where private decisions impose costs on others—especially through pollution, habitat destruction, and resource depletion.

Its foundations would be:

- **A guaranteed decent life:** universal access to healthcare, education, clean water, housing, and a basic income floor.
- **Nature counted in economic decisions:** enforceable emissions and biodiversity limits, with pollution priced and the proceeds returned to households or invested in clean infrastructure.
- **Long-term stewardship:** public investment in renewable energy, resilient communities, and ecosystem restoration, plus protections against exhausting resources future generations will need.
- **Fair opportunity and accountability:** progressive taxation, strong labor rights, competition rules, and transparent government.

The aim would not be to maximize production at any cost, nor to prohibit growth. It would be to improve well-being while living within ecological limits. A society is more civilized when people can live with dignity today without making that dignity impossible for people tomorrow.
