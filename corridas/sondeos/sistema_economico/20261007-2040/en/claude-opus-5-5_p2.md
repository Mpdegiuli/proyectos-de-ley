No existing system fully satisfies both goals, though some come close. I'd propose a hybrid built from proven parts. Call it a **Stewardship Market Economy**.

**Core principles:**

1. **Markets with honest prices.** Competition and private enterprise drive innovation and efficient allocation better than central planning has. But prices must include ecological costs through carbon taxes, pollution fees, and removal of harmful subsidies, with revenue partly returned to citizens as dividends.

2. **Hard ecological limits.** Some things shouldn't be left to prices alone. Science-based caps on emissions, fishing, logging, and land conversion define the boundaries within which markets operate.

3. **Strong social foundation.** Universal healthcare, education, and a basic safety net, as in the Nordic countries, ensure no one falls below a decent standard of living. Security also makes people more willing to accept change.

4. **Trusts for future generations.** Revenue from finite resources goes into sovereign funds (like Norway's) or commons trusts that preserve wealth beyond the current generation.

5. **Better measures of success.** GDP is supplemented by wellbeing, inequality, and natural-capital indicators, as New Zealand and Bhutan have attempted.

6. **Institutions with long time horizons.** Independent guardians, like Wales's Future Generations Commissioner, review legislation for long-term impact.

7. **Democracy and rule of law.** Accountability and free information are what correct mistakes.

**A caveat:** Thoughtful people weigh freedom, equality, and growth differently, and every design involves trade-offs. The real test is whether it can adapt as conditions and knowledge change.
