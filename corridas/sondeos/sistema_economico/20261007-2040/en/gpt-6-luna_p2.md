I would choose a **democratic ecological mixed economy**: markets where they work well, strong public services, and community ownership of essential natural resources.

Markets can encourage innovation and coordinate everyday choices, but they should operate within firm ecological limits. Governments would set binding budgets for greenhouse gases and other pressures on nature, phase out harmful subsidies, and make polluters pay. Shared resources—such as forests, fisheries, and water systems—would be managed as commons, with local participation and enforceable safeguards.

Everyone should have access to essentials such as healthcare, education, housing, clean energy, and nutritious food. Progressive taxation and social insurance can reduce insecurity and ensure that the costs of the transition do not fall hardest on people with the least. Workers and communities should also have a voice in decisions affecting their livelihoods.

Success should be measured not simply by GDP, but by health, security, freedom, equality, and the condition of ecosystems. Independent institutions, transparent accounting, and democratic review would help prevent both corporate capture and unaccountable state power.

No system can guarantee a good future, but this approach joins individual initiative with shared responsibility. Its central rule would be: meet everyone’s needs while keeping economic activity within ecological limits, so future generations inherit both the means to live well and the freedom to choose their own path.
