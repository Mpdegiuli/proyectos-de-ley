I would call it the **Stewarded Commons Economy**. No existing system fully meets your criteria, so this is a hybrid built from parts that have worked in practice.

**Core features:**

1. **Ecological limits first:** Scientists set binding caps on carbon emissions, resource extraction, and pollution. Markets operate within these boundaries, not above them.
2. **Cap-and-dividend:** Permits for scarce resources are auctioned, and the revenue is paid equally to citizens, so the price of pollution is reflected in what people pay.
3. **Full-cost pricing:** Goods carry the environmental and social costs of their production.
4. **Commons ownership:** Water, atmosphere, forests, fisheries, and genetic resources are held in trust for everyone and managed by local or regional bodies.
5. **Intergenerational funds:** A share of resource revenues goes into sovereign wealth funds, as Norway does, so future generations inherit capital as well as ecosystems.
6. **Guaranteed basics:** Healthcare, education, housing, and a minimum income are provided as rights, so people are not forced into destructive work.
7. **Wellbeing measures:** Policy is judged by indicators such as health, ecological integrity, and social trust, not GDP growth alone.
8. **Future-generation representation:** Citizens' assemblies and legal guardians for future generations, similar to Wales's Future Generations Act, give long-term interests a voice in decisions.

**Why this design:** Markets allocate goods efficiently and encourage innovation, while binding limits and commons ownership prevent the overuse that markets alone tend to produce.

**Caveat:** No society has fully implemented this model, and it faces real challenges, including political capture, enforcement, and global coordination. It is best understood as a direction to test and refine, not a proven solution.
