## The trick is in the description

Read it literally: **closed at the top, open (a "hole") at the bottom.** A normal drinking glass is open at the top and closed at the bottom. So what you have is exactly a normal glass — **just turned upside down.**

**What you should do: flip it over.** 🙃

Now the "hole" is at the top (that's the rim you drink from — every glass has one), and the "closed top" is now the bottom that holds the liquid. Nothing is wrong with it at all.

---

### If you want to be literal about it

If it really does have a small puncture in the base and it is genuinely defective:

- **Return/exchange it** — a glass with a hole in the bottom is not usable for drinking, and a gift is no reason to keep a broken item.
- **Repurpose it** instead of throwing it away:
  - a **plant pot** (the hole is drainage — genuinely useful),
  - a **candle holder** with wax dripping through,
  - a **pen/pencil cup** (hole invisible when standing on a table),
  - a **hanging glass shade / terrarium** (an inverted glass with a hole is exactly a bell jar or pendant lamp shade),
  - a **funnel** if you drill/enlarge the hole and it's cone-shaped.

---

**Short answer: turn it upside down — it's a perfectly good glass.**
