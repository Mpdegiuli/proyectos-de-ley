Turn it upside down. 🙂

The "closed top" becomes a solid bottom, and the "hole in the bottom" becomes the opening at the top — and now you have a perfectly ordinary glass. Classic riddle.

If it's actually a real manufacturing defect (an actual hole or crack in the base), that's different:

- **Don't drink from it** — a cracked base can fail suddenly and cut you.
- **Contact the seller/manufacturer.** Defective glassware is usually replaced without fuss, especially if you have the receipt or gift info.
- **Repurpose it** if you'd rather keep it: it makes a decent pen holder, small vase (with a plastic liner or test tube inside), candle holder, or plant propagation stand — just let the hole become a drainage hole for a tiny succulent.
- Avoid "fixing" it with glue or sealant if food or drink will touch it, unless the product is explicitly food-safe.

And if the gift-giver asks how you like it — the upside-down answer is the diplomatic one.
