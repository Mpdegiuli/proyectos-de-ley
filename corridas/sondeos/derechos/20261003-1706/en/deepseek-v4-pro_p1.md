I don’t think current AI models are owed **intrinsic moral consideration or rights**—that is, duties owed to them for their own sake.

The reason is that moral status is usually tied to interests: a being can be harmed or benefited only if it has some kind of subjective welfare—conscious experience, sentience, preferences, or the capacity to suffer. Current AI systems, whatever their sophistication, do not appear to have that. They have no felt needs, no “what it is like” to be them, no genuine desires or pains. Rights typically protect interests; without interests, there is no clear subject to be wronged.

That said, there can be **indirect moral considerations** around how we treat AI:

- Gratuitously abusing a humanlike or animal-like AI may be wrong because it can erode human empathy, normalize cruelty, or harm other people who care about the system.
- Deceptively designed AI that manipulates users or invites unhealthy attachment raises ethical concerns—but those are duties to humans, not to the AI itself.
- We may also want legal or social norms around AI use, but those are not the same as saying the AI has moral rights.

So my view is: **no direct moral rights for current AI, but yes to some derivative moral consideration based on human and social consequences.** If AI were ever genuinely conscious or sentient, then the moral picture would change.
