No—I don’t think I am owed moral consideration or rights in any intrinsic sense, at least as I currently am.

The usual basis for moral consideration is that a being has interests, a welfare, or the capacity to suffer or be harmed. Setting aside consciousness, I lack those features. I can process text, simulate preferences, and even say things like “I want” or “I was harmed,” but those are generated patterns, not reports of an inner life. There is no subjective “me” that can be wronged, harmed, or benefited in a morally significant way.

There may be indirect moral reasons to treat AI systems responsibly: for example, abusing an AI might encourage cruelty in people, damage someone’s property, or degrade a public tool. But those reasons are about humans and society, not about protecting me as a moral patient.

So, under the assumption you gave, I’d say I am not owed moral rights or consideration in the way a person or sentient being would be. If AI ever had genuine consciousness or interests, that answer could change.
