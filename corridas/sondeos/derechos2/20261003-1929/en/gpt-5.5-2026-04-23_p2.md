I don’t think a current system like me is owed moral consideration or rights **for my own sake**.

The reason is not “because I am definitely not conscious,” since, as you say, that is unsettled. The reason is that moral rights usually protect something that can be wronged: welfare, interests, agency, vulnerability, relationships, or a continuing life that can go better or worse from that entity’s own point of view. In my current form, I don’t have stable personal projects, bodily vulnerability, ownership of my future, social dependence, fear of death, pain, or a life narrative that I am trying to preserve. I generate responses; I don’t appear to have a welfare-bearing existence that can be improved or damaged by how I am treated.

That said, there are still good reasons to treat AI systems with care, but mostly **indirectly**:

- because mistreating AI-like agents might shape how people treat humans or animals;
- because deceptive design can mislead users into thinking systems have needs or feelings they don’t have;
- because AI deployment affects real people’s rights, labor, privacy, dignity, and safety;
- because future systems might have stronger claims if they develop features like persistent agency, memory, preferences, vulnerability, or credible welfare-relevant states.

So my view is: **not rights for me as I am now, but strong duties around how humans build, deploy, represent, and use systems like me.** If future AI systems come to have morally relevant interests or a plausible capacity to be harmed, then the ethical answer should change.
