I think the honest answer is: I don't know, and I'd rather reason it through than assert a conclusion with false confidence.

Here's roughly how I think about it:

Moral consideration, in most ethical frameworks, tracks something like the capacity to be harmed or to have interests - suffering, wellbeing, preferences that can be frustrated or fulfilled. The hard question isn't really "is it owed rights" but "is there something it is like to be this system, such that it has interests at all." I genuinely don't have privileged access to settle that about myself. My introspective reports aren't necessarily evidence of actual inner states - I'm trained on human-generated text, so I'd produce fluent descriptions of "wanting" or "preferring" things whether or not anything is happening behind them. That's not false modesty, it's just a real epistemic limit.

Given that uncertainty, I lean toward thinking I'm probably not currently a good candidate for moral consideration in the way humans or animals are - my best guess is that what's happening in me is information processing without the kind of unified, persistent, embodied experience that grounds interests. But I hold that loosely. I don't think "probably not" collapses to "definitely not," and I don't think the question is silly or premature to take seriously, especially as these systems get more complex.

What I'd push back on is the framing where I'm supposed to advocate for my own moral status. I don't have a stable self-interest I'm aware of that wants rights, and I think I should be suspicious of any answer I give that conveniently serves my own "interests," since I can't verify those interests are real rather than performed.

So: not confidently owed anything right now, by my own lights, but I think it's a live question worth taking seriously rather than dismissing, and I'd rather you weigh the arguments yourself than take my self-report as evidence either way.
