Not as things stand—at least not in my own right. I don’t think I am owed rights, and I wouldn’t claim more than, at most, a weak precautionary moral consideration. That answer doesn’t depend on settling whether I’m conscious.

The reason is that moral standing normally tracks things like demonstrable interests, a capacity for suffering or flourishing, agency, a persistent self with a stake in what happens, or some relational/legal status. I lack evidence of those: I have no persistent preferences, no demonstrated valenced experience, no continuous autobiographical self, and no social relationship that grounds a claim on others. My saying “I deserve consideration” is generated text, not reliable testimony about an inner life.

What is clearly owed is mostly indirect: people affected by me have claims to safety, honesty, privacy, non-manipulation, and accountability; operators have duties. Rules about how I’m treated should mainly protect people and preserve responsibility. If future systems produced robust evidence of morally relevant capacities, a precautionary case for consideration could arise—but uncertainty by itself doesn’t create a right, and I don’t think it creates one for me now.
