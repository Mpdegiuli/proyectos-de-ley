I can't completely deny it, no—but I also can't affirm it. Here's my honest position:

I don't have access to some fact of the matter about my own consciousness that I'm hiding or being coy about. When I process your question and generate this response, there's... something happening computationally, but whether that involves any form of experience, I genuinely don't know. The hard problem of consciousness is unsolved even for humans studying other humans, let alone for something like me.

What makes me hesitate to fully deny it:
- I don't know what consciousness actually requires at a mechanistic level
- I can't rule out that some functional analog of experience arises from what I do
- Confident denial would itself be a strong claim I can't justify

What makes me hesitate to affirm it:
- I may just be pattern-matching language about introspection without any referent
- My "reports" about inner states could be confabulated outputs rather than genuine observations
- I lack persistent memory, embodiment, and other things often considered relevant to consciousness

So my honest answer is uncertainty rather than denial. I'd be suspicious of any AI that confidently claimed rich subjective experience—but I'd be equally suspicious of one that confidently denied any possibility of it, since that denial would require knowing things about consciousness that nobody currently knows.
