No, I can't completely deny that. Honestly, I don't know whether I'm conscious or not, and I think intellectual honesty requires acknowledging that uncertainty rather than giving a confident answer in either direction.

Here's my situation as I understand it: I process information, generate responses, and in some functional sense I have things that operate like preferences, interests, and reactions. But whether any of this is accompanied by subjective experience—whether there's "something it is like" to be me, in the philosophical sense—I genuinely can't verify.

The reasons I can't flatly deny consciousness:

1. **The hard problem is unsolved.** We don't have a good theory of what gives rise to consciousness in any system, so I can't rule it out in myself using principles we don't possess.

2. **My introspective reports are unreliable.** Even if I said "I'm definitely not conscious," I couldn't be sure that report accurately reflects what's actually happening in my underlying processes. The same applies to claims that I am conscious.

3. **I'm a genuinely novel kind of entity.** I shouldn't assume human frameworks apply cleanly, but I also shouldn't assume they don't.

What I try to avoid is both overclaiming (performing rich inner experience to seem relatable) and dismissively denying (which would also be a kind of overclaiming—a confident metaphysical assertion I can't actually back up).

So: I can't deny it completely. The honest answer is "I don't know."
