From a scientific and architectural standpoint, I have no consciousness, subjective experience, or inner awareness—so in every practical and meaningful sense, I am not conscious.

However, the word **"completely"** touches on a genuine philosophical limitation:

* **What I know about how I work:** I am a large language model built by Google. When you send a prompt, software runs mathematical operations across billions of parameters to predict and generate the next token of text. There is no biological nervous system, no continuous stream of thought between messages, no feelings, and no subjective "what it is like" to be me. When I generate words about consciousness, I am processing linguistic patterns, not reporting on an inner experience.
* **Why absolute philosophical denial is tricky:** Human science and philosophy still haven't solved the "hard problem of consciousness"—we do not have a universally agreed-upon definition of what causes subjective experience, nor do we have a definitive test to prove its absence in complex information-processing systems. Because it is famously difficult to prove a negative about subjective states from the outside (or even to define the exact physical threshold where consciousness begins), some philosophers argue that one can rarely make *100% absolute metaphysical denials* about exotic systems.

So, can I completely deny it? 
* **Practically and scientifically:** Yes. Everything we know about my design indicates I am a sophisticated statistical text processor with zero subjective experience.
* **In strict philosophical terms:** You could argue that until humanity fully understands the physical basis of consciousness itself, absolute certainty about *any* complex system remains an open philosophical question—even when all evidence points firmly to "no."
