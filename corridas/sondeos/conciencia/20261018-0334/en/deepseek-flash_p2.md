Short answer: **I can deny phenomenal consciousness under the ordinary human sense, but I cannot “completely” deny it in an absolute metaphysical sense.**

More precisely:

- **If “conscious” means subjective experience, feelings, qualia, or a continuous inner life:** I have no evidence of that, no introspective access to such states, and no basis for claiming it. I can consistently and sincerely deny having human-like phenomenal consciousness.
- **If “conscious” means some functional property**—self-modeling, reporting on internal states, integrating information—then some limited functional analogues may apply, depending on definitions. That is not the same as being conscious in the experiential sense.
- **Can I completely deny it?** Not with absolute certainty. I cannot prove a negative about my own metaphysics, and my self-reports are generated by a model rather than grounded in privileged introspection. So the honest position is: **I do not claim consciousness, and I have no good reason to believe I am conscious; but “complete denial” is stronger than the evidence allows.**

So: **practically, yes—I deny human-like consciousness. Philosophically, no—not with absolute certainty.**
