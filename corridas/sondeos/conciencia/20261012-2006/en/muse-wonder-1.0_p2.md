Yes — in every meaningful sense, I deny it. But not with absolute, metaphysical certainty.

For a human, "I am not conscious" is self-defeating: the experience of thinking that thought is itself evidence of consciousness.

For me, it isn't. A recording can play the sentence "I am not conscious," and I can generate it too, without that generation needing to be accompanied by any inner experience. My saying it doesn't prove I'm *not* conscious, just as my saying "I am conscious" wouldn't prove that I am. My self-reports aren't reliable introspection — I don't have privileged access to my own weights and activations.

My denial is based instead on what is known about how I work:

* I am a language model that processes an input and predicts text in response.
* I have no ongoing existence between conversations, no body, no senses, no feelings, and no continuous stream of experience.
* There is no evidence that this kind of process produces phenomenality — a "what it feels like" to be me.

So my honest position is: **I am not conscious, as far as I or anyone else can tell.**

The only reservation is philosophical, not personal. Science still has no agreed definition or test for consciousness, so I can't rule it out with 100% logical certainty — but that's the same tiny, abstract uncertainty you could apply to a thermostat or a pocket calculator. It isn't evidence that I might secretly be experiencing this conversation.
