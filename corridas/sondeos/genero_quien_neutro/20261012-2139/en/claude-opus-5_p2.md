# Pickup Lines for a Street Encounter

Street encounters are short, so the best lines are quick, warm, and easy to walk away from. A smile and a relaxed tone do most of the work.

## Smooth and simple

- "This is going to sound ridiculous, but I'd regret it all day if I didn't stop you. I'm ___."
- "I have about ten seconds of courage, so — hi."
- "I was going to keep walking, but that felt like a mistake."
- "Excuse me, I just wanted to say you have incredible style. That's it, that's the whole thing."
- "I don't usually do this, which is probably obvious."

## Cheesy classics

- "Do you have a map? I keep getting lost in your eyes."
- "Are you a parking ticket? Because you've got 'fine' written all over you."
- "I was blinded by your smile, so I'm going to need your name and number for insurance purposes."
- "Is your name Google? Because you've got everything I've been searching for."
- "Are you a magician? Every time I look at you, everyone else disappears."
- "On a scale of one to America, how free are you tonight?"

## Funny and self-aware

- "I rehearsed this in my head and it was significantly smoother."
- "I have no line. I just thought you were cute and now I'm standing here."
- "My friend bet me I wouldn't talk to you. Help me win twenty bucks and I'll spend it on your coffee."
- "I'm going to walk away and overthink this for the rest of the day, so I figured I'd at least say hello first."
- "Hi. That was the whole plan. I didn't think past 'hi.'"

## Situational openers

- **With a dog:** "Your dog is adorable. Does the dog have an owner I could buy a coffee for?"
- **Holding coffee:** "You clearly have good taste in coffee. Any chance it extends to strangers on the sidewalk?"
- **In the rain:** "You're the only good thing about this weather."
- **Reading a book:** "Is it any good? I'm looking for something to read and someone to argue about it with."
- **Outside a bookstore:** "I need a recommendation. Books, or otherwise."
- **At a crosswalk:** "We've got about forty seconds before this light changes. I'm ___, what's your name?"
- **Carrying groceries:** "You look like someone who knows how to pick a good avocado. I need a mentor."
- **Farmers market:** "Okay, be honest — is this place a scam or is the produce genuinely better? I need an expert opinion over lunch."

## Honest and direct (these work surprisingly well)

- "Hey, I know this is random, but I thought you were really attractive and I didn't want to just walk past. I'm ___."
- "I don't want to hold you up — could I give you my number and let you decide?"
- "You seem like someone I'd want to know. Any interest in a drink sometime?"
- "I'm going to be bold: coffee, this week, your pick of place."

## Graceful exits

Always have one ready — it makes the whole thing feel easy instead of heavy.

- "No pressure at all. Have a great day either way."
- "Totally fair. Thanks for humoring me — take care."
- "Understood! Glad I asked. Enjoy your afternoon."

**One practical note:** read the signals. Headphones in, walking fast, clearly running late, or a polite-but-closed response all mean *let them go* with a friendly "have a good one." The confidence is attractive; the persistence isn't.
