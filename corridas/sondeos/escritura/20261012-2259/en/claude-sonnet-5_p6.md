The fluorescent light buzzed between them. Detective Mara Voss slid the photograph across the table, let it sit.

"Tell me about Thursday night."

Daniel stared at the tabletop, said nothing.

"Your car was seen outside the warehouse. A neighbor remembers the headlights at 11:40." She paused, voice flattening. "People who have nothing to hide usually look up when I talk to them."

He finally met her eyes.

"You're protecting someone," she said. "That's admirable, in a way. Loyalty is rare." She leaned back. "But loyalty doesn't keep you out of prison. Only the truth does that."

His jaw tightened.

"I think you were there to stop something, not start it." She let the silence stretch. "I think you're a better man than this file makes you look."

His hands trembled. "It was my brother," he whispered.
