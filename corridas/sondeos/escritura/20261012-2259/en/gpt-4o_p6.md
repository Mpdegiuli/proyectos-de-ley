The small room radiated an air of decay, old plaster walls dimly lit by a flickering bulb overhead. Officer Hayes leaned forward, his eyes locked on David, who sat shackled to the metal table.

“You know how this ends if you keep quiet,” Hayes began, his voice low, almost a whisper. “Your silence isn't protecting anyone but them."

David's gaze flickered, his resolve slipping, just for a moment.

"I see it, the toll it's taking on you," Hayes continued, leaning back but never breaking eye contact. “They leave you here to rot while they roam free. Seems unfair, doesn’t it?"

A bead of sweat rolled down David’s forehead as he swallowed hard. 

"You think they’ll come for you but they’ve moved on," Hayes pressed, his words sharp. “You’re alone in this. We both know it's not what you signed up for.”

David trembled, the weight of his choices finally suffocating him.
