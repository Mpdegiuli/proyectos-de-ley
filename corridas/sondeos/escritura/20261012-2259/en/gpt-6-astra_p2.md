Small silver aqueduct, built for no river,
I lift you from the dust beneath the desk.
Your double arch once held a kingdom fast:
the shopping list, the bill, the penciled kiss.
Now all those provinces are scattered.
Only your patient geometry survives.
I lay you on black velvet in the light
and name this age by what you would not sever.
How little metal held our days together.
How gently you release them to the earth.
