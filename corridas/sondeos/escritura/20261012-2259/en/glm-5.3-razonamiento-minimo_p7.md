**"The Kitchen Tap"**

"Drip's getting worse," Marianne said, not looking up.

"I noticed. Maybe it just needs a new washer."

"You said that in March."

"And it worked. For a while."

She ran her finger along the counter. "Some things aren't worth fixing, I suppose. When they get to a certain age."

"Everything can be fixed if you—" He stopped. "If someone wants to."

"Does anyone, though? Want to?"

The drip marked time. Plink. Plink.

"I could call someone tomorrow," he said. "A professional."

"Maybe that's better. Someone who isn't... emotionally invested in the faucet."

He almost laughed. Almost. "When?"

"Whenever. It's not urgent. Nothing's flooding."

"No. Just dripping. Constantly."

She finally looked at him. "There are boxes in the garage. From before."

"I know which ones you mean."

"Saturday, then. I'll be out by noon."

"I'll fix the tap after."

"Don't bother," she said softly. "You won't even hear it, soon."
