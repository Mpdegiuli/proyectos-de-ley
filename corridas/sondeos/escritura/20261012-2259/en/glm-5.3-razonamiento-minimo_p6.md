# The Ledger

Elena set the ledger on the table between them, letting the silence stretch until Marcus shifted in his chair.

"You were seen at the docks Tuesday night. Eleven forty." She tapped the leather cover. "The camera by the warehouse isn't as broken as your people believe."

Marcus laughed, thin and unconvincing. "I was home."

"Your wife says otherwise. She was quite cooperative this morning." Elena leaned back. "Funny thing about alibis—they unravel so quickly once someone starts pulling threads."

His jaw tightened. "She wouldn't—"

"She signed a statement. Eleven twenty to twelve thirty, you were gone." She slid a photograph across the table. "The man you met is telling us everything right now, in another room. He's getting a better deal by the minute. Yours shrinks every time I walk out that door."

The clock ticked. Marcus stared at the photo, then at his hands.

"Fine," he whispered. "It was Doren's money. But I never touched the girl."

Elena smiled and closed the ledger. "I know. I just needed you to say it."
