Every morning begins before dawn; Elias never allows the sun to beat him to the day’s work. He spends his first waking hour repairing discarded radios, breathing life into things others have abandoned. When the local factory offered him a lucrative management position on the condition that he fire three veteran workers, Elias declined without a second thought, deciding instead to remain on the assembly line. He speaks in carefully measured sentences, never interrupting, always waiting for the other person to exhale before offering a solution. If a neighbor leaves a tool out in the rain, Elias cleans and oils it before returning it silently. He navigates his life by a strict code of unseen obligations, constantly choosing the harder, quieter path. He dismisses praise, deciding that a functioning world is the only reward he requires.
