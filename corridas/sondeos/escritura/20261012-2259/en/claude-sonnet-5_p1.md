Mira wakes before dawn, not because she must, but because stillness unsettles her. She counts inventory twice, corrects her brother's math homework without being asked, and walks the longer route home to avoid a neighbor she once lied to. When the factory where she works announces layoffs, she volunteers first, believing someone with fewer debts deserves the safety of staying employed. She spends her evenings repairing neighbors' radios for free, refusing payment even when offered, insisting that kindness shouldn't carry a price tag. When a stranger accuses her of theft she didn't commit, she doesn't argue—she simply returns the next day, and the day after, until suspicion wears itself out. She keeps promises long after convenience fades, chooses silence over cruelty, and never explains her reasoning, letting her choices speak instead of her words.
