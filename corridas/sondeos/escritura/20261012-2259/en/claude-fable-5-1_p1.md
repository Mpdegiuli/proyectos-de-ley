Mara counts the cutlery every night before locking the diner, not because anyone has ever stolen a fork, but because counting is how she tells herself the day is finished. When the regulars argue about the news, she refills their coffee and says nothing, then slips the quieter one an extra slice of pie. She has turned down three offers to sell the place, each time without explanation, though she keeps the letters in a drawer she opens on bad nights.

When her nephew showed up at closing with a bag and no plan, she didn't ask what he'd done. She handed him an apron and told him the grill needed scraping. She pays her suppliers before she pays herself, forgives debts she never mentions, and has rehearsed exactly one apology for twenty years without delivering it. On Sundays, she drives to the cemetery and sits in the car with the engine running.
