Mara had one rule: never leave a problem for someone else to inherit. When the river alarms failed, she abandoned the council meeting and went to the old pumping station, where she found the gate chained shut. The officials wanted to wait for engineers; Mara chose to break the seal and divert the water through the market district, saving the hospital but flooding her own workshop. By dawn, she had recorded every decision and sent the ledger to the families who would pay for the damage. They demanded a culprit. She gave them her name, then organized repairs before they could decide what punishment she deserved. Mara did not expect gratitude. She expected the next alarm to sound, and made sure someone would answer it.
