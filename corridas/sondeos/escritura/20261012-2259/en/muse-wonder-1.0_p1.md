Mara keeps the lighthouse log even on nights when no ships pass, writing down wind, tide, and the names of gulls she has invented. When the supply boat fails to arrive, she rations lamp oil, eats less, and still lights the beacon first. She rescues a stranger from the rocks, shares her last tea, and asks no questions until he can sleep safely. When he offers money to smuggle an unmarked crate ashore, she refuses, rows it back through rough water, and reports it despite his threats. Each dawn she repairs what storms break, teaches herself knots from a ruined manual, and chooses, again, to stay and keep watch for people she may never meet.
