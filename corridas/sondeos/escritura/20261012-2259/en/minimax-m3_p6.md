The fluorescent light buzzed above the steel table. Across from me, Marco worked a piece of gum like it was the only thing still alive in him.

"I know about the dock at eleven," I said. I let the silence settle into his shoulders. "Forty-eight minutes is a long stretch for a man who claims he never left his apartment."

He shifted. I set a folder on the table but didn't open it.

"Your sister still believes you're in Geneva. Lovely time of year there. Unless she'd prefer not knowing where you really were."

The gum cracked between his teeth.

"Tell me what you moved. Because the man who bought it is already downstairs."

His eyes flicked to the folder. Then to the door. Then to the floor, where his hands had begun to shake.
