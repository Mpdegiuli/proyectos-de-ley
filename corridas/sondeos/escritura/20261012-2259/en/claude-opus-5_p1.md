She arrives an hour before anyone else and works by the glow of the monitor, because the quiet makes her honest. When the audit turns up the discrepancy, she does not report it to her supervisor; she drives to the warehouse, counts the pallets herself, and only then makes the call that ends three careers, including the one belonging to the man who hired her. She apologizes to no one and explains to everyone. She keeps a list of the people she has disappointed and sends them money anonymously when she can. On weekends she teaches a free class on reading contracts, and she never checks whether anyone comes back twice. Offered the promotion, she asks for a transfer instead — the branch no one wants, the books nobody has balanced in years.
