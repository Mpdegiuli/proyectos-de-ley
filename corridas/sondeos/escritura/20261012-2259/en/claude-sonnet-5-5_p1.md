Marisol keeps a ledger of promises she has made, and she has never left one unpaid. When the cannery where she works announces layoffs, she volunteers her own name last, after quietly teaching the youngest workers how to read the safety contracts. She spends her severance on a secondhand ferry engine rather than a safer, smaller dream, because the island's only link to the mainland is failing and nobody else will fix it. She argues with the council for months, loses, and then does the work anyway, unpaid, at night. When the mayor offers her credit for the repairs, she refuses and asks instead that the boatmen's names be carved on the dock. She doesn't announce her choices; she simply makes them, then shows up at dawn to see them through.
