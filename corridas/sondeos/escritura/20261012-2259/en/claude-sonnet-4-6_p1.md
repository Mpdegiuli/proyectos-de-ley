# The Weight of Small Choices

She wakes before her alarm, spending the first quiet minutes writing down three things she is grateful for, even on the mornings when finding them feels like searching through rubble. At work, she takes notes during meetings not to remember what was said, but to notice who was interrupted. She returns every call, even the difficult ones. When a colleague accepts credit for her idea, she says nothing in the moment, then spends the evening deciding whether silence was wisdom or cowardice. She volunteers at the food pantry on Saturdays but refuses to post about it. She has a habit of apologizing too quickly and is slowly trying to stop. When faced with a choice between comfortable and honest, she hesitates — genuinely hesitates — and that hesitation is perhaps the most truthful thing about her.
