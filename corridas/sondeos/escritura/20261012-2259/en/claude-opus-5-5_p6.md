The lamp buzzed. Harrow set the photograph on the table, face down.

"You were at the docks Tuesday night."

Lena said nothing.

"Your sister wasn't. She was home, putting your nephew to bed. Sweet kid. Seven, I think." He tapped the photograph. "Funny how quickly a family can become a file."

"Leave her out of this."

"I'd love to. You make that possible." He leaned back. "Someone moved the crates. Someone strong enough, with keys. You don't have the keys."

Her jaw tightened.

"So it's a man. Older. Trusts you enough to let you watch."

"You don't know anything."

"I know you just looked left. People look toward what they're protecting." He turned the photograph over: an empty warehouse door. "Tell me his name, and your sister sleeps well tonight."

A long silence.

"Viktor," she whispered.
