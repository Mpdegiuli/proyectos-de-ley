Victor slid the photographs across the metal table, leaving them face down. He didn’t need to flip them. The sweat beading on Silas’s upper lip told him everything. 

"You were the last one in the vault," Victor said, his voice a soft, flat drawl. "The ledger is gone. Your gambling debts are suddenly paid."

Silas stared at his own shaking hands. "I never touched it."

"Your wife thinks you're on a business trip," Victor continued, leaning in until the scent of his expensive cologne choked the stale air between them. "I can make that trip permanent. Or, you can give me an address. Right now."

Silas swallowed hard, his eyes darting to the locked door. 

"They won't protect you," Victor whispered. "I am the only one who can keep you breathing."

A heavy silence fell. Silas broke first. 

"Locker 42, Union Station." 

Victor smiled. "Good boy."
