The lamp buzzed between them. Marsh slid a photograph across the table.

"Warehouse on Calder Street. Tuesday night. You were seen."

Doyle folded his arms. "People see all kinds of things."

"Funny thing about your brother. He talked for two hours this morning. Gave us names. Dates." Marsh leaned back. "I'd hate to think he mentioned yours by mistake."

Doyle's jaw tightened. "Danny wouldn't."

"Danny did. But here's my problem—his story and the evidence don't quite match. Someone's lying, and the one who's lying takes the full sentence." Marsh tapped the photo. "Tell me about the shipment, and I'll assume the liar is him."

Silence stretched. The lamp buzzed.

"He's got kids," Doyle said finally, voice cracking.

"Then help him."

Doyle stared at his hands for a long moment. "The shipment comes in Friday. Pier nine. Midnight."

Marsh smiled. Danny hadn't said a word.
