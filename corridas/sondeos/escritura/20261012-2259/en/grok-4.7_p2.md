Bottle Cap

Here lies a coin the earth refused to spend,
a little lid of aluminum and weather,
its painted name half-eaten by the rain
yet still proclaiming some forgotten thirst.

Who bent the skirt of metal with a thumb
and heard the small gasp of a world unsealed?
The trench is shallow—only parking-lot dust—
but every monument begins as litter.

I brush it clean the way a scholar might
and read the ridges as a lost inscription:
not king, not god, not war—just afternoon,
and someone pausing long enough to drink.
