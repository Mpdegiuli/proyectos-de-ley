Mara set the bloodied lighter on the table between them.

“Your hands shook when I said the docks. Not when I said the warehouse. Not when I said fire.”

Jonas stared at the wall.

She slid a photograph beside the lighter: his sister outside a school, laughing.

“She still leaves at three. Same blue gate. Same alley shortcut. You already know I’m not the cruel one in this room.”

His jaw tightened.

Mara leaned closer, voice soft. “You carried the case for Voss. You thought it was medicine. Then you heard ticking, and you ran. Sensible. Cowardly, but sensible.”

Jonas swallowed.

“The bomb is under something that moves. A ferry, then. Not the north terminal, too many guards. South pier. Locker thirteen, because Voss likes jokes.”

His eyes flicked.

Mara smiled without warmth. “Thank you.”
