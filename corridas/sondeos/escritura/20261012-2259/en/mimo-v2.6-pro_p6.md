"You want to talk about where you were last night." The interrogator's voice was flat, surgical. He set a photograph on the table — a girl, smiling, alive.

The suspect's eyes flicked to the image. A mistake.

"The lab already ran your shoe. Glass from the window matches." He leaned in. "You think silence protects you. It doesn't. It just tells me you have something to hide."

The suspect's jaw tightened.

"Right now, a jury sees a man who refused to cooperate. But if you tell me who else was in that house—" he paused, letting the silence thicken like blood—"I can make sure they understand you were forced into this."

A bead of sweat rolled past the suspect's temple.

"You're thinking about her mother. Whether she knows." The interrogator turned the photograph face-down. "Give me a name. Give me the truth. Let me help you survive what's coming."

The suspect's lips parted.
