The lamp buzzed. Vasquez set down the folder without opening it.

"You're going to tell me where the car is."

Mikhail folded his arms. "I want a lawyer."

"Sure. I'll note the time." She didn't move. "Your brother already sat in that chair. Same scratch on the armrest—he made it, actually. Nervous hands."

"He doesn't know anything."

"That's what worries me. A man who knows nothing can't trade anything." She let the silence stretch until it hurt. "The lot on Carver Street has cameras now. Installed Tuesday. You'd have driven past them Thursday."

His jaw tightened. She saw it.

"So it's not Carver. Somewhere flooded last week, then—you wouldn't risk the water. That leaves the quarry road."

"You're guessing."

"I'm listening." She finally opened the folder, blank page facing him. "Tell me I'm wrong about the quarry. Tell me slowly."

Mikhail looked at the page for a long moment.

"The gate's unlocked," he said.
