Elena never waited for permission. When the river flooded the lower town, she waded in first, pulling children onto the roof of her stalled truck before the current took it. She decided that night to stay, even after the evacuation order, because the old man who refused to leave still had three goats and no one else would feed them. In the weeks that followed she mapped the ruined streets by walking them at dawn, marking which houses still had dry basements and which wells had not yet turned foul. She chose to share the last of her rice with the neighbor who had once reported her for unpaid taxes. When the relief trucks finally arrived she did not join the line; she directed them instead, pointing out the families who would never come forward on their own.
