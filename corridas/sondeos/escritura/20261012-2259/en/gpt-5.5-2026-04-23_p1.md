Mara begins each day by fixing what others have learned to step around: a broken lock, an unpaid bill, a frightened child left outside the clinic. She keeps lists of debts she does not intend to collect and promises she refuses to break. When the town council votes to sell the river to the company that poisoned it, she steals the records, copies them by hand, and leaves them on every doorstep before dawn. She lies only when the truth would endanger someone weaker, and even then she remembers the cost. Offered safety in exchange for silence, she chooses trial instead. She is not fearless; she simply treats fear as one more voice at the table, never the one allowed to decide.
