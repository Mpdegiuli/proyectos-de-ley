Every morning, Mara opens the town library before anyone asks her to. She returns overdue books without fines, then records the lost revenue in a ledger she intends to show the council. When the river floods, she unlocks the archive and takes its maps to the rescue crews, though the mayor ordered the building sealed. She refuses praise afterward. Instead, she asks why the evacuation plan ignored the oldest streets. At night, she teaches neighbors who distrust her how to read the council’s budgets. She keeps a list of people she has failed and visits each one when she can. When a choice promises her safety at someone else’s expense, she checks the facts, listens to those affected, and makes a different choice.
