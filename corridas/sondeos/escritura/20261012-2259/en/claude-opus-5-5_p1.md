Mara returns library books a day early, then stays to reshelve the ones other people left on tables. She keeps a ledger of small debts (a coffee owed, a favor promised) and settles every one before the month ends. When her brother lost his job, she didn't offer advice; she rewrote his résumé overnight and slipped it under his door unsigned. She quit a well-paid position after her manager asked her to backdate a safety report, and she told no one why. Each Sunday she calls her grandmother and lets her repeat the same stories, laughing in the same places. She plants tomatoes knowing the squirrels will take most of them. Faced with two roads, she chooses the one that costs her more, then refuses to mention the price. Her friends call her stubborn. Strangers, the ones she helps, never learn her name.
