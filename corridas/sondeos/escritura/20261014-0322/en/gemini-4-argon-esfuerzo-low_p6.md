Miller set the damp photograph on the metal table. He didn’t sit, letting his shadow fall across Julian’s trembling hands. 

"They found your brother’s car by the river, Julian. The trunk was empty, which means the ledger is still in play."

Julian stared at the floor, his jaw clenched tight.

"Loyalty is admirable," Miller continued, his voice dangerously soft. "Right up until the moment you realize Marcus already bought his plane ticket. One seat. First class to Zurich."

Julian’s eyes snapped up, widening just a fraction. 

"There it is," Miller whispered, leaning in. "He told you to wait at the safehouse, I imagine. Promised he’d come back for you." 

Julian’s breath hitched. 

"Tell me where the ledger is, and I make sure Marcus never boards that flight. Stay silent, and you rot here while he spends your cut."

"The train station," Julian choked out. "Locker forty-two."
