"Look at the ficus," she said, setting her mug on the counter. "The leaves are turning brown again."

He didn’t look up from his newspaper. "It never liked this corner. Not enough light."

"I've tried watering it more. I bought that expensive fertilizer you suggested. I even misted it." 

He sighed, folding the paper slowly. "Sometimes it doesn’t matter what you do, Sarah. Once the roots are bound, it just suffocates in the pot."

"We could repot it. Move it to the bedroom."

"We tried that in the spring, remember? It dropped half its leaves in a week. It’s just exhausting, sweeping them up every morning, pretending it’s going to bounce back."

She traced the rim of her cup, her voice dropping to a whisper. "So, what? We just throw it out?"

He finally met her eyes, his expression gentle, yet final. "You can take it, if you think it'll do better at a new place. Otherwise, yes. I think we just let it go."
